How we compare

Member-funded. Measured by value.

We examine unions as member-funded service businesses. The question is not simply what representation costs, but what members receive—and how clearly the evidence connects resources to service and results.

Cost

Dues, assessments, and resources devoted to representation.

Service

Access, responsiveness, expertise, and help when members need it.

Results

Contract protections, implementation, remedies, and documented outcomes.

Accountability

Financial visibility, member control, and meaningful oversight.

Read each comparison in the context of its carrier, agreement, time period, and evidence limits. Missing information is not a zero score. A result at one carrier does not automatically describe an entire union.

Common questions

Questions worth asking.
Evidence worth examining.

Start with the questions behind the research. Then explore the records, comparisons, and limitations in each section.

Does the lowest dues rate mean the best value?

Not necessarily. Cost is one part of the comparison, alongside accessible service, enforceable protections, documented results, and accountability.

Will CrewSignal declare one union the winner?

Only where a defined comparison supports a conclusion. Evidence can differ by carrier, bargaining period, service, and member priority. The purpose is to show what the evidence supports—not assume a universal winner.

How should I read a gap in the evidence?

As a limitation, not proof of good or bad service. Check the sources, dates, and scope of each comparison, and distinguish a documented outcome from an inference about what caused it.

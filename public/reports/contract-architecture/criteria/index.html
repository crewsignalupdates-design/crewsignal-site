Contract Architecture

Contract Analysis Criteria

The standardized rubric CrewSignal uses to evaluate flight attendant labor agreements for clarity, enforceability, and operational reliability.

New: proposed Reserve protection criteria and United–Alaska evidence comparison. This pilot does not replace the existing scorecards below.

Purpose and Method

CrewSignal evaluates flight attendant collective bargaining agreements using a structural and operational rubric. The goal is to assess how contract language performs under real operational pressure — including irregular operations — and how drafting choices allocate clarity, responsibility, and risk.

This rubric evaluates the contract as written. It does not assess negotiating intent, internal union strategy, or provide legal advice. Airline-specific analyses apply the same criteria to ensure consistent, comparable results across unions and carriers.

Interpreting Scorecard Profiles

The standardized scorecard used in Contract Architecture reports is designed to evaluate structural design choices within individual agreements, not to rank carriers or unions in isolation.

Differences in score profiles may reflect distinct operational models (e.g., long-haul vs. domestic), differing regulatory environments (e.g., FAA vs. EASA-anchored operations), or negotiated decisions to externalize certain constraints to binding law or regulation rather than embed them directly in contract language.

As a result, higher or lower scores in specific domains should not be read automatically as indicators of stronger or weaker bargaining outcomes. In some agreements, protections are enforced primarily through contract-internal mechanisms; in others, they are anchored to externally binding regulatory frameworks with the agreement providing compensation, clarity, and enforceability around those limits.

The scorecard is intended to support contract-specific analysis and reader understanding, enabling informed comparison across agreements over time. Broader qualitative analysis—such as evaluating negotiation patterns across unions or identifying recurring strengths in particular domains— will be addressed separately and explicitly in future comparative work.

I. Structural Clarity

Question: Is the contract logically organized and navigable under operational pressure?

  • Clear section sequencing (definitions → rules → remedies)
  • Consistent use of defined terms
  • Amendments integrated vs. bolted on
  • Cross-references that resolve questions (not scavenger hunts)

Significance: Poor structure increases interpretive discretion at the moment of enforcement.

II. Rule Construction & Precision

Question: Do provisions fully resolve situations, or leave ambiguity?

  • Mandatory vs. discretionary language (“shall” vs. “may”)
  • Closed conditional logic (“if / then / unless”)
  • Explicit time windows, notice rules, and triggers
  • No undefined “third states” of responsibility

Significance: Ambiguity shifts risk to the flight attendant during irregular operations.

III. Burden Allocation

Question: Who must act to preserve contractual rights?

  • Automatic protections vs. employee-initiated claims
  • Company obligations triggered by events vs. contact
  • Default outcomes when no action is taken
  • Silent forfeiture risks (procedural traps)

Significance: Contracts that require perfect member behavior to preserve rights are structurally weaker.

IV. Enforcement Pathway

Question: How does a violation actually get remedied?

  • Self-executing remedies vs. grievance-dependent outcomes
  • Time limits for correction and payment
  • Retroactivity and interest provisions (if any)
  • Discipline exposure arising from the same event

Significance: A right without a reliable remedy is not operationally meaningful.

V. Internal Consistency

Question: Does the contract contradict itself?

  • Uniform treatment of identical concepts across sections
  • Exceptions clearly bounded and visible
  • No “silent overrides” in side letters or MOUs
  • Consistent terminology across the agreement

Significance: Inconsistency is a primary driver of adverse arbitration outcomes.

VI. Operational Reality Alignment

Question: Does the contract reflect how airlines actually operate?

  • Reserve vs. lineholder distinctions grounded in operational reality
  • Integration of federal legality and duty/rest rules
  • Assumptions fit national network operations and mobile crews
  • Clear handling of irregular operations edge cases

Significance: Contracts written for static workplaces fail mobile crews.

VII. Transparency & Accessibility

Question: Can members realistically understand and use the contract?

  • Plain-language drafting where possible
  • Definitions and key concepts discoverable and consistently applied
  • Length vs. density tradeoffs managed
  • Usable under time pressure (not just at a desk)

Significance: Opacity consolidates interpretive power away from the membership.

Standard Output

Each airline-specific review includes:

  • Structural strengths
  • Structural weaknesses
  • High-risk ambiguity zones
  • Enforcement reliability (qualitative)

Topic-based modules (e.g., reassignment, pay protection, duty-day legality) apply standardized hypotheticals across multiple contracts after structural baselines are established.

Reserve protection pilot: an explicit point framework

Method preview — MV-1.0-RC2-Reserve-Pilot, November 13, 2026. Not an adopted replacement for existing ratings.

The existing structural lenses and six-domain scorecards remain distinct from this proposed topic module. The pilot asks whether a specified enforceable protection is available to a defined employee under an identified agreement and date. It does not assess negotiating intent or convert a contract result into a finding about actual service performance. Source-backed bargaining history and member-priority alignment are separate analyses.

The proposed design has twenty tests in five groups of four. Each test is worth five points if every required component is established for the operative profile. Each group therefore has a maximum of twenty points, and the module has a maximum of one hundred. A complete 75/100 would equal fifteen satisfied tests and a 3.75/5 display—not 75% effective representation or a return on dues. This is a hypothetical explanation, not an airline rating.

There is no discretionary partial credit, carrier-size adjustment, wage offset or union-identity adjustment. Unknown or disputed evidence is not zero and does not shrink the denominator. R01 and R02 intentionally form nested tiers: meeting R02 necessarily meets R01. A genuine contractual prohibition can satisfy a conditional test by eliminating the burden, but that conclusion requires affirmative supporting language—not silence or a lack of reported incidents.

The equal weights and thresholds are CrewSignal editorial choices, not statutory requirements, established workforce preferences or externally validated cutoffs. They were developed after reading carrier provisions, not through blinded selection. Before numerical ratings are released, the applications must be reconciled and the specified threshold and weight sensitivity examined. A later methodological change must be versioned and applied consistently; it cannot be presented as a negotiated gain.

Evidence and interpretation

Every proposed point decision must identify the exact subsection, physical and printed PDF pages, agreement version, material exceptions, implementation basis, reviewer and explanation. Contractual fact, CrewSignal benchmark and interpretation remain separate. “Does not meet” means the selected benchmark is not satisfied; it does not imply that the agreement provides no protection. A negative finding requires a bounded review of relevant provisions and known potentially controlling supplements.

Current, historical and negotiated-future provisions must not be blended into one card. An attractive discretionary release is not an employee right. A premium is not automatically a breach remedy or a replacement day. Actual grievances and response-time outcomes belong in a separate service-performance assessment.

Display rules

Any eventual numerical display must include all five group subtotals and the failed or unresolved essential safeguards: R03, R08, R12 and R19. These flags do not create hidden deductions or caps. A high total with a material safeguard gap is not described simply as an excellent program. Neither this module nor its companion irregular-operations draft is a whole-contract score; overlapping enforcement topics must not be averaged into a new composite without an explicit aggregation method.

For an applicability-established record, Y met tests and U unresolved tests produce conservative possible-point bounds of 5Y to 5(Y+U). These are not statistical confidence intervals. A single total requires all twenty decisions and the release checks to be complete. The United–Alaska historical comparison releases neither totals nor ranges because decisive applicability and interpretation issues remain.

Validation and version history

The planned weight check raises each group from 20 to 25 while reducing the other four to 18.75, and then lowers it to 15 while raising the other four to 21.25. Threshold checks examine contact-hour cutoffs one hour either side, free-day shares of 38% and 42%, and first-decision clocks of 20 and 45 days. These are diagnostic alternatives, not permission to choose a preferred winner. Actual-carrier robustness is not established by testing only fictional examples.

RC2 clarifies extension triggers, remote response, assignment versus delayed return, compulsory rest interruption, agreed deadline extensions, initiation of binding review and defined credit remedies. In particular, R06’s remote scope and R18’s agreed-extension treatment are explicit changes or clarifications, not undisclosed reinterpretations of RC1. The forty pilot applications remain subject to clause-specific evidence and review. Existing published ratings remain unchanged.

Availability exposure — proposed maximum 20 points

R01 · Rolling contact: fourteen hours

Compulsory remote contact totals no more than 14 elapsed hours in any rolling 24 hours under the ordinary awarded Reserve pattern. Combine all mandatory windows and checks; do not count overlapping minutes twice.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R02 · Rolling contact: twelve hours

The same aggregate ordinary-contact measure is no more than 12 elapsed hours in any rolling 24 hours. This is an intentional additional tier beyond R01, not a second description of the same threshold.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R03 · Extension conditions and caps · Essential safeguard

Every compulsory extension requires a stated, reviewable qualifying condition that must exist before the extension may be imposed and constrains the employer's decision independently of notice, volunteer ordering and numerical caps. Each extension also has a numerical maximum duration per event and a numerical maximum number per bid period. A bare election by Scheduling or an unrestricted operational-needs reservation is not a qualifying condition. No general operational-needs clause can waive the required limits. An absolute prohibition on compulsory extension satisfies this test.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R04 · Reporting after the window

For an employee not already on duty, Scheduling cannot compel a report after the Reserve window, including an authorized extension, ends solely because contact or assignment occurred before it ended. A later report requires affirmative voluntary agreement.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

Notice and contact — proposed maximum 20 points

R05 · Day-ahead window notice

The ordinary Reserve window/type is notified at least 24 elapsed hours before it begins. An employer-initiated adverse change inside that period requires employee consent. This tests the availability window, not notice of a specific trip.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R06 · Fifteen-minute remote response

For compulsory remote Reserve callout while the employee is not already on duty or airport standby, the employee receives at least 15 elapsed minutes from the final required, documented contact attempt to respond, without an earlier missed-call sanction. More protective remote contact rules qualify; a source supporting only a shorter period fails. Instructions during existing duty or airport standby are outside this specific test but must be disclosed separately in the evidence panel.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R07 · Two hours from completed notification

Every compulsory callout in the defined base/profile permits at least 120 elapsed minutes from completed assignment notification to report. A shorter interval may be volunteered but declining it cannot reduce the guarantee or trigger discipline.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R08 · Outside-window contact and acceptance · Essential safeguard

Outside the defined contact window and outside duty, no mandatory check, acknowledgment or return call applies. Merely answering unsolicited contact does not create a compulsory assignment; affirmative voluntary acceptance is required.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

Days off and rest — proposed maximum 20 points

R09 · Forty percent fully free award days

The contractual full-active-period award guarantees at least 40% of bid-period calendar days fully free of duty, Reserve contact and mandatory checks. Use unrounded free_days/period_days and the minimum allowed award, not a favorable synthetic roster. Employee-chosen extra work is excluded from this guarantee test.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R10 · Minimum days off protected from imposed work

Company-initiated compulsory assignment, conversion, added duty or imposed revision cannot project a reduction below the agreement's minimum awarded days off without affirmative consent. This includes new or revised assigned work during irregular operations. An unexpected actual delay of previously lawful work, without new or revised assigned work, is assessed separately under R11. A unilateral operational-needs exception defeats this test; voluntary trading does not. Later restoration does not constitute consent to the original reduction.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R11 · Equivalent replacement of an invaded day

If company-directed duty or delayed return invades an awarded off day, an equivalent day free of duty and contact must be restored no later than the end of the following bid period, without reduction in guarantee. Money alone is not restoration.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R12 · Compelled rest interruption and full restart · Essential safeguard

When required company contact or work interrupts protected rest, a new full applicable contractual rest interval must precede the next report, and using that right does not reduce the guarantee or trigger discipline. Establish the compelled participation first: an unanswered attempt or a call the employee is expressly free not to answer is not, by itself, an in-scope interruption. If answering creates a compulsory further obligation, examine that subsequent act separately. Full applicable rest means the complete contractual interval due to the defined profile absent a valid voluntary waiver, not only its noncontact component or a regulatory floor. Merely restating a legal minimum without the specified contractual consequences is insufficient.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

Assignment and employee choice — proposed maximum 20 points

R13 · Objective assignment order

A mandatory, ascertainable assignment order governs the defined Reserve group. Qualification, legality and other exceptions are specified and reviewable; an unrestricted Scheduling-discretion exception defeats the point.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R14 · Assignment timestamps and reasons

The employee or authorized representative can obtain timestamped records and the priority/exception explanation sufficient to test the employee's assignment against that order. A public rule without access to decision-specific evidence is insufficient.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R15 · Objective peer exchanges

A legal exchange of Reserve/off days with a qualified employee must be accepted when published objective eligibility conditions are met. Mere ability to request a trade subject to unrestricted approval is insufficient.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R16 · Off-day work without extra compulsory availability

The employee can pick up otherwise available, legal off-day work under published objective conditions without acquiring additional compulsory Reserve availability. No point is lost because a particular trip happens not to be available; money earned is assessed separately.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

Correction and enforcement — proposed maximum 20 points

R17 · Two-business-day acknowledgment

A written correction/grievance route identifies filing requirements, filing deadline and responsible recipient, and requires acknowledgment within two business days of receipt. Required forms/data must be accessible to the claimant or authorized representative.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R18 · Written first determination

The enforceable default requires a substantive written first determination within 30 calendar days of receipt of a claim containing the published minimum required information. An employer-controlled completeness clock, unilateral tolling, a deadline beginning only after resolution, or an indefinite standing extension fails. A genuinely case-specific written extension agreed by the employee or a representative authorized under the agreement, with a finite revised deadline and no unilateral employer right to compel it, does not by itself defeat the default-deadline test. Identify who may consent and whether the extension can be refused, and disclose extension use separately. Do not describe the default as an unconditional maximum.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R19 · Connected binding escalation · Essential safeguard

The employee or representative authorized under the agreement can initiate a connected path to binding independent review after denial or expiry of the first-decision deadline. Employer silence or refusal of a joint submission cannot indefinitely veto entry. The linked provisions must establish the full initiation-to-review route for the selected claim type. No fixed hearing, award or collection deadline is implied by this test; disclose those times separately. Union referral authority is not a promise to pursue every individual claim.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

R20 · Enforceable wage and credit restoration

For the defined Reserve scheduling breach, the contractual correction or adjudication route can require restoration of the wages and identified native qualifying credit lost through that breach. Name the underlying wage entitlement, the credited-hours or TFP category, its relevant guarantee or benefit-qualification use, the binding forum's authority to enforce it, and any remedial limitation. Expressly connected provisions or an authoritative application may supply that proof; one sentence using CrewSignal's terminology is not required. Broad jurisdiction alone, optional settlement authority, or a premium for authorized work is insufficient. Do not assume a payment carries every benefit credit. The monetary amount is not counted again in this score.

Compare the cited United and Alaska provisions.

Recognition

Who represents Flight Attendants—and what a recognition clause does not decide.

Source review: October 28, 2026 · U.S. airline / Railway Labor Act (RLA) context · United example, not a universal clause

What recognition means

Recognition identifies the employees represented and the representative with whom the employer bargains. In United’s 2026–2031 agreement, Section 1.A.1 recognizes CWA-AFA as the National Mediation Board-certified representative of the Company’s Flight Attendant craft or class for bargaining over pay, rules and working conditions. That is a United-specific contractual application, not a universal description of every airline’s recognition clause. Sources: United §1.A.1, PDF p.5.

In this setting, craft or class means the employee grouping involved in representation—not a travel cabin, base, or bid position. A recognition provision identifies the bargaining relationship; it does not, by itself, measure the quality of representation. Sources: 45 U.S.C. §152, Fourth and Ninth.

Recognition and NMB certification are related, but distinct

The Railway Labor Act (RLA) protects employees’ choice of representative from employer interference. Under Section 2, Ninth, the National Mediation Board (NMB) investigates representation disputes and certifies the representative; the carrier must then bargain with the certified representative. Section 181 extends the relevant RLA framework to covered air carriers and their employees. Sources: 45 U.S.C. §152, Third, Fourth and Ninth; 45 U.S.C. §181.

A contract’s recognition language is not a private substitute for that statutory process. United’s own merger clause makes continued pre-merger recognition conditional on consistency with the RLA and applicable NMB rulings or orders, and places post-merger representation under those authorities. Sources: United §1.B.5.a, PDF p.6.

Keep three questions separate

Representation: who speaks for the employees?
The RLA/NMB process addresses the representative for the relevant craft or class. A transaction announcement does not answer that question.
Contract continuity: what obligations survive?
A successorship clause can require assumption of an agreement and set transition obligations. Its exact triggers and exceptions must be read.
Seniority integration: how are employee lists combined?
United addresses this separately in §1.B.3 and §1.B.5.c. The applicable integration rules are not established merely by naming the representative.

Sources: 45 U.S.C. §152, Ninth; United §1.B.1–3, PDF pp.5–6; United §1.B.5.a and .c, PDF p.6.

Corporate change does not automatically decide representation

The NMB Manual’s merger procedures extend to specified purchases, leases, acquisitions of control and similar transactions, not just events labeled “mergers.” The Board considers integration indicators such as operations, labor/personnel functions, management, workforce and ownership. An alliance, common ownership, or commercial coordination should therefore not be treated as an automatic finding of a single transportation system. The application, evidence and Board determination remain distinct steps. Sources: NMB Manual §§19.1–19.5, PDF pp.31–33.

A carrier’s transaction notice is not the same as an employee-side representation application. In its March 20, 2026 Republic Airways decision, the NMB reiterated that employees or their representatives—not the carrier—must request the representation investigation. That procedural holding is useful here; it is not a statement about the current representation status of every group at those airlines. Sources: 53 NMB No.17, printed p.68 / PDF p.5.

Under Manual §19.7, existing certifications continue until the NMB issues a new certification or dismissal. A single-system determination and the subsequent representation disposition are distinct. Do not automatically predict either an election or a particular winner from a corporate transaction. Sources: NMB Manual §§19.6–19.7, PDF p.33; NMB representation overview.

Worked example: purchase announced, operations still separate

Hypothetical. Assume two carriers announce a purchase and a recognition provision resembling United §1.B.5.a applies. It would be unsupported to conclude that the purchasing carrier’s union automatically represents every employee immediately, or that the former union has permanent immunity from an NMB proceeding. Check the actual certifications and Board orders, the contract’s continued-recognition language, and the operative transaction facts. Contract continuity and seniority integration require their own analysis. Sources: United §1.B.4–5, PDF p.6; NMB Manual §§19.6–19.7.

Significance for CrewSignal’s comparisons

CrewSignal’s analytical questions: Does the agreement identify coverage clearly? Does its transition language preserve a workable bargaining relationship while respecting the statutory representation process? Are associated work-protection rules and remedies explicit? These are evaluation questions, not newly adopted numerical scores.

Recognition must also be distinguished from work scope. United §1.A.2 addresses customary Flight Attendant work, management reservations, and specified exceptions for other personnel performing that work. The presence of a recognized union does not remove the need to examine those limits, relevant side letters, and enforcement evidence. Sources: United §1.A.2, PDF p.5.

Scope, findings and limits

A Reserve program is more than its monthly guarantee. Its value also depends on when the employee must be available, how Scheduling may change that obligation, which choices remain voluntary, and what happens when a right is disputed. This comparison places United and Alaska provisions beside the same twenty proposed protection benchmarks. Both workgroups are represented by CWA-AFA, but representation by the same union is not evidence of identical contractual outcomes.

Publication status: evidence comparison and method preview—not a released scorecard. The criterion-level interpretations are provisional. The findings below distinguish the language reviewed from its historical applicability. United’s October Reserve Availability Period (RAP) regime has not been established for several successor provisions. Some Alaska interpretations also remain disputed. Neither missing evidence nor an unresolved interpretation is scored as zero. No carrier total, range, star rating or overall winner is reported.

The employees and dates being compared

The profile is an eligible, nonprobationary, full-active Los Angeles (LAX) Reserve with 5.5 completed pay-service years. United’s comparison period is September 29–October 29, 2026. Alaska’s is October 1–31, 2026, for an Alaska-covered AM Reserve—not a Hawaiian employee or someone voluntarily selecting Extended Reserve. These historical profiles are not advice about an employee’s current assignment.

The agreement text, a later implementation notice and an explanation by a union or employer can serve different evidentiary roles. The United joint implementation index and transition explanation and United joint implementation notice — Section 8 / Ready Reserve Call Out support keeping successor language separate from provision-specific activation. For Alaska, the Alaska implementation timeline — Reserve rows, PDF p.3, Alaska Scheduling Committee recap — June 25, 2026 / Block2Block implementation and Alaska final-document notice — March 24, 2026 supply the identified version and implementation context; none is a substitute for reviewing individual exceptions.

Three useful distinctions

Response opportunity is not the whole contact system. Under the proposed remote-call benchmark, the reviewed United rule permits ten minutes after the last required contact and the Alaska rule permits fifteen. The full call sequence and on-duty exceptions remain beside the finding in R06.

An awarded day off, protection against a new assignment, and restoration after a delay are different rights. The minimum-day comparison appears in R09; imposed-work and restoration issues appear separately in R10 and R11.

Receipt, a first determination, appeal and payment are different promises. The reviewed ordinary scheduling-grievance routes do not supply the two-business-day acknowledgment required by R17. That is not a finding that representatives are unresponsive in practice. The decision and escalation provisions are assessed separately in R18 and R19.

How to read the comparison

The full proposed method is on the Criteria page. Each benchmark below is a CrewSignal editorial standard, not a statutory minimum, proven workforce preference or provision written by the airlines. “Meets” and “does not meet” describe the reviewed wording under that standard; they are not released points. “Evidence unresolved” identifies a missing decisive fact. “Interpretation disputed” identifies materially competing readings.

Every entry identifies historical applicability separately. A successor-text shortfall with unresolved activation cannot be described as an operative October failure. A benchmark shortfall also does not mean that the agreement supplies no meaningful protection. Essential safeguards—R03, R08, R12 and R19—are marked, but are not additional deductions.

R03 · Extension conditions and caps · Essential safeguard

Proposed benchmark: Every compulsory extension requires a stated, reviewable qualifying condition that must exist before the extension may be imposed and constrains the employer's decision independently of notice, volunteer ordering and numerical caps. Each extension also has a numerical maximum duration per event and a numerical maximum number per bid period. A bare election by Scheduling or an unrestricted operational-needs reservation is not a qualifying condition. No general operational-needs clause can waive the required limits. An absolute prohibition on compulsory extension satisfies this test.

R18 · Written first determination

Proposed benchmark: The enforceable default requires a substantive written first determination within 30 calendar days of receipt of a claim containing the published minimum required information. An employer-controlled completeness clock, unilateral tolling, a deadline beginning only after resolution, or an indefinite standing extension fails. A genuinely case-specific written extension agreed by the employee or a representative authorized under the agreement, with a finite revised deadline and no unilateral employer right to compel it, does not by itself defeat the default-deadline test. Identify who may consent and whether the extension can be refused, and disclose extension use separately. Do not describe the default as an unconditional maximum.

Same union, different provisions: what can be concluded?

These comparisons can establish a difference between specified provisions without explaining why negotiators accepted that difference. Contract strength, change from the prior agreement, and representation in practice remain separate questions. A representative’s motives, bargaining effort or causal contribution cannot be inferred from a single clause, an overall wage rate or this unscored comparison.

The next analytical link is stronger protection elsewhere → practical member impact → opportunity to pursue it → documented proposal or tradeoff → ratified result → implementation. Member polling and bargaining records may illuminate those choices, but a ratification vote is not a clause-by-clause preference survey. Context may explain a gap; it does not change the protection benchmark. A regional and a legacy carrier must satisfy the same test.

Contractual protections are one part of value for members’ dues. This article does not calculate return on dues, attribute all wages to the union, or establish actual grievance-response performance. It complements the lineholder compensation worked example without altering its calculations or treating compensation as a substitute for time, choice or enforceability.

Why no numerical ratings are shown

The proposed twenty-test design is visible so readers can examine the standards as well as the contracts. A final total requires a settled operative profile, supported decisions, reconciliation of changed applications and the announced sensitivity checks. Those conditions are not yet complete for these two historical profiles. Accordingly, no score, possible-score range, star rating or carrier ordering is released, and no existing six-domain score is relabeled or recalculated.

The evidence can still be useful now: readers can identify a particular protection, follow the exception, compare the other agreement, and see precisely which conclusion remains unsettled. A later change will identify whether it comes from a contract amendment, implementation evidence, a corrected interpretation or a revision of CrewSignal’s method.

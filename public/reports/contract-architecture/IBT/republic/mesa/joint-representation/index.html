IBT · Republic Airways / Mesa Airlines

Current Certification & Joint Transition
Representation and governance under the Railway Labor Act

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific time). The earlier joint transition is distinguished from the current NMB certification.

Current Certified Representation

On October 13, 2026, the National Mediation Board extended the IBT Airline Division’s certification to the entire flight-attendant craft or class at the combined Republic/Mesa carrier and extinguished CWA-AFA’s former Mesa certification. The current legal position is therefore one certified representative, following an earlier period of joint bargaining and transition work. (54 NMB No. 2, Case R-7690, printed pp. 10–12; conclusion p. 12)

That decision does not establish that every private cooperation arrangement ended or every administrative handoff was completed. Legacy Mesa dues, steward routing, grievance transfers and continuing specialist support require their own implementation records. The IBT representation review separates certification from the documented Local 135 service interface.

The Earlier Joint Transition

Before the representation change, IBT represented Republic flight attendants under certification R-6225 and CWA-AFA represented Mesa flight attendants under R-6499. The unions worked together on the merger transition, joint collective bargaining agreement (JCBA) and seniority integration. The NMB’s September decision records those steps and the parties’ use of Republic’s 2022–2027 agreement as the JCBA foundation. (53 NMB No. 30, September 1, 2026, printed pp. 125 and 128)

The joint-transition documents remain relevant to the rights, processes and work undertaken during integration. Their historical role should be read alongside the later certification decision, rather than described as permanently preserving two certifications.

What the NMB Decided

The unions jointly applied for a representation determination in April 2026. CWA-AFA stated that it was not intervening to seek the combined certification and agreed that its certification should transfer to IBT. Following the September single-system finding, no intervenor filed an application within the stated period. The Board extended the larger group’s certification and terminated the smaller group’s certification. (October determination, Procedural Background and Discussion, printed pp. 11–12)

The September single-system finding and October certification are separate legal events. This case cannot be described as avoiding NMB intervention, and the certification decision is not proof that all operational integration or local administrative changes were finished.

Reading the Contract and Transition Materials

The NMB identifies Republic’s 2022–2027 flight-attendant agreement as the JCBA foundation and describes continuing implementation at Mesa. The base contract, JCBA changes and MTA address different parts of the transition. A base-contract provision should not be presented as fully current without considering the applicable overlays. (September determination, printed p. 128; Republic base agreement; Local 135 JCBA materials)

The unions described the JCBA as midterm improvements while preserving full negotiations beginning in January 2027. Its timetable and scope should not be treated as a complete substitute for that later bargaining. (January 22, 2026 ratification notice)

See Mesa Extension & Transition Analysis for the document bundle and IBT contract administration for procedure and cost-source limitations. The review did not reconcile the entire set of amendments clause by clause.

A Published Funding Mechanism

MTA Section F provides employer reimbursement for specified merger work, professional fees and flight-pay loss, capped at $1 million in aggregate across both union parties. Coverage through December 31, 2025 has a conditional continuation tied to submitting the JCBA for ratification by that date. Actual payment and allocation remain unverified. (Published MTA, pp. 9–10)

The public copy’s DocuSign record is incomplete; NMB independently records that an MTA was entered. A completed counterpart and payment records are still needed. The financial review explains this limit and the difference between employer reimbursement, union spending and inter-union allocation.

What the Structure Means for Members

The record identifies bargaining work, an integrated seniority list, implementation events and a change in certified representation. Evaluating the resulting service requires the next layer of evidence: payroll corrections, unresolved seniority issues, grievance transfers, member access and the work funded by each organization.

Certification, contract rights and service delivery should remain distinct. A current service directory, allocation agreement or case record is needed to determine who handles a legacy Mesa member’s issue and how the work is financed. The NMB decision alone cannot answer those operational questions.

Source and Date Limits

This page uses the NMB’s dated decisions for the representation outcome and chronology, and attributes union statements about cooperation and bargaining. It preserves historical documents without treating earlier representation descriptions as current. Outstanding implementation and financial questions are not findings that services were absent.

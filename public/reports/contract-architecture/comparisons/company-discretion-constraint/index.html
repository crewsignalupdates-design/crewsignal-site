Company Discretion Constraint

How far may management change assignments, suspend ordinary rules, or offer benefits at its option?

Reader's answer: A contract can both restrict routine scheduling decisions and reserve special authority for particular events. The quality of the protection depends on which decisions are limited, what exceptions apply, what notice or compensation is owed, and whether the rule can be enforced. One score does not show all of those differences.

Endeavor: what the agreement provides

Published score: 3.4/5. The applicable source set is the 2020–2025 base collective bargaining agreement as modified and extended by LOA 20 through March 31, 2027. This worked example identifies three separate kinds of discretion rather than treating them as one rule. Return to Endeavor's scorecard.

The benefit is not an unconditional employee entitlement.The company must give at least 60 days' notice before implementation or increase and 120 days' notice before termination or reduction, plus specified hiring-freeze and notification conditions.LOA 20.3 (PDF p.3); LOA 20.4 (PDF p.4)

Why 3.4? The existing Endeavor scorecard recognizes express restrictions and documented procedures alongside substantial retained discretion. The clauses above support that qualitative interpretation; they do not independently derive a numerical value of 3.4 or justify treating a 0.1-point difference as objectively measured. These differences should not be treated as precise measurements without a consistently specified point methodology.

Important distinction: LOA 20's geographic stipend concerns discretionary pay, while §1.H and §5.I concern managerial and scheduling authority. They belong to a shared discretion analysis but should not be mistaken for equivalent employee rights.

Evidence and scoring rules

A meaningful comparison begins with the operative agreement and amendments, then considers management rights, assignments and reassignments, irregular-operation exceptions, notice, employee choice, compensation, grievance access and implementation dates. Contractual differences are most useful when tested against the same employee circumstances and linked to the controlling provisions.

The existing six-domain, 30-point scorecards and CrewSignal's seven qualitative structural lenses should be read as interpretive assessments, not as a fully calibrated point-allocation formula. The separate Reserve-specific criteria use a different framework and are not included in these scores. Small numerical differences should not be treated as a mathematically established ranking of contractual value. Read the Contract Analysis Criteria.

Missing documentary support must be recorded as not reviewed or not verified, never as zero or absent protection. Actual compliance, grievance outcomes, and union spending are separate evidence questions and are not inferred from a structural contract score.

Controlling collective bargaining agreements and executed amendments take precedence over summaries. Endeavor's source links support its contractual explanation; the other entries provide direct access to each carrier's published analysis.

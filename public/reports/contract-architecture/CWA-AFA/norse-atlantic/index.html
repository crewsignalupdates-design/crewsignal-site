Agreement Metadata

The current 2025–2028 agreement was ratified May 14, 2025 and applies to U.S.-based Norse Atlantic cabin crew represented by CWA-AFA under the Railway Labor Act. The 2022–2024 first agreement is a historical comparator, not the controlling successor text.

Duty and rest legality remain anchored to relevant EASA and authority definitions, while contract pay, operational protections and grievances are governed by the negotiated agreement and the RLA process. Read the signed successor agreement.

Contract Architecture Overview

The 2025 contract retains Norse's cross-border regulatory framework rather than replacing it with a domestic-only duty and rest system. It adds more explicit pay credits, operational notice and hotel requirements, while reserving significant management discretion under the defined legal framework.

The most consequential remaining gap is Reserve: bargaining over a complete Reserve subsystem remains deferred. The regular RLA grievance and System Board structure continues. Read CrewSignal's successive-agreement findings.

Scheduling & Assignment Framework

Published roster rules and a minimum of twelve days off remain central. The 2025 agreement improves visibility of pickup, check-in, deadhead and check-out time in the roster. Security-search assignments have separate check-in and pay provisions; these should not be confused with additional legal flight duty time.

Reserve bidding and availability rules are not fully resolved in the signed agreement and are reserved for further negotiation. The absence of a finished subsystem is an important limitation, not proof that Reserve employees have no legal protections. Agreement Article 6.

Economic Structure

The monthly guarantee remains sixty block hours. The 2025 agreement increases pay rates, improves the value of eligible non-flying events, and introduces additional allowances. Per diem rises from the prior $3.25 hourly departure-to-arrival model to $4.25 hourly from check-in to check-out, with a later $4.50 rate at the agreement's second year.

Most qualifying non-flying events receive a three-block-hour baseline subject to event conditions, with additional credit for certain longer assignments and exclusions against simultaneous flying. Pay credit, duty time and eligibility must be distinguished when applying these provisions. Agreement Article 3.

Enforcement & Dispute Resolution Architecture

Grievances and the System Board remain the negotiated paths for interpretation and enforcement under the Railway Labor Act. The 2025 improvements in pay, lodging and schedule transparency may create better-defined obligations, but they do not establish a universal monetary penalty for every contract violation.

Actual grievance awards, employee payment and subsequent Company compliance must be verified separately. Agreement Articles 16 and 17.

Structural Strengths, Weaknesses & Comparative Flags

Improvements: Higher and longer-window per diem, more consistent non-flying credit, clearer hotel-room standards, inflight-rest facility expectations, sick-leave increases and base-closure notice and protection provisions. These materially distinguish the ratified 2025 agreement from the 2022 baseline.

Limitations: A fully developed Reserve subsystem is still outstanding; certain sick-leave circumstances introduce documentation triggers; EASA-based legality and contract pay provisions require careful reconciliation. The current 20.6/30 score comes from CrewSignal's existing 2025 assessment.

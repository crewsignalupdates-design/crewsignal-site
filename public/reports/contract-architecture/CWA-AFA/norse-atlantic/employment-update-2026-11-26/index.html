Norse Atlantic: Bankruptcy Decision, Pay and Employment Protections

November 27: the Norwegian operating company’s bankruptcy decision

Norse Atlantic Airways AS has decided to seek bankruptcy. Norse’s November 27 announcement says the Norwegian operating subsidiary’s board resolved to file a petition with Agder District Court. It also says all Norse flights are canceled and ticket sales have stopped. Read the November 27 company announcement.

The listed parent, Norse Atlantic ASA, and other group companies are outside the announced filing. The parent and UK subsidiary were seeking a solution for the remaining business. The announcement describes a trustee appointment as a future step; it does not identify an appointed trustee or a court order opening the case.

Norse’s passenger notice specifies that all flights operated by Norse Atlantic Airways AS and Norse Atlantic UK Ltd from 14:00 CET on November 27 are canceled, including return flights. Those UK cancellations do not place the UK company within the Norwegian bankruptcy petition described above. Read Norse’s passenger notice.

Aircraft lease notices and the earlier flight suspension

Earlier on November 27, Norse reported termination notices covering the leases of all 12 aircraft, disputed their validity and said short-term financing talks continued. The notices do not establish that aircraft were repossessed. Read the lessor-notice disclosure. These aircraft leases are separate from the PIA wet-lease flying whose suspension PIA described to Reuters on November 25. Read the PIA account.

Norse’s November 26 statement had suspended that day’s flights while financing and possible takeover discussions continued, with bankruptcy then conditional on failure to secure funding. The November 27 decision above is a later development. Read the November 26 statement.

Before the all-flights announcement, E24 reported the November 27 Oslo–Phuket and Manchester–Barbados departures canceled, citing airport listings. That earlier account established those two route cancellations; the subsequent company announcement supplies the broader operating statement. Read E24’s route report.

For the wider service response, read Union Services in an Airline Crisis, which examines CWA-AFA, parent CWA, outside resources and continuing assistance to former Spirit flight attendants.

Payroll: Norwegian cabin crew and U.S. flight attendants

In a November 27 release, Fellesforbundet said the Oslo-based cabin crew it represents had not received their November pay. It directed members with individual questions to their local branch. The release does not report recovered wages or establish a missed paycheck for U.S.-based flight attendants. Read Fellesforbundet’s pay and member-assistance statement.

E24 reported on November 26 that the union representing Norse pilots employed in Norway said their November salaries had not been paid. It also described a management email about an unfunded payroll. That reporting does not establish a missed payment to U.S.-based flight attendants. Read E24’s payroll report.

For the U.S. flight attendants covered by the current agreement, Article 3.R ordinarily places half the monthly guarantee on the month-end paycheck, subject to agreed-date and weekend or holiday provisions. The next ordinary month-end date is Monday, November 30. Article 3.R, PDF pages 11–12.

The November 27 company announcement and the council’s public November 25 preparedness notice do not confirm whether the November 30 U.S. payroll will be paid, specify individual U.S. furlough or termination dates, or give a health-coverage end date. The employer’s bankruptcy decision makes those questions urgent; it does not answer them. Company announcement; council preparedness notice.

Where the current contract applies

Article 1.E names Norse Atlantic Airways AS as the direct employer of U.S.-based cabin crew and refers to the Norwegian operating certificate. That is the company named in the bankruptcy decision. The same clause makes the agreement binding on Norse Atlantic ASA and all subsidiaries. Read Article 1.E, PDF page 4.

The May 2025 agreement identifies employer duties and enforcement routes. Its broader binding language does not itself establish a parent-funded wage payment, the treatment of a claim in insolvency or an employee’s recovery. The announcement also does not establish a transfer of U.S. employment to the UK subsidiary.

What the union has provided and announced

CWA-AFA’s November 25 preparedness notice told members to preserve employment, pay and benefit records, described leadership contact with management, and pointed to its Employee Assistance Program. The published guidance is an observable service. The notice also described management’s contingency for hotels and return travel; it does not document union-funded transportation or completed repatriations. Read the Norse council notice.

On November 26, Norse Cabin Union chief steward June Hodne-Jensen told E24 that the union had requested and received a management update as employees waited for pay. Read E24’s interview.

In its November 27 afternoon update, Fellesforbundet said its 19 cabin-crew members were without jobs and directed them to local branches for assistance. Its earlier account recorded regular contact with workers and management and postponement of Norwegian cabin bargaining with NHO Luftfart/Norse, without a new date. That bargaining is separate from the U.S. CWA-AFA agreement. Read Fellesforbundet’s updated notice.

These accounts concern Fellesforbundet’s represented group. They document contact and an assistance commitment, not aid paid, completed wage recovery or U.S. payroll and employment outcomes.

On November 26, the International Transport Workers’ Federation described coordination with affiliated unions and objectives involving jobs, pay, data protection and reemployment. These are federation activity and stated objectives; they do not establish completed recoveries or a parent-CWA grant. Read the ITF statement.

The linked company, council and federation statements do not report Norse-specific totals for relief grants, workers transported, pay recovered or job placements. Private member assistance may occur without a public case report.

What would change the employment picture

  • Court proceedings: an opening order, trustee appointment and instructions identifying the debtor, claim procedures and deadlines.
  • Financing or a transaction: a dated agreement identifying the parties, employing entities and actual transaction structure.
  • Operations and employment: any restart or transfer plan, and specific employer or union notices giving U.S. employment-status and effective dates.
  • Pay and coverage: the applicable pay period and due date, confirmation of payment, and benefit-administrator information.
  • Assistance and enforcement: the provider, eligibility, funding source, actions taken and resulting transport, payment, coverage or placement.

These records would connect the crisis announcement to its consequences for employees, and connect institutional commitments to services actually received.

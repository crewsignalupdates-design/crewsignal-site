The signed September 17 agreement governs the contract term. The July implementation LOA anticipated earlier ratification; the later CWA-AFA transition notice documents the dates announced after the September vote.

Why the implementation dates changed

The July 9, 2025 implementation LOA proposed an August 1 basic effective date and a September 3 scheduling/Reserve start, conditional on execution by August 1. The initial ballot did not produce the required majority, and CWA-AFA reran voting September 3–17. The final agreement was signed September 17.

The union's subsequent transition schedule identified new wage rates and specified travel and training provisions for September 17; additional pay for qualifying days off and hours above the Bid Unit Guarantee for October 1; and bid-transition and new Reserve rules for October 29. A later union update reported all provisions in place. That announcement does not independently establish correct administration or payment in an individual case.

Agreement Metadata

At a glance: ATI's 2025 agreement includes a 56-hour Bid Unit Guarantee under current operations, defined scheduling and grievance procedures, and broad residual management authority. See the six-domain scorecard.

The compact sixteen-section agreement runs from September 17, 2025 through September 16, 2029, with the Railway Labor Act notice mechanism stated in Section 16. The July implementation letter contains an earlier, conditional schedule; the union's later ratification transition notice identifies the announced implementation dates. Read the duration clause.

Contract Architecture Overview

The agreement groups definitions, scheduling, pay, travel, vacation and enforcement by subject. These headings help members locate the governing provision, but eligibility, exceptions and cross-references still matter. Section 2 definitions.

Section 13.F retains broad management powers over subjects not expressly restricted. Section 14 provides a separate, structured route to challenge an alleged contractual violation. Management rights; grievance procedure.

Scheduling & Assignment Framework

Bid lines must be published at least fifteen days before a bid period. The contract gives flight attendants four days to bid and a forty-eight-hour protest window after awards. Its normal 28-day unit has twelve days off, though other bid arrangements and changes are expressly contemplated. Bidding; current operations.

Reserve duties include defined contact and response requirements. Management may change operations or scheduling practices for service or business reasons, but must provide union notice and negotiate the effects of the change. Reserve rules; change authority.

Economic Structure

Under current operations, the Bid Unit Guarantee is 56 hours. Qualifying live flight time receives one-for-one credit; Company-aircraft and certain surface deadheads generally receive one-for-two credit, while commercial international deadheading follows the published bid line. Hours above guarantee earn the applicable hourly rate. Pay and credit rules.

Qualifying work or a holdover on a scheduled day off earns four hours of pay above guarantee. On request, a shortage of at least $100 must be corrected within five business days. Certain eligible Premium Economy deadheads booked in economy have a separate $250 or $500 compensation rule. Day-off pay; payroll corrections; deadhead seating remedy.

Enforcement & Dispute Resolution Architecture

Section 14 provides just-cause protections, written grievance requirements and a System Board of Adjustment with a neutral arbitrator. Grievances generally must be filed within twenty days after the party knew or reasonably should have known the facts. Read the grievance procedure.

If the Company misses a required hearing or decision deadline, the grievance is automatically advanced to the System Board. That keeps a claim moving but does not itself establish a violation or award money. Scope grievances have a separate expedited route under Section 1.E. Automatic advancement; scope arbitration.

Structural Strengths, Weaknesses & Comparative Flags

Strengths: Defined bidding and pay rules, a five-business-day payroll correction provision for qualifying shortages, recorded scheduling calls and automatic grievance advancement after specified Company deadline failures. Recorded calls; payroll; grievances.

Limitations: The Company retains broad discretion over operations and matters not specifically covered. Changes in scheduling practices require notice and bargaining about effects, not necessarily employee consent. Scope exceptions can apply when qualified staffing or aircraft is insufficient and their no-furlough/no-displacement conditions are satisfied. Section 13.F; Section 3.D; Section 1.B.

Agreement Metadata

At a glance: Eastern's first agreement gives a seventy-hour guarantee, a defined fourteen-hour RAP with extra-contact exceptions, and an arbitration path. View the six-domain findings.

Eastern flight attendants ratified their first CWA-AFA agreement on February 27, 2025, following representation certification in 2022. The agreement becomes effective upon ratification and continues through February 27, 2030, with an early-opening notice permitted no sooner than August 27, 2029. Sections 1 and 24.

The union-published PDF retains its tentative-agreement cover even after ratification. This report examines the ratified agreement's text rather than treating it as an unapproved proposal.

Contract Architecture Overview

The first agreement organizes scope, compensation, vacation and sick leave, hours, bidding and Reserve, grievances, and a neutral System Board across 24 numbered sections. Section 1 includes successorship language and an expedited arbitration route for scope disputes, alongside identified wet-lease, damp-lease and customer-specific staffing exceptions.

Specific negotiated protections coexist with substantial Company authority over line construction and customer-linked assignments. The exceptions and employee reporting deadlines matter as much as the headline rule. Read Sections 1, 2 and 7.

Scheduling & Assignment Framework

Section 7.A ordinarily schedules 20 work or Reserve days and ten days off in a 30-day month, or 20 work or Reserve days and eleven days off in a 31-day month. The Company chooses the lines of flying, but must publish bids, provide four days to bid, and award in seniority order subject to the stated exceptions.

Section 7.H establishes Reserve Availability Periods of no more than fourteen hours, a ten-minute assignment confirmation rule and at least two hours to report. Importantly, additional contact outside the assigned RAP may be required when staffing needs justify it, with different response conditions. The fourteen-hour RAP must not be described as an absolute daily contact ceiling. Section 7.F provides recording and retention rules for Crew Scheduling calls. Read Section 7.

Economic Structure

Section 3.B guarantees seventy credited block hours per bid-period month, subject to reductions for unpaid absences and dropped work. Section 3.C pays qualifying work on an off day at 120% of the hourly rate using 3.5 hours or actual flight/deadhead pay, whichever is greater. Deadhead pay under Section 3.D is generally 50% of scheduled or actual block time, whichever is greater.

Section 3.F requires payroll underpayment correction within five business days after notice. Guaranteed credited hours, additional day-off pay, and deadhead pay are not interchangeable concepts. Read Section 3.

Enforcement & Dispute Resolution Architecture

Section 13 provides a written grievance process generally requiring filing within thirty days after awareness, investigation and representation rights, and escalation toward the System Board. A missed Company response is not an automatic financial award; the text sets escalation provisions, including an automatic Board appeal where no Company decision issues within the defined time.

Section 14 establishes a three-member System Board including a neutral chairperson, and Section 1.E provides an expedited path for scope grievances. Board awards and any money actually recovered remain separate questions. Read Sections 1.E, 13 and 14.

Structural Strengths, Weaknesses & Comparative Flags

Established protections: A first signed bargaining framework with 70-hour monthly guarantee, defined bidding periods, enforceable grievance access, a neutral System Board, specified payroll-correction deadline, and recognition/successorship safeguards.

Important limits: Company control over line construction, specified contracting-out exceptions, short Reserve response times and additional on-call contact outside the fourteen-hour RAP. These should be weighed against the contract's formal protections rather than assumed away.

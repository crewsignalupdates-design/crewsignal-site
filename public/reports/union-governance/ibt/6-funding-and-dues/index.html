International Brotherhood of Teamsters

Financial Transparency & LM Reporting

What the International and local accounts show about resources, service costs and financial accountability for NetJets, Republic/Mesa and Sun Country flight attendants.

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific)FY2023 onward; source coverage stated

Financial Disclosures

CrewSignal examines what members finance, where spending authority sits, and what records connect that spending to work delivered. For IBT, this requires reading the International, its funds and the relevant local unions as distinct reporting bodies. Treasury size and expense totals provide context; service records establish what a flight-attendant group received.

The parent is EIN 53-0215427; OLMS file 000-093. The audited statements consolidate the General Fund, Strike and Defense Fund, Scholarship Fund, Disaster Relief Fund and Supplemental Benefits Trust. They do not consolidate every local treasury. The parent Form 990 summaries and LM-2 have their own reporting boundaries and classifications.

Financial years below end December 31; amounts are U.S. dollars. Sources include FY2023 audited statements, FY2024 audited statements, IRS-derived Form 990 summaries and the LM-2 source record. No reviewed source supplies a complete allocation of parent or local costs to the three carrier groups.

How Resources Reach Representation

  • International: per-capita receipts, central staff and specialist services, designated funds, administration and payments to affiliates.
  • Local unions: member access, bargaining and enforcement work, local collections and expenses. A local can cover several employers and occupations.
  • Employers and other contributors: contractually paid release time, shared dispute expenses and reimbursements can support representation alongside union funds.

The International department descriptions and Airline Division service description identify legal, financial, research and bargaining capacity. They do not establish the cost or result of an assignment to a particular carrier. A combined comparison must identify the final payer and workgroup, and eliminate transfers between bodies already included in the total.

Fund Rules and the Current Governing-Document Limit

The FY2024 audit’s Note 2 describes organizing designations of 10% of per-capita tax, excluding Canada, and Strike and Defense Fund transfers of 15%, excluding Canada and public-sector members without a legal right to strike, with rail-merger exceptions. These are policies described in the FY2024 statements, not a newly verified statement of every rate or exception governing in November 2026. The strike fund is board-designated; that designation should not be described as a donor restriction. (FY2024 audit, Note 2, printed pp. 32–33)

The official site still links the Constitution adopted in June 2021; a consolidated text incorporating all June 2026 amendments was not located. The September 3, 2026 court order separately addresses oversight funding and financial controls (¶¶45 and 47). Current authority and expenditure obligations must be read with those later instruments and the applicable local rules. See Overview & Governing Documents and Governance Structure & Decision Authority.

Two Views of the Same Costs

The natural-expense classification for professional fees is $20,541,791 in 2023 and $23,691,111 in 2024. It overlaps the functional categories above. Do not add professional fees to legal/litigation or Civil RICO as extra spending. The salary, employee-benefit and tax category is likewise another view of costs, totaling $70,903,712 and $75,082,793. (Both audits, Note 23, printed p. 48.)

The broader strike-and-defense function also differs from direct strike-benefit assistance. The audited cash-flow statements report assistance payments of $8,234,121 in 2023 and $23,479,051 in 2024. None of these totals establishes a flight-attendant share.

What Would Establish a Service?

For a legal, financial or advisory engagement, identify the matter, the requested work, staff or vendor, approving body, invoice or payroll cost, paying treasury, reimbursements and beneficiary group. Then connect the cost to work delivered and a documented result. Complete LM-2 itemizations may identify payees; an engagement record and case ledger are still needed to distinguish bargaining assistance from institutional administration or another purpose.

Related Entities and Commercial Services

The FY2024 audit describes cost sharing with related entities and specified exceptions. It records administrative costs absorbed by the International of $148,164 for disaster relief and $190,428 for the scholarship fund. Related-entity amounts due at year-end are balances, not annual payments or carrier benefits. (Note 13, printed p. 43)

Affinity-program royalty revenue was $2,939,824 in 2023 and $3,746,497 in 2024. Those commercial arrangements invite questions about member prices, selection, service quality and the financial terms. A royalty from an optional legal-plan product is revenue, not the International’s bargaining-related legal expense. (FY2023 Note 17, printed pp. 43–45; FY2024 Note 17, printed pp. 44–45)

The union-hosted flight-attendant agreement identifies Local 284 as an authorized representative in §§1.1–1.2. Targeted primary contract text establishes that relationship; full-file comparison and current option notices remain incomplete in this review. A primary Local 284 financial return was not recovered, so no newly verified local dollar table is presented.

Local 284’s EIN 31-0917877 remains a secondary identification lead pending primary filing confirmation. Even after that return is obtained, the local’s other workgroups must be separated before attributing costs to NetJets flight attendants. Contract §22.5(G) also allocates certain board, travel and witness costs between the union and company; those obligations are not actual payment totals.

Republic/Mesa and Local 135

The Local 135 Republic directory identifies flight-attendant representatives, stewards and committees. The October 13, 2026 NMB determination, R-7690 extends IBT Airline Division certification to the combined Republic/Mesa flight-attendant craft and terminates the former Mesa certification. That representation decision is distinct from the corporate merger, joint-contract ratification and operational integration.

The Local 135 summaries above cover the whole local, including years before the combined certification. They cannot be relabeled as a combined-carrier service budget. The merger agreement below identifies a more specific financial pathway, but paid claims and the allocation among the International, Local 135 and CWA-AFA remain to be established.

Republic/Mesa: A Financial Trail for the Merger Work

The published Merger Transition Agreement (MTA) contains a concrete employer reimbursement mechanism. Section F, printed pp. 9–10, covers specified seniority-integration, MTA, JCBA negotiation and ratification costs, professional fees and expenses, and flight-pay loss. Section E.4 puts specified travel within the same cap.

The ceiling is $1,000,000 in total across both union parties combined, including flight-pay loss. It is not $1 million for each union and does not establish that the ceiling was exhausted. The union parties are IBT Airline Division, as represented by Local 135, and CWA-AFA; the public record does not establish the share paid to each treasury or service provider.

Section F covers costs incurred through December 31, 2025. Continued reimbursement depends on the stated condition that a JCBA was submitted for a ratification vote by that date and remains subject to the aggregate cap. A later ratification date does not, by itself, establish the submission date.

The accessible MTA counterpart’s DocuSign certificate is incomplete: it shows status Sent and three signatures (PDF pp. 15–16). The September 1, 2026 NMB decision independently records entry into an MTA on October 19, 2025 (printed p. 128). A completed counterpart is still needed for version control. No actual reimbursement total or inter-union allocation has been verified.

Records Needed to Follow the Money

  • The final executed agreement, amendments and date of submission for ratification.
  • Professional engagements, invoices, representative release-time calculations and reimbursement claims.
  • Employer payment records, the receiving union treasury and matching ledger entries.
  • The allocation to IBT, Local 135, CWA-AFA and each service, including any unreimbursed cost financed by members.

These records distinguish an employer-paid service, a union outlay later reimbursed and centrally absorbed staff work. Count the final economic cost once and connect it to the work delivered. The contract-administration review explains the related release-time and shared dispute-cost provisions.

What the Financial Record Can Establish

The review establishes parent financial scale, separate reporting scopes, selected costs, specific affiliate receipts, whole-local tax summaries and a merger reimbursement pathway. It does not establish the complete cost of representing any one flight-attendant group or a return-on-dues score. Reserves, professional-fee totals and the number of management layers require service evidence before they can support a judgment about value.

Questions for the Parent and Relevant Locals

  • Which complete original or amended LM-2 reports and attachments finish the FY2023–2025 series, and what does the missing Item 69 explain?
  • How do the full Forms 990 and Schedule D reconcile the audit, tax-summary and local net-asset differences?
  • What legal, financial, research or advisory work did the International provide to each carrier group, who requested it, and what did it cost?
  • How much was retained or spent at each level after inter-union remittances and employer reimbursements?
  • Which cases, bargaining assignments and implementation tasks were completed, how quickly did members receive help, and what remedies or other results were delivered?
  • What current governing rules, approvals and corrective-action records make those services and costs accountable to members?

The next step is a matched record of dues, assignments, invoices or payroll, reimbursements and service outcomes for the same entity, workgroup and period. Missing data remain an unresolved question rather than zero expenditure, nonfiling or proof of misconduct.

International Brotherhood of Teamsters

Governance Structure & Decision Authority

Who can decide, intervene, spend and review—and what those powers mean for service at the local level.

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific time). Constitutional references use the published 2021 baseline; later court requirements are identified separately.

Responsibilities and Reserved Powers

The International Brotherhood of Teamsters (IBT) combines local operating authority with powers held by Joint Councils, industry divisions and International officials. A member’s service contact and the body authorized to make the final decision can differ. The governing-document review explains the missing consolidated 2026 constitutional text.

IBT governing bodies, authority and accountability questions
BodyAuthority in the published ConstitutionFunding and accountability questions
Local union Bylaws designate the principal executive officer, who supervises local business, staff and labor controversies. Article XIV §3 permits reasonable decisions not to pursue grievances judged without merit. Article XXII §1, p. 160; Article XIV §3, p. 119. How much of the local treasury supports staff and casework? Who explains a refusal or delay, and which review route applies?
General President and General Executive Board The President interprets governing rules, subject to appeal, approves subordinate bylaws and can impose trusteeships under specified procedures. The GEB holds governing power between conventions where authority is not delegated elsewhere. Article VI §§2–5, pp. 39–46; Article IX §1, p. 62. Who authorizes specialist assistance or intervention? Which treasury pays, and what service or corrective result follows?
Joint Council Geographic affiliation, judicial functions and local representation follow Article XV. Its delegate structure is distinct from convention-delegate elections. Bylaws provide for per-capita financing. Article XV §§1–7, pp. 119–123. What do the remittances buy? How promptly are disputes or appeals decided, and are reasons available to the affected members?
Industry division The GEB charters divisions and the President approves their bylaws. Their bargaining role also depends on certification and contract recognition. Article XVI, pp. 123–124. Which negotiations, staff assignments and expenses belong to the division? How are they distinguished from local work and employer reimbursements?
Audit Committee Three International Trustees and an independent accounting professional perform the review and reporting duties described in Article VIII. The Trustees may attend GEB meetings but do not vote there. Article VIII §§1–3, pp. 60–61. What findings reach decision-makers, what corrections follow, and what can members learn from published financial records?

Page numbers above are printed constitutional pages. The PDF viewer’s page number is five higher.

Division authority cannot be treated as universally subordinate to a local’s bargaining role. On October 13, 2026, the NMB extended the IBT Airline Division certification to the combined Republic/Mesa flight-attendant group and extinguished the former CWA-AFA certification. The certified organization and the local service interface are separate facts to establish. (NMB R-7690, conclusion, printed p. 12; carrier service map)

Internal Review and Intervention

Ordinary disciplinary charges generally begin before the local executive board, with exceptions that change the hearing body. Article XIX provides internal appeal routes through the Joint Council, GEB and, where available, the convention. Written charges, notice, disqualification of interested decision-makers and findings are part of the framework. The correct route depends on who acted and the kind of decision. (Article XIX §§1–6, pp. 134–144)

A trusteeship can replace local management: Article VI §5 authorizes a trustee to remove officers and control the affected body, subject to its hearing and appeal provisions. The September 2026 order also gives the Independent Review Officer discretionary review of trusteeship imposition after internal appeals are exhausted. A power to intervene is therefore a significant limit on local control. (Article VI §5, pp. 41–46; September 3 order, ¶32)

The September 2026 Oversight Transition

The signed September 3 order preserves the court’s enforcement jurisdiction and independent election supervision. Its disciplinary provisions change who receives, investigates and reviews complaints:

  • Internal compliance and audit: the Office of Compliance Standards and Investigations (OCSI) and internal and affiliate audit departments become permanent, adequately resourced functions. New complaints received by the Independent Investigations Officer (IIO) after effectiveness generally go to OCSI, subject to specified confidentiality, inquiry and referral exceptions. The IIO cannot begin new investigations; pending matters follow completion and referral provisions, and its authority ends December 31, 2026. (¶¶19–23.)
  • Continuing independent review: the Independent Review Officer (IRO) continues for three years from effectiveness, with defined disciplinary review, records access, annual reporting and policy-review powers. Trusteeship review is discretionary after internal appeals. An extension requires the court process in ¶38. (¶¶24–39.)
  • Eventual successor: an Independent Compliance Professional succeeds the IRO when its authority ends and becomes a permanent Audit Committee member. The President appoints this professional in consultation with the GEB; removal requires good cause and 75% GEB approval. (¶¶40–44.)

Source: signed September 3, 2026 order, docket 4658, ¶¶5–6, 9–16 and 19–44. The future compliance role should not be read as an appointment already completed.

Complaint Access During the Transition

The Independent Disciplinary Officers’ current notice warns that its legacy reporting channels cannot guarantee confidentiality or anonymity during the transition. A focused review of the official About, Departments and Contact pages did not locate a named public OCSI intake route or policy. That is a limit of the retrieved record; it does not prove no route exists. The general contact form is not verified confidential OCSI intake.

Accessible reporting, investigation time, reasoned dispositions and correction of audit findings are service measures for the replacement system. Any claimed savings also need to account for continuing OCSI, audit, IRO and eventual compliance-professional costs. The order requires adequate funding of the independent functions. (¶¶19, 33–37 and 45)

Testing the Effect on Local Service

Follow a request from the member to the deciding official, then identify the work performed, payer, reimbursement and result. This makes it possible to evaluate whether an intermediate or International body adds expertise, resolves a delay or introduces another approval. The financial review keeps parent and local accounts distinct so the same service is not counted twice.

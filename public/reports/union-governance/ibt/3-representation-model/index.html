International Brotherhood of Teamsters

Representation & Member Services

Who represents flight attendants, how they obtain help, what local and International bodies provide, and what evidence shows about the services financed by members.

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific time). Dates and unresolved questions reflect the public record reviewed through that date.

Who Represents Members, and Who Delivers the Service?

A member may ask a local steward for help while the certified bargaining representative, specialist resources and funding sit at another level. CrewSignal follows those relationships to evaluate access, decisions, work delivered and results for the dues members pay.

At the combined Republic/Mesa operation, the National Mediation Board (NMB) identifies the IBT Airline Division as the certified flight-attendant representative. Local 135 is the documented Republic service and bargaining interface. Describing the division only as a supplement to local bargaining authority would miss this distinction. (October 13, 2026 certification, 54 NMB No. 2, printed p. 12; Local 135 Republic directory)

The Governance Structure & Decision Authority page explains the constitutional powers of locals, Joint Councils, divisions and the International. The carrier map below identifies the relationships supported by the current flight-attendant record.

Sun Country and Allegiant: Ownership and Representation

Allegiant completed its acquisition of Sun Country on May 13, 2026. Its third-quarter SEC filing says the airlines still operate separately, forecasts a single FAA operating certificate in 2028, and identifies joint labor agreements as part of full integration. Corporate ownership, operational integration and bargaining representation are separate milestones. (Closing Form 8-K, Item 2.01; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, “Acquisition of Sun Country Airlines”)

Allegiant flight attendants are documented as TWU Local 577; Sun Country remains the IBT/Local 120 group in the reviewed record. IBT’s January 15, 2026 merger statement incorrectly names the Allegiant flight-attendant union. The employer’s April 16, 2024 contract announcement identifies TWU Local 577. Announcements about IBT pilots, mechanics or dispatchers at the same airline do not establish IBT flight-attendant representation.

Local Service Access

Local 135’s Republic directory gives members base-steward, committee and business-agent contacts. It directs ordinary questions to the base steward or relevant committee and identifies agents for immediate assistance. The grievance committee describes disciplinary meetings three days a week and written call records; safety representatives report twice-monthly ASAP and fatigue-program meetings, while scheduling representatives describe monthly work with Crew Planning. These are published commitments and access routes; the page does not establish response times or case outcomes. (Local 135 directory, Members and committee sections)

For each carrier, the service test is whether members can reach the responsible person, understand the next step, obtain a timely decision and challenge an unresolved problem. A contact directory is an input to that evaluation. Case records, reasons for decisions and implemented remedies show what followed.

Specific committee authority, grievance machinery and published cost obligations appear in Contract Administration, Enforcement & Committees.

Sun Country: Bargaining Work and Reported Results

The union’s dated account places the start of negotiations in December 2019, a 19-month pandemic pause, rejection of a May 2023 proposal by 96% of voting members, and the start of mediation in December 2023. An agreement in principle followed in January 2025, before the March ratification announcement. Those interruptions and member decisions matter when assessing the elapsed time. (IBT’s January 2024 bargaining account; January 30, 2025 agreement announcement)

IBT’s March 3, 2025 release describes an immediate 21% wage increase, increases of up to 58% over the term, and improvements in retirement, holiday pay and progression. These are the union’s descriptions of negotiated gains. The employer’s February 12, 2026 financial results, Special Items footnote separately report ratification-bonus payments to eligible flight attendants. This provides evidence of an implemented payment; it does not establish each member’s net benefit after dues or the cost of the union services that produced the agreement.

International Support: Capacity and Documented Activity

The Airline Division describes bargaining, benefits analysis, legal/Railway Labor Act assistance, contract data, safety and training support. Its aeromedical program is expressly for pilots. The listed capabilities establish what the parent says it can provide; carrier-specific assignments, hours, invoices and results are needed to measure delivery to flight attendants. (Airline Division services, including Contract Negotiations, Legal Representation and Training & Development)

Reported peer-support training in 2024

IBT’s February 6, 2024 account reports Critical Incident Response Program training at Local 135 involving flight-attendant, pilot and technician peer volunteers. It provides a referral route through business agents or safety representatives. The account does not identify the participating flight attendants by carrier or provide a complete budget, utilization series or measured outcomes. (“Solidarity and Care, Even in the Air”)

A historical attribution of parent support

In an October 5, 2015 release about a NetJets flight-attendant tentative agreement, Local 284 credited the Airline Division and the International’s Economics, Capital Strategies, and Strategic Research and Campaigns departments. This identifies specific claimed contributions worth tracing. It does not establish their cost, independently allocate credit or demonstrate equivalent work in 2023–2026. (Union-issued 2015 release, Local 284 president’s statement)

What CrewSignal Will Measure

  • Access: coverage by base, first-response time, emergency availability and completed referrals.
  • Decisions: who authorizes bargaining positions, settlements, arbitration and specialist spending; what reasons members receive.
  • Delivery: case handling, contract education, implementation work and specialist assistance actually provided.
  • Results: timely remedies, corrected payroll or schedules, implementation of negotiated terms and recurrence of problems.
  • Cost: local and International spending, employer funding, reimbursements and the allocation to the members served.

The review has not recovered a complete carrier-level service budget, case-outcome series or reliable member-satisfaction sample. Those gaps limit a value-for-dues calculation; they do not establish that no service occurred. Financial Transparency & LM Reporting follows the funding evidence, and Elections & Member Control examines members’ influence over leadership.

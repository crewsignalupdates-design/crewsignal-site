From Contract Terms to Delivered Remedies

A negotiated provision has practical value when it is implemented and enforced. CrewSignal examines the route from a member’s concern to investigation, a decision, escalation and a completed remedy, together with the authority and funding behind each step.

The published 2021 Constitution addresses majority approval of ballots cast and rank-and-file participation in bargaining committees in Article XII, and local grievance discretion in Article XIV §3. This is a dated constitutional baseline: a consolidated text incorporating all June 2026 amendments was not located. A grievance filing does not automatically establish that arbitration will follow. (Article XII, printed pp. 85–89; Article XIV §3, printed p. 119; governing-document status)

Internal authorization, a carrier’s contract procedures and lawful strike conditions must be distinguished. The Sun Country FY2025 Form 10-K’s RLA discussion explains why an amendable date does not simply end an agreement. Official IBT strike guidance and general FAQs contain inconsistent benefit figures; this review does not state a universal current airline strike benefit.

Grievances and System Boards

Republic’s base agreement

Article 18 describes a settlement conference, a two-member System Board or agreed mediation, and escalation to three-member arbitration. It includes union-representation rights. Article 19 defines the Board, neutral selection and decision process. These are the base agreement’s procedures, subject to applicable amendments. (Articles 18–19, printed pp. 18-1–19-5; PDF pp. 57–62)

Sun Country’s published text

Article 26 provides monthly grievance hearings and a 30-day appeal period after an unsatisfactory decision; Article 27 establishes a three-member System Board. The date-placeholder qualification above applies to this source. (Articles 26–27, PDF pp. 120–125)

NetJets’ conditional expedited process

If the company exercised the extension option, §30.2(E) provides expedited arbitration for designated grievances, including April 30, 2026 designation and July 31 hearing-start milestones. The reviewed record does not establish that the option, case designation or hearings occurred. (Amended agreement, §30.2)

For each process, the unresolved performance questions are the age and disposition of cases, reasons for declining or withdrawing them, missed deadlines, settlement implementation and access to review. A procedural route alone does not demonstrate effective enforcement.

Committee Access and Accountability

Local 135’s Flight Attendant Advisory Board is designed to meet quarterly with the local Executive Board. Its recommendations are nonbinding, but business agents must reply and recommendations enter the board agenda. The published current-member roster still says updates are forthcoming. (Republic directory, Flight Attendant Advisory Board)

This supplies a concrete accountability test: are the roster and meeting schedule current, do members’ concerns reach the decision makers, and do recorded recommendations receive responses and follow-through? The member-services page identifies the ordinary access routes. Agendas, minutes and case records would show how those routes perform.

Republic/Mesa Merger Work and Funding

The NMB records a January 22, 2026 JCBA ratification, April 21 seniority-list presentation and July 22 boarding-pay implementation. These are identifiable work products and implementation events. They do not allocate all credit or cost among the local, International, CWA-AFA and employers. (September 1 decision, printed p. 128)

The published MTA’s Section F provides employer reimbursement for specified merger work, professional services and flight-pay loss, with a $1 million combined ceiling for both union parties. Coverage through December 31, 2025 has a conditional continuation tied to submitting the JCBA for ratification by that date. The incomplete public execution copy and unverified payments limit what can be concluded about actual receipts. (MTA, pp. 9–10 and execution record; full reimbursement analysis and evidence limits)

Evidence Needed to Assess Enforcement

  • Case handling: issues opened and closed, age of pending cases, decision reasons, appeals and remedies implemented.
  • Member access: first-response and escalation times, base coverage and the route for challenging an unanswered concern.
  • Specialist work: counsel and expert assignments, time records, authorization, invoices and results attributable to the carrier group.
  • Implementation: payroll corrections, seniority appeals, scheduling changes and completion of negotiated protections.
  • Cost reconciliation: release-pay calculations, employer reimbursements, local/International support and allocation methods.

The public evidence reviewed does not supply a complete case-level performance series or carrier service budget. It supports specific authority, procedures and reported work; a service ranking requires consistent outcomes and denominators.

International Brotherhood of Teamsters

Overview & Governing Documents

The governing rules, recent changes, and organizational responsibilities behind services financed by members.

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific time). Document versions and unresolved questions reflect the public record reviewed through that date.

The Business Question: What Do Members Receive?

Members finance representation and can reasonably evaluate its cost, accessibility, responsiveness and results. CrewSignal asks who receives a request, who can decide it, what work is delivered, who pays, and how a member can challenge a failure. A large treasury or another organizational layer does not, by itself, establish value for dues.

Members also have a role in governing their union. The IBT Constitution provides participation and election rules; covered U.S. unions are also subject to statutory protections for member speech, participation, elections and access to specified records. Those rights are part of the service-accountability assessment. (2021 Constitution, Article IV; U.S. Department of Labor: member rights)

Governing Documents and Their Limits

  • IBT Constitution — adopted June 22–24, 2021: the official About page still links this 2021 booklet. It is the dated constitutional baseline used here. A consolidated text incorporating all June 2026 convention amendments was not located. The March 2022 upload path does not make it a 2022 constitution.
  • 2025–2026 election rules — effective September 10, 2025: the final rules govern convention-delegate and International officer elections. Their preamble excludes ordinary subordinate-body officer elections. See Articles II–IV for delegate elections, nominations and membership balloting; Article XVII states the effective date.
  • Revised court order — September 3, 2026: the signed order in United States v. IBT, docket 4658, changes disciplinary oversight while retaining independent election supervision and court enforcement. Paragraphs 7–18 address elections; paragraphs 19–44 establish the oversight transition.
  • Local and carrier instruments: approved bylaws, bargaining agreements and government certifications establish additional responsibilities. For example, the NMB’s October 13, 2026 Republic/Mesa decision, conclusion, printed p. 12, identifies the IBT Airline Division as the certified representative for the combined flight-attendant group. See Representation & Member Services for the carrier-specific service map.

The constitutional baseline and later governing instruments must be read together. Failure to locate a consolidated booklet does not establish that no amendments were adopted.

The June 2026 Convention: A Text Gap Remains

Teamsters for a Democratic Union, a member caucus, reported that the convention adopted a constitutional amendment requiring New Officer Training and Education for newly elected officers. The exact adopted wording, article reference, exceptions and effective date were not recovered. The report establishes a specific amendment to investigate; it does not supply the complete operative text. (TDU convention report, June 17, 2026)

The Winter 2026 magazine’s election platform also proposed changes concerning strike benefits and officer accountability. Campaign proposals cannot establish what the convention ultimately adopted. The next document needed is the adopted amendment text or a consolidated constitution, with its effective dates. The 2021 baseline’s amendment procedure appears in Article III §9, printed pp. 26–27.

Local Operations Within Reserved International Powers

The IBT structure combines local administration, Joint Councils, industry divisions, International officers and the General Executive Board. The Constitution assigns substantial operational duties locally, while reserving interpretation, bylaw approval, governing and trusteeship powers to International officials. Joint Councils also have judicial functions. The allocation therefore requires more precision than a general description of local autonomy. (2021 Constitution, Articles VI, IX, XV–XVI and XXII)

The September 2026 order makes the Office of Compliance Standards and Investigations and internal and affiliate audit departments permanent. It ends the Independent Investigations Officer’s authority on December 31, 2026, continues the Independent Review Officer for three years from the order’s effective date, and provides for a later Independent Compliance Professional. Independent election supervision and the court’s enforcement jurisdiction continue. (Signed order, ¶¶5–6, 9–16 and 19–44)

See Governance Structure & Decision Authority for intervention and review routes, including the current complaint-intake uncertainty, and Elections, Terms & Member Control for the actual June 2026 election outcome.

From Authority to Delivered Service

Shared expertise can help a local resolve a difficult case or negotiate a contract. Additional approvals can also affect how quickly members receive help. The organizational chart alone establishes neither result. Evaluation requires requests, authorizations, staff or adviser work, payments, reimbursements and outcomes tied to the members served.

The Financial Transparency & LM Reporting review follows available records from FY2023 forward and keeps parent, local and carrier figures distinct. Earlier governing documents and service examples are used where they explain current authority or warrant a longer investigation.

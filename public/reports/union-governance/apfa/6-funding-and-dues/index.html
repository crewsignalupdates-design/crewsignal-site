Read the financial records on their stated terms

APFA’s union fiscal year runs April 1–March 31. This page brings together the original IRS returns for fiscal years ending in 2023–2025, the available LM-2 evidence, APFA’s FY2026 filing announcement and separate PAC summaries. Annual cash flows, year-end balances, internal fund movements and calendar-year compensation answer different questions.

The original IRS returns report three consecutive annual revenue shortfalls totaling $6,718,898. APFA’s later FY2026 announcement reports a $709,527 increase in cash and a $315,711 increase in net assets. The source and accounting distinctions below are essential to reading that history.

Why the two reporting series need a bridge

For FY2025, LM-2 disbursements are $14,146,427, while Form 990 expenses are $16,417,076. Year-end net assets are $12,650,781 on the LM-2 series and $12,518,570 on Form 990, a $132,211 difference. Timing, classifications and noncash items require a reconciliation before causes can be assigned. A difference between forms is not, on its own, a demonstrated reporting error.

Sources: FY2025 LM-2, Statements A–B; previous site transcription, body not refreshed; FY2025 original, Parts I, X and XI.

Guide to reading the LM-2

APFA’s report describes the reporting union’s finances. It does not present the CWA-AFA sector/MEC/LEC reporting structure or establish a separate treasury for each APFA base. Each schedule should be read with its reporting period, definitions and explanations.

What the filing sections show
Filing section How to read it
Items 1–9 File identity, fiscal dates, amended/hardship/terminal indicators, mailing address and record location.
Items 10–21 Reported activities and controls, including PACs, audit/review, bond coverage, liabilities/pledges, election-date field, membership and dues. Treat election dates as statements made in that filing.
Statement A Beginning and ending assets and liabilities. Net assets equal assets less liabilities. A balance is measured at a date; it is not annual spending.
Schedules 1–10 Supporting detail for receivables, loans, sales/purchases, investments, fixed and other assets, accounts payable and other liabilities.
Schedules 11–12 Officers, employees, gross salary and other direct/indirect disbursements. Allocations to functional categories are already included in those totals.
Schedule 13 Membership categories supporting Item 20. The FY2024 explanation describes active members who pay in full; this is not a roster of every employee at the airline.
Statement B Cash receipts and disbursements during the year. Its balance change is separate from changes in total net assets.
Schedule 14 Other receipts, distinct from the more specific Statement B receipt categories.
Schedule 15 Representational activities, including negotiating, administering and enforcing collective bargaining agreements. The total does not isolate spending at individual bases.
Schedule 16 Political activities and lobbying. Do not equate this union category with a separate PAC’s election-cycle total.
Schedule 17 Contributions, gifts and grants.
Schedule 18 General overhead that supports operations and is not allocated to another functional category.
Schedule 19 Union administration, including internal governance, elections and meetings.
Schedule 20 Benefits; read the payee and purpose descriptions. FY2024’s detail principally concerns staff insurance and staff/officer retirement.
Item 69 and signatures Additional explanations and the certification/signature dates. Signature dates do not by themselves establish the government’s receipt date or the latest amendment.

Sources: FY2024 report body: header, Statements A–B, Schedules 1–20 and Item 69; March 27, 2025 OLMS letter, APFA classification.

Dues decisions and internal funds

The current Constitution states $41 regular monthly dues. Dues increases require the specified membership vote; assessments require the specified Board approval and membership ratification. The rule allocating 25% of ratified dues increases does not mean that 25% of all dues is allocated that way.

How internal funds support the operating account

APFA’s 2025–2026 updates call NNRF the Negotiations and Negotiations-Related Fund. The 2022 investment-policy resolution uses the singular wording “Negotiations and Negotiation-Related Fund” and describes withdrawals for eligible negotiation expenses reimbursing the APFA operating account, subject to its resolution and officer-approval controls. APFA’s July 2025 explanation distinguishes the restricted NNRF accounts from special-activity funds housed in a nonrestricted investment account. “Nonrestricted” in that description does not establish the current amount available for any use.

These internal reimbursements identify which APFA resources support costs. They are not new receipts from an outside party and do not remove the underlying union-wide expense. IRS donor-restriction terminology also differs from restrictions imposed by union policy; a return’s absence of donor-restricted net assets does not make every account available for general operations.

Sources: March 9, 2022 investment policy, PDF pages 2–3; July 9, 2025 treasurer update, NNRF and Special Activity Funds; FY2025 Form 990, Part X.

Base budgets, paid release and the FY2027 plan

Base representation operates within APFA’s union-wide budgeting and representative-compensation framework. Base headcount rules govern paid release; they do not create a documented dollar allocation to every Base Council Representative or a separate annual financial filing for each base.

The March 2026 Policy Manual uses one full-month trip removal per 1,150 flight attendants, prorated above that level. A base below the threshold receives one, subject to the stated annual review and smaller-base conditions; the calculation excludes furloughed flight attendants. Payment or trip removal charged to a base budget requires the Base President’s authorization. The constitutional BCR seat rule is a separate measure of authorized positions.

Sources: Section 5.C.8.a(2), printed page 5.6 / PDF page 64; Article III, Section 7, BCR positions.

Member records and remaining evidence

On June 18, 2026 APFA said its annual budget was available in the members-only area and announced a phased rollout of representative paid/worked-hours reports, interactive budget-versus-actual reporting and special-fund reporting. The public announcement was inspected; the authenticated reports and completion of later phases were not. The public financial landing page requires login, which does not establish that the records are absent for members.

The Constitution and Policy Manual provide member access routes through the Base President or National Treasurer. Those routes and the public filing record should be kept distinct when discussing transparency.

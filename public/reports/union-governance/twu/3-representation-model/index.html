Representation Begins Through Local Unions

TWU's constitutional structure places Local Unions at the center of member-facing representation. Article XII provides that TWU membership functions through Local Unions. Subject to the International Constitution, Local bylaws, and delegated authority assigned to officers and Executive Boards, the membership is the supreme authority within the Local; between membership meetings, the Local Executive Board administers Local affairs.

The distinction becomes concrete in grievance representation. Under Article XXIV, a member complaint is filed with the Local, section, or designated officer and referred to the appropriate section committee or Local representative for evaluation and handling with management. The Constitution further provides that the member's Local acts as the member's representative for grievances and employment-related matters unless the International Union itself is party to the CBA.

Primary source: TWU Constitution

Carrier-Local Structures Vary

TWU's International Constitution establishes a common framework without requiring every Local to use the same internal representation model. Local Executive Board members may be elected at large or through branches, sections, divisions, or another basis permitted by the Local bylaws, with non-at-large allocations required to provide reasonably proportionate representation.

At JetBlue, Local 579's 2025 election rules provided for four principal officers, one Executive Board Member at Large, and seven Base Executive Board Members—one each for BOS, EWR, FLL, JFK, LAX, MCO, and SJU—elected by eligible members at the respective bases.

At Southwest, Local 556's public bylaws establish domicile representation, including Domicile Executive Board Members elected by their respective domicile memberships and domicile-elected Shop Stewards.

Committees as Local Representation Infrastructure

Local 579's current public structure illustrates how substantial representational activity can be organized below the Executive Board. Its committee directory identifies grievance, scheduling, hotel, health/safety/security, professional standards, EAP, FADAP, elections, COPE, and other functions. Several identify an Executive Board liaison alongside a committee chair and participating members.

Those records establish significant carrier-Local representation infrastructure. They do not, without additional evidence, establish how every committee member is selected, what authority each committee may exercise independently, or what approval is required from officers or the Executive Board.

The Air Division

Article XII authorizes the International Executive Council to establish Divisions composed of groups of Locals with common interests and concerns. Each Division is administered by a director appointed by the International President with IEC approval.

TWU's Air Division identifies a Division Director and International officers, representatives, and staff serving an aviation jurisdiction spanning multiple carriers, crafts, and classes. The Division can therefore be materially important without being treated as an elected intermediate government.

Support, Participation, and Authority Are Different

Support means supplying expertise, personnel, research, legal assistance, bargaining assistance, training, or other resources. Participation means taking part in a process without necessarily controlling its decision. Authority means possessing a documented right to vote, approve, reject, direct, review, or supersede a decision.

The bargaining provisions demonstrate why the distinction matters. An International President's representative may participate in bargaining and have a voice while having no vote on Local negotiating decisions. Separately, the final CBA requires International written approval before it becomes binding.

The Service Organization and the Bargaining Representative

The four flight-attendant locals provide member-facing representation at Southwest, Allegiant, Flight Services International and JetBlue. The agreements name TWU as the bargaining representative. Local delivery, International authority and the entity paying for a service should therefore be identified separately.

FSI is a staffing company, not an air carrier. Local 578 represents FSI employees, including those assigned to Atlas charter flights; FSI remains the employer named in the agreement. FSI agreement, Section 3.A, printed page 5.

CrewSignal evaluates access to representatives, timely handling of problems, enforceable results, transparent decisions and identifiable cost. A department or committee establishes a source of potential support. Dated activity and case records show what it delivered.

Documented Coordination and Member Access

TWU reported that leaders from all four flight-attendant locals met for two days in April 2023, receiving staff and adviser briefings on communications, legislative issues and safety campaigns. That is a dated coordination activity; the report does not allocate its costs or measure resulting workplace improvements. TWU Cabin Crew Coalition update, April 10, 2023.

Local 579 publicly identifies grievance representatives and provides a contract-violation intake form that requests supporting records and relevant company responses. An Identified International Training Program

The Spring 2024 TWU Express announced a four-week International internship including Klarissa-Ann Principe of Local 577 and Maria Teresa Hank of Local 556. Its stated focus was political and legislative work in Government Affairs and COPE, with potential support to other International departments.

This identifies a specific program and selected participants connected to two flight-attendant locals. The announcement does not establish completed attendance, the payer or amount of released wages and travel, or later effects on member service. Those records are needed to evaluate the program’s cost and results.

Significance

TWU's representation model is better described as Local delivery supported by International and industry resources within a common constitutional framework.

This does not establish whether the arrangement is more or less effective than another union's structure. That requires evidence about actual representation, bargaining, contract administration, expenditures, and outcomes. It does establish that the Air Division's existence should not be treated as proof that Local decisions automatically pass through a divisional approval hierarchy.

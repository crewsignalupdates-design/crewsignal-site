Financial Governance, Dues & LM Reporting

How member dues move through TWU, who controls carrier-Local treasuries, what federal filings disclose, and where public financial visibility remains incomplete.

CrewSignal separates verified governing-document requirements, carrier-specific practice, analytical interpretation, and unresolved source questions. Structural features are not treated as proof of bargaining effectiveness or institutional causation without supporting evidence.

Financial Governance Is More Than Financial Disclosure

TWU's financial structure reflects the same allocation-of-authority model seen elsewhere in its governance. Members fund representation through dues or applicable service charges. Carrier Locals maintain their own treasuries and financial officers, while a portion of Local receipts supports the International Union through the constitutional per-capita structure.

Local 579's current member-facing dues guidance states that 70% of dues remains for Local expenses and lists regular dues of $49.81 per month effective January 2026.

Carrier-Local Treasury Governance

Local 556's bylaws require its Executive Board to adopt the annual budget and require an annual audit by a Certified Public Accountant. Committees cannot create liabilities or indebtedness for the Local without written Executive Board authority. The Financial Secretary-Treasurer maintains the Local's financial records under International Administrative Committee rules.

Local 578 provides an independent example. Its Financial Secretary-Treasurer receives Local money, keeps the financial books under IAC rules, reports to the Local Executive Board and International Secretary-Treasurer, and submits the records to an annual CPA audit under its bylaws.

TWU International: Upstream Financial Visibility

CrewSignal's existing FY2023–FY2025 International LM-2 review reports FY2025 membership of 157,778; $35,787,812 in per-capita tax; $27,679,831 collected on behalf of affiliates for transmission; $67,535,161 in total receipts; and $8,407,755 in reported representational activities. Those figures remain part of the International-level record and should be read as an upstream union filing rather than a JetBlue-, Southwest-, Allegiant-, or FSI-specific service ledger.

The International filing can show the size and categories of the upstream treasury. It does not by itself establish how much was spent representing a particular Flight Attendant bargaining unit or whether a particular expenditure caused a bargaining outcome.

Southwest / Local 556: Carrier Financial Control Case

Local 556 currently provides the clearest TWU Flight Attendant example for connecting a public carrier-specific governance document with a stand-alone federal financial filing. CrewSignal's existing FY ending September 30, 2025 review reports 21,598 members; $61 monthly regular dues; $14.429 million total receipts; $7.226 million in representational activities; and $4.298 million in per-capita tax.

Those values describe reported financial activity. They do not by themselves establish the quality or causal effectiveness of bargaining or member services.

JetBlue / Local 579

Local 579 has comparatively strong member-facing dues visibility: it publishes the progression of its dues and lists $49.81 beginning January 2026, together with the stated 70% Local share. CrewSignal has not yet independently surfaced a clean stand-alone annual Local 579 OLMS filing comparable to Local 556.

The appropriate status is therefore descriptive: Local identity is verified; current dues are publicly disclosed; officers and the Financial Secretary-Treasurer are publicly identifiable; and stand-alone annual filing visibility remains incomplete in this review.

FSI / Local 578 and Allegiant / Local 577

Local 578 is affirmatively identified as the FSI Flight Attendant Local through TWU governing and carrier materials. A stand-alone annual OLMS filing has not yet been independently surfaced in CrewSignal's review. The older Local 578 public dues FAQ may be retained as historical information but should not be presented as a current 2026 dues rate without a newer source.

Local 577 is likewise identifiable as the TWU bargaining representative for Allegiant Flight Attendants. The unresolved issue is stand-alone annual financial-filing visibility, not Local identity.

Entity-Name Caution: Local 592

Local 592's FY2025 LM-2 uses the unit name “TWU AIR DIVISION,” but Local 592 is a specific multi-carrier dispatcher Local rather than the entire Air Division treasury. CrewSignal therefore verifies the filer against union governing and organizational records instead of assigning organizational scope from an LM report's unit-name field alone.

Political-Action Reporting Is Separate

The TWU Political Contributions Committee is separately reported through the Federal Election Commission. Current 2025–2026 cycle data through August 31, 2026 reports approximately $2.222 million in total receipts, $1.956 million in total disbursements, and $902,750 ending cash on hand.

CrewSignal treats this as separate federal political-finance activity. PAC spending is not combined with LM-2 representational spending and is not used to imply that a political dollar would otherwise have funded bargaining.

CrewSignal Financial Evidence Standard

  • Verified financial fact: a value reported in an authenticated LM filing, governing document, audited statement, FEC filing, or other primary financial record.
  • Structural analysis: what governing documents show about who controls, approves, audits, or receives funds.
  • Resource inference: an observation about apparent financial capacity based on verified figures.
  • Causation hypothesis: a proposed connection between resources and bargaining, contract enforcement, staffing, delay, or outcomes. CrewSignal does not present such a connection as fact without additional evidence.

Significance

TWU's financial structure mirrors its broader governance model. Carrier Locals possess identifiable treasuries, officers, budgets, and member-facing responsibilities, while the International receives constitutionally structured financial support and retains financial rules, reporting, and oversight authority.

CrewSignal treats financial visibility as a source-by-source question, not a union-wide label. Treasury size, representational spending, and Local resources are evidence of financial structure and capacity; they are not, by themselves, evidence of bargaining effectiveness or causation.

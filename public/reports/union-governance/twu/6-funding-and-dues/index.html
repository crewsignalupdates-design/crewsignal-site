Follow the dues through the organization

TWU operates a member-funded service business with separate International and Local treasuries. The financial question is what those resources make available to members: bargaining support, contract enforcement, professional advice, representation time and accountable administration. Member voting and oversight rights remain part of that evaluation.

This review reads the International and Locals 556, 577 and 579 as separate reporting entities. It uses complete FY2023–FY2025 IRS Form 990 bodies for those four entities, the complete preserved parent FY2025 LM-2, and the official convention financial report. Local 578 remains a financial-source gap. Form 990 revenue, LM-2 cash receipts and four-year audited totals are not interchangeable.

Published Agency-Fee Expense Reports

TWU’s Winter 2023, 2024 and 2025 magazines publish statements calculating chargeable and nonchargeable expenses on a modified-cash basis, accompanied by text headed “Independent Auditors’ Report.” Their years end August 31. The published reports address that defined expense calculation. They do not supply the missing local financial-statement audits, the International’s complete semiannual audit series, or an opinion on internal-control effectiveness.

The reproduced report pages show no auditor name, signature or separately dated signature block. Their contents can be evaluated as official TWU publications, while the originally issued auditor reports remain records to obtain. Chargeability percentages should not be read as service-quality scores, administrative-overhead ratios or carrier-specific benefits.

Local 556 and the FY2024 drawdown

Local 556 reported a $2,732,790 deficit in FY2024 and a much smaller $24,194 deficit in FY2025. Ending net assets declined from $6,261,416 in FY2023 to $3,504,271 in FY2025, a $2,757,145 reduction. The FY2025 rollforward also includes a disclosed $161 negative adjustment; it is not all annual operating deficit.

Between FY2023 and FY2024, reported expenses increased $4,069,887. The current-officer/director compensation and other-salaries lines together increased $2,684,486, about two-thirds of that increase. Legal fees rose from $231,175 to $438,356; affiliate payments rose from $3,170,066 to $3,625,528. These category changes locate the questions, but do not establish why hours, staffing, release reimbursements or service demand changed.

FY2025 legal fees were $346,473 and payments to affiliates were $4,297,662. The sharp decline in the “general & admin” label should not be treated as a like-for-like efficiency gain: the 2025 form separately reports office, information technology and occupancy lines that were blank in the earlier returns. A detailed ledger is needed to reconcile classification changes.

The current governance review also records an International oversight and financial-review assignment in September 2026 and a November 4 index excerpt referring to a preliminary review. Those events are later than these financial periods. They do not establish that the FY2024 deficit caused the oversight assignment or prove misconduct. See decision authority and current oversight. The 2024 bylaws and tax disclosures identify reporting and audit controls. The three Local 556 returns also contain inconsistent audit answers: Part IV line 12a says no, while Part XII line 2b reports a separate independent audit. The actual audit reports are needed to resolve that inconsistency and assess compliance.

Local 577 and the cost of released representation time

Local 577’s reported net assets rose to $598,068 in FY2025. A useful service-input line is “Flight time loss contractors”: $119,037 in 2023, $154,761 in 2024 and $259,297 in 2025. This shows an increasing recorded cost of representation capacity; it does not identify hours, cases handled or member results.

FY2025 legal and accounting fees were $10,912 and $10,690. The returns report separately audited financial statements. The actual audit reports, their scope, recommendations and member-access arrangements remain records to obtain.

The Allegiant agreement’s release-pay rules and the January 31, 2025 CISM compensation MOA further identify who pays. The latter shifts scheduled peer-on-call rotations to company compensation of four hours per day, subject to quarterly review and a contractual reversion mechanism. The funding rule is documented; current use, continuation and service results require separate evidence. See contract administration and service funding.

Local 579 compensation liquidity and dues

Local 579’s FY2025 return reports $100,157 in direct current-officer compensation, but it also reports $379,510 in reimbursed officer wages and $106,266 in reimbursed officer fringe benefits. Schedule O explains that some compensation is paid through JetBlue and reimbursed by the union. A comparison using only the first line would omit substantial officer-associated cost. Nonofficer wages and related reimbursements must also be read across their separate lines.

Net assets rose $128,758 during 2025, but cash plus savings fell from $816,913 to $424,914, while net fixed assets increased from $19,996 to $532,399. Greater net assets therefore did not mean a larger liquid cash buffer. The underlying asset purchase, approval and service rationale should be documented before judging the value of that investment.

Legal fees increased from $68,839 in 2023 to $102,913 in 2024 and $171,883 in 2025. The return does not allocate that legal work to grievances, negotiations or other matters. The FY2025 return reports independently audited financial statements, but the actual audit body and resulting recommendations remain separate records to obtain. The FY2023 return reports a compilation or review rather than an audit; the later audit disclosure is not projected backward.

The public dues page lists $35 per month during 2023–May 2025, $48.36 during June–December 2025 and $49.81 beginning January 2026, with 70% remaining local. Those dated rates cannot be applied to all years as a constant. Part VIII identifies the $2,114,969 FY2025 membership-dues amount within the return’s contribution category; the summary label does not mean charitable donations funded the local.

Schedule O says the local files an annual financial report with the Department of Labor. CrewSignal has not recovered the corresponding standalone LM body in this review. The correct gap is retrieval and verification of that filing, not an assertion that no filing exists.

The public dues page also presents an agency-fee rate of 75.62% and $37.66 per month effective January 1, 2026, describes its basis as a 2024 audit, and links the separate 2024 report. That linked report prints 74.67% chargeable; 75.62% appears in the recovered 2025 report. The year, linked document and stated rate require clarification. This mismatch does not establish what was actually collected or an improper fee. Local 579’s published explanation · linked 2024 report · 2025 report.

Local 578: Identity Confirmed, Annual Finances Unresolved

The preserved IRS Texas exempt-organization registry identifies Transport Workers Union of America Local 578 as EIN 99-3009278. Its codes identify a 501(c)(5) labor organization subordinate to group exemption 0694, a Form 990 or 990-EZ filing-requirement classification, and an August fiscal-year-ending month.

This establishes a registry identity. It does not establish the local’s charter date, the exact period or accounting basis of a filed return, or inclusion in a particular annual group return. The inspected row has no tax-period entry or actual asset, income or revenue amounts. The registry’s exemption-ruling date and database creation dates must not be used as the local’s formation date.

No standalone annual return body was recovered for the financial years reviewed. The next records are the OLMS identity, actual annual returns or verified group-return inclusion, audits, opening balances, dues receipts and parent support. The absent amounts remain unavailable rather than becoming zeros. A separate historical EIN lead remains unverified as a predecessor and is not combined with this record.

The local’s July 2024 president update reports obtaining an EIN and setting up local operations. Its historical dues FAQ is not treated as a current 2026 rate. The signed FSI 2026–2031 agreement supplies release-pay and arbitration cost rules while the annual financial records are sought.

Carrier-Local Treasury Governance

Local 556's bylaws require its Executive Board to adopt the annual budget and require an annual audit by a Certified Public Accountant. Committees cannot create liabilities or indebtedness for the Local without written Executive Board authority. The Financial Secretary-Treasurer maintains the Local's financial records under International Administrative Committee rules.

Local 578 provides an independent example. Its Financial Secretary-Treasurer receives Local money, keeps the financial books under IAC rules, reports to the Local Executive Board and International Secretary-Treasurer, and submits the records to an annual CPA audit under its bylaws.

Accounting differences and future obligations

The parent FY2025 Form 990 reports $86,511,538 of net assets versus $85,028,155 in the LM-2. Cost-versus-market valuation of the Housing Investment Trust and Treasury holdings, convention inventory and smaller balance differences account for an arithmetic bridge. This is not a management-certified reconciliation; small inter-entity, cash and liability differences still need explanation. The two totals should not be presented as evidence of missing money.

The convention report uses a different June 30 date and a four-year reporting period. It also states that lifetime post-retirement health and welfare obligations are not accrued under modified cash. Low reported liabilities therefore do not mean every future commitment is included. The parent’s twice-yearly CPA audit requirement and local annual audit rules should be evaluated against the actual reports, periods and member-access arrangements. Sources: Constitution Articles IX and XVI; convention report, PDF pages 11 and 23; Form 990 Part XII; LM-2 Statement A and Item 69.

Separate entities and political reporting

Local 592’s use of “TWU AIR DIVISION” in an LM unit-name field does not make its dispatcher-local filing the entire Air Division treasury. Entity identity and scope must be established before aggregation. Likewise the separately identified TWU Reserve Fund is not synonymous with the parent’s operating reserves.

The TWU Political Contributions Committee has a separate FEC record, C00008268. Its cash and spending should be reported with a dated coverage period and kept separate from these union financial statements. This review does not carry forward the old August 2026 PAC snapshot as a current November balance.

What would demonstrate value for members

A useful next disclosure would connect each major service to the approving authority, payer, budget, actual cost, staff time, response standard and result. For grievances, that includes age and disposition, recoveries and reasons for declining cases. For bargaining, it includes funded staff and advisers, release hours, deliverables and implementation work. For oversight, it includes the assignment, findings, corrective actions and member access to the results.

The records already show resources, rules, payments and some negotiated service funding. They do not yet support a carrier-by-carrier return-on-dues score. CrewSignal will keep the verified financial trail visible while treating allocation, implementation and outcomes as questions requiring evidence.

Different Forms of Democratic Authority

TWU does not use a single electoral mechanism across every level of the organization. At the Local level, members directly elect officers and Executive Board representatives under the International Constitution and applicable Local bylaws. At the International level, Local Unions send delegates to the Constitutional Convention, and the Convention elects International officers and members of the International Executive Board. Industry Division directors, by contrast, are appointed rather than elected to the Division office.

The distinction matters because direct election, delegate election, ex-officio representation, and appointment are different sources of institutional authority.

Primary source: TWU Constitution

Local Elections

Article XV establishes three-year terms for Local officers and Executive Board members as the general rule, subject to specified exceptions, and provides for election by secret ballot. Local Executive Board representation may be organized at large or through branches, sections, divisions, or another bylaw structure designed to provide equitable representation.

Local 579's 2025 election placed President, Vice President, Recording Secretary, Financial Secretary-Treasurer, an at-large Executive Board member, and seven Base Executive Board positions before the membership. Each base representative was elected only by eligible members at that base.

The retrieved Local 556 bylaws, approved in January 2024, similarly provide for Domicile Executive Board Members elected by their own domicile memberships and domicile-level Shop Steward elections.

Convention Representation Comes Through Locals

Article XI allocates Constitutional Convention representation to Local Unions: one delegate for the first 300 members or fewer and another delegate for each additional 300 members or majority fraction. A Local entitled to multiple delegates may make certain elected officers ex-officio delegates through approved bylaws; other Convention delegates must be elected by secret ballot after a reasonable nomination opportunity.

The Constitution does not establish the Air Division as a separate voting constituency through which Local members must pass to reach the Convention.

Delegates' roll-call voting strength is also specified: one vote for the first 100 members represented and another for each additional 100 or majority fraction, with a ten-vote maximum per delegate. The 2025 Convention call distinguishes those weighted roll-call votes from the ordinary one-vote-per-delegate procedure. Delegate count and voting strength therefore should not be treated as interchangeable.

International Leadership

International officers and International Executive Board members are elected at each regular International Convention. Their electoral mandate therefore comes through the Convention's delegate structure rather than through an organization-wide direct membership ballot.

The regular Convention is the supreme authority in the International Union when convened. Between Conventions, the International Executive Council exercises the highest International authority when in session.

Actual 2025 outcome: TWU reported on September 16, 2025 that John Samuelsen and the United Unstoppable slate ran unopposed, with 78 International officers declared elected by acclamation. The top five were Samuelsen, Executive Vice President Alex Garcia, Secretary-Treasurer Jerome Lafragola, and Administrative Vice Presidents Curtis Tate and Mike Mayes.

Article XII §6 permits an unopposed nominee to be treated as elected without a ballot. This records how the Convention election concluded. It does not measure the preference of every individual member. International terms run until successors are installed at the next regular Convention; the Constitution ordinarily places those Conventions roughly four years apart, with specified timing adjustments.

Industry Division Leadership Is Different

Article XII provides that a Division director is appointed by the International President with IEC approval. That fact does not establish anything about the quality of Division representation. It defines the source of the director's institutional authority: appointment within the International structure rather than direct election by the Air Division membership.

Local 556: Succession and the Current Bylaw Question

The retrieved January 2024 bylaws distinguish two presidential situations. Article VI §3(c) assigns presidential duties to the First Vice President during the President's absence. Section 3(d) provides for succession when the President cannot complete the term. The same edition's §4(a) supplies a different rule for other board vacancies, including offering a position to the next-highest vote recipient during the first 18 months of a term.

The official September 21, 2026 news-index entry announces an International bylaw update, but the full interpretation and its effective terms were not recovered in this public review. The 2024 vacancy clause should therefore be identified as the retrieved historical text, with its current application unresolved. The International President's power to interpret the Constitution, and the review of that interpretation, matter as well as the wording of the older bylaw PDF.

An October 15 leadership update is also listed in the official index. A statement reproduced by an October 19 report says President Kristina Peterson took leave and First Vice President Sam Wilkins would perform the President's duties during her absence. This is a reported temporary arrangement; the full official notice and any subsequent change were not authenticated. It does not establish a resignation, removal, or new election. The Local 556 chronology identifies the evidence and outstanding records.

Elections Beyond the Executive Board

Member electoral authority can extend to functional positions. In the retrieved 2024 bylaws, Local 556's five-member Contract Negotiating Committee consists of the Local President, two members appointed by the Executive Board, and two members elected by the membership through secret ballot.

That creates a mixed selection mechanism inside one bargaining body: one ex-officio participant, two appointed participants, and two directly elected participants.

Control Between Elections

Members' authority is exercised through more than the next officer ballot. Constitutional mechanisms include local and section meetings, contract ratification, appeals over application of the Constitution or local bylaws, and proposed constitutional amendments. Article XXXIII permits amendment by a two-thirds Convention vote or a membership referendum through its specified initiation process.

Officer-removal rules also differ by office. Article VIII §3 combines an IEC trial process with membership-referendum approval for removal of specified International officers. Local officer discipline, suspension, removal, and appeals follow separate procedures in Articles XIX–XXII. A general claim that members can directly recall every officer would not accurately describe these rules.

For CrewSignal's service evaluation, the further questions are practical: Can members find the governing rule, use a review process in time, obtain an explanation of a disputed service decision, and see whether corrective action follows? Written rights and verified use of those rights are separate evidence.

Member Control Is Decision-Specific

For this particular decision, does the member vote directly, select a representative who votes, act through an elected board, or receive representation from an appointed official?

TWU combines direct Local elections, geographic or base representation, Local-originated Convention representation, delegate election of International leadership, and appointed industry-Division administration. CrewSignal therefore analyzes member control decision by decision rather than assuming that organizational height determines member influence.

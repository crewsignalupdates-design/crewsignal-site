Different Forms of Democratic Authority

TWU does not use a single electoral mechanism across every level of the organization. At the Local level, members directly elect officers and Executive Board representatives under the International Constitution and applicable Local bylaws. At the International level, Local Unions send delegates to the Constitutional Convention, and the Convention elects International officers and members of the International Executive Board. Industry Division directors, by contrast, are appointed rather than elected to the Division office.

The distinction matters because direct election, delegate election, ex-officio representation, and appointment are different sources of institutional authority.

Primary source: TWU Constitution

Local Elections

Article XV establishes three-year terms for Local officers and Executive Board members as the general rule, subject to specified exceptions, and provides for election by secret ballot. Local Executive Board representation may be organized at large or through branches, sections, divisions, or another bylaw structure designed to provide equitable representation.

Local 579's 2025 election placed President, Vice President, Recording Secretary, Financial Secretary-Treasurer, an at-large Executive Board member, and seven Base Executive Board positions before the membership. Each base representative was elected only by eligible members at that base.

Local 556's public bylaws similarly provide for Domicile Executive Board Members elected by their own domicile memberships and domicile-level Shop Steward elections.

Convention Representation Comes Through Locals

Article XI allocates Constitutional Convention representation to Local Unions: one delegate for the first 300 members or fewer and another delegate for each additional 300 members or majority fraction. A Local entitled to multiple delegates may make certain elected officers ex-officio delegates through approved bylaws; other Convention delegates must be elected by secret ballot after a reasonable nomination opportunity.

The Constitution does not establish the Air Division as a separate voting constituency through which Local members must pass to reach the Convention.

International Leadership

International officers and International Executive Board members are elected at each regular International Convention. Their electoral mandate therefore comes through the Convention's delegate structure rather than through an organization-wide direct membership ballot.

The regular Convention is the supreme authority in the International Union when convened. Between Conventions, the International Executive Council exercises the highest International authority when in session.

Industry Division Leadership Is Different

Article XII provides that a Division director is appointed by the International President with IEC approval. That fact does not establish anything about the quality of Division representation. It defines the source of the director's institutional authority: appointment within the International structure rather than direct election by the Air Division membership.

Elections Beyond the Executive Board

Member electoral authority can extend to functional positions. Local 556's five-member Contract Negotiating Committee consists of the Local President, two members appointed by the Executive Board, and two members elected by the membership through secret ballot.

That creates a mixed selection mechanism inside one bargaining body: one ex-officio participant, two appointed participants, and two directly elected participants.

Member Control Is Decision-Specific

For this particular decision, does the member vote directly, select a representative who votes, act through an elected board, or receive representation from an appointed official?

TWU combines direct Local elections, geographic or base representation, Local-originated Convention representation, delegate election of International leadership, and appointed industry-Division administration. CrewSignal therefore analyzes member control decision by decision rather than assuming that organizational height determines member influence.

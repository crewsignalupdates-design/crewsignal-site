Bargaining, Ratification, Grievances & Contract Administration

Who negotiates, who votes, who must approve, and how contract disputes move through TWU representation.

CrewSignal separates verified governing-document requirements, carrier-specific practice, analytical interpretation, and unresolved source questions. Structural features are not treated as proof of bargaining effectiveness or institutional causation without supporting evidence.

Bargaining Authority Has Multiple Control Points

Article XXV establishes several distinct elements of bargaining authority. Collective bargaining for a membership group is to be guided by the bargaining program and policy of the preceding International Convention. Any proposed agreement is subject to ratification by the members covered by it. The International President must be notified of negotiations and receive proposed demands in advance. The President or a designated representative may participate in bargaining with a voice, but without a vote in Local negotiating decisions. Every CBA also requires written approval from the International President or an authorized designee before becoming binding.

These provisions establish multiple control points. They do not support describing TWU bargaining as simply Local-controlled or simply International-controlled, and the Constitution does not prescribe a universal order between member ratification and International written approval.

Primary source: TWU Constitution, Article XXV

Carrier Bylaws Can Add Additional Gates

Local 556 provides a documented Flight Attendant example. Its public bylaws create a five-person Contract Negotiating Committee: the President presides, the Executive Board appoints two members, and the membership elects two members by secret ballot.

The same bylaws require a two-thirds Executive Board vote approving a proposed tentative agreement before it is presented to members. A tentative agreement or side letter must then be available for a specified membership review period before voting begins.

Those are Local 556 rules, not universal TWU procedure.

Grievances Begin Within the Local Structure

Article XXIV directs a member's complaint to the Local, relevant section, or designated officer. The complaint is referred to a section committee or Local representative to determine its merits and handle the matter with management. It further identifies the member's Local as the member's representative in presenting, maintaining, adjusting, and settling grievances and employment-related matters unless the International itself is party to the CBA.

The Constitution reviewed here does not establish a universal intermediate Air Division merits-review stage for every grievance.

Arbitration Is Not an Automatic Constitutional Right

Article XXIV does not obligate either the Local or International to process every complaint, take every grievance to arbitration, or litigate every matter. Its stated obligation is fair and good-faith representation free of hostile discrimination. Applicable CBAs, the Railway Labor Act, the duty of fair representation, Local bylaws, and other governing rules can add requirements.

CrewSignal therefore distinguishes a contractual grievance procedure, the union's internal decision over case handling, and an individual member's ability to demand arbitration.

Carrier-Level Contract Administration

Local 556's bylaws assign its President or designee responsibility for Board of Adjustment submissions, assembling facts for grievances proceeding toward adjustment or arbitration, ensuring proper presentation, and assisting officers, representatives, and members with grievance processing. Local 579 publicly identifies a Local Grievance Committee and contract-violation intake mechanism, while other committees address CBA-facing functions such as scheduling.

International and Air Division Assistance

TWU's Air Division maintains International staff with bargaining and representation experience. Where International personnel participate in negotiations, arbitrations, legal work, or major disputes, CrewSignal documents that involvement. International assistance is not characterized as International decisional control unless the governing document or transaction record establishes the relevant approval, voting, or directive authority.

Significance

TWU's contract-administration architecture is not accurately summarized as Local initiation followed by routine Division review and then International oversight. The verified structure is more functional: Local grievance agency and carrier administration; carrier-specific committees and officers; International resources where used; direct member contract ratification; and defined International approval and constitutional control points.

Authority by Function, Not Organizational Height

TWU authority does not operate through a single upward chain. Different governing bodies control different stages of a decision, and a single process can contain both direct member authority and International approval requirements.

Collective bargaining provides a clear example. The 2025 Constitution requires proposed agreements to be ratified by the members covered by them. The International President must be notified of bargaining and may personally participate or designate a representative, but that International participant does not vote on Local negotiating decisions. Separately, a collective bargaining agreement cannot become binding without written approval from the International President or an authorized designee.

The Constitution establishes multiple control points; it does not prescribe one universal linear hierarchy for every governance action.

Primary source: TWU Constitution, Article XXV and related provisions

Member Authority

Direct member authority exists at several important points. Within the limits established by the Constitution and Local bylaws, the membership is the highest authority in a Local. Members elect Local leadership through constitutionally regulated Local election procedures, participate through membership or section meetings, ratify proposed collective bargaining agreements covering them, and exercise specified voting rights in other consequential actions.

Member authority therefore should not be described merely as the starting point of a process that subsequently transfers upward. In some matters, the member vote is itself an indispensable decision point.

Local Administrative Authority

Between membership meetings, the Local Executive Board administers Local affairs. Local bylaws establish additional carrier- or workplace-specific structures within the International constitutional framework. The Constitution permits Locals to establish sections, divisions, branches, and appropriately allocated Executive Board representation where circumstances warrant.

Contract administration likewise contains substantial Local authority. Under Article XXIV, grievances begin with the Local, section, or designated Local officer; Local representatives evaluate and handle them; unresolved matters may proceed to the Local President or representative. Unless the International Union itself is party to the CBA, the Constitution designates the member's Local as the member's representative for grievances and employment-related matters.

International Participation and Support

International participation should be distinguished from International control. TWU maintains an Air Division staff and other International resources that can provide industry knowledge, bargaining experience, legal or strategic assistance, training, and institutional continuity. The existence of those resources does not establish that every Local decision is made by or routed through the Air Division.

Support is not necessarily control; participation is not necessarily a vote; oversight is not necessarily day-to-day administration.

The 2025 Convention's Resolution 6 reports more than 50 training sessions for officers and stewards across TWU divisions in the preceding years, covering subjects including bargaining, arbitration, treasury administration, and elections. The April 2026 Air Division presidents' conference also described International expertise and resources available to Locals. These are identifiable program reports. Carrier-specific costs, use of those resources, and member results require additional records.

International Approval and Oversight

TWU International institutions nevertheless possess significant documented control points. Local bylaws require IAC approval. The International President interprets and enforces the Constitution subject to constitutional review. The International has broad inspection authority over Local and related financial records. Collective bargaining agreements require written International Presidential approval. The Constitution also creates International appellate, disciplinary, charter, and specified strike-approval mechanisms.

These powers should be described specifically rather than generalized into a claim that all final decision-making resides nationally.

Financial Authority and the Controls Around It

Article XVII §3 sets the general International per-capita payment at 30% of dues and agency fees received by a Local, subject to the constitution's provisions for relief. This identifies the funding relationship. It does not allocate that payment among bargaining, legal work, organizing, administration, or a particular carrier's services.

Convention, IEC, IAC, and International President

“International” is not one undifferentiated actor. The Convention, International Executive Council, International Administrative Committee, International President, International Secretary-Treasurer, and International staff or Division personnel can possess different constitutional responsibilities. CrewSignal names the governing actor whenever the distinction changes the authority being analyzed.

Review, Discipline, and Formal Intervention

The International President's constitutional interpretations may be appealed by an adversely affected member or Local to the International Executive Council and then to the Convention. Local disputes have their own appeal routes under Articles XV and XIX–XXII, including the International Committee on Appeals. A unanimous Committee on Appeals decision and a divided decision have different further-review paths. The relevant procedure and deadline depend on the action being challenged.

Inspection authority also differs from exclusive administration of a Local. Article V §3 provides broad record-inspection authority. Article XVIII §3 separately permits an emergency administrator appointed by the IAC with approval from a majority of individual IEC members, pending the specified Article V proceedings. The administrator provision assigns exclusive power to act for the Local. An ordinary representative assignment or financial examination does not, by itself, establish that this procedure has been used.

For Local 556, the September 11, 2026 reproduced letter supports an oversight and financial-review assignment. The subsequent November notice refers to a preliminary review. Their documented scope and the unresolved full statements are set out in the current-developments record.

Internal procedures operate alongside statutory member rights. The Department of Labor's LMRDA guidance describes rights involving participation, speech, dues and assessments, financial disclosure, fair disciplinary procedures, and elections. The constitution's exhaustion language should not be read as an unlimited bar to a legal remedy.

CrewSignal Authority Test

For each material governance decision, identify: Who initiates? Who administers? Who participates? Who votes? Who must approve? Who may appeal? Who may reverse or supersede?

This functional test is used throughout the TWU reporting and is designed to support later cross-union comparison without changing the test to fit the organization being studied.

The financial and service questions follow the same decision: What money funded it, what service was delivered, how quickly, with what result, and what remedy exists if performance falls short? This connects formal structure to the value members receive while keeping documented authority separate from measured performance.

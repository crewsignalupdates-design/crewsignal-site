Overview

The Transport Workers Union of America is a multi-industry labor organization whose members operate through chartered Local Unions within a common International constitutional framework. TWU governance is not accurately described as a simple ladder in which authority passes from members to Locals, then through industry Divisions, and finally to International leadership.

Article XII of the 2025 TWU Constitution establishes a more differentiated structure. TWU membership functions through Local Unions. Subject to the International Constitution, Local bylaws, and authority assigned to Local officers and Executive Boards, the Local membership holds the highest authority within the Local. Between membership meetings, the Local Executive Board administers Local affairs. Separately, the International Executive Council may establish industry Divisions composed of Locals with common interests; each Division is administered by a director appointed by the International President with IEC approval.

Primary source: TWU Constitution, 2025 edition

Local Authority Within an International Framework

Local authority is substantial but not independent of the International Union. Each Local must adopt bylaws governing its structure and procedures, but those bylaws must conform to the International Constitution and established International policies and do not become effective until approved by the International Administrative Committee. Amendments are subject to the same approval requirement.

This creates a useful governance distinction: Local authority is constitutionally bounded rather than either wholly autonomous or wholly subordinate.

The Role of Industry Divisions

TWU industry Divisions provide an organizational structure for Locals sharing common concerns. The Air Division covers numerous carriers, crafts, and classes and is supported by a Division Director and International staff representatives.

The constitutional status of the Division matters. Article XII authorizes the IEC to establish Divisions and specifies appointment of their directors by the International President with IEC approval. It does not, by itself, establish the Air Division as an elected intermediate government through which all Local bargaining, grievance, or membership authority must flow.

CrewSignal therefore distinguishes industry coordination and International support from formal decision authority. Where a Division has a specific bargaining, grievance, policy, or merger role, that role should be established from the Constitution, Local bylaws, a CBA, formal policy, or another controlling source rather than inferred from organizational placement.

International Governance

At the International level, the regular International Convention is the union's highest constitutional authority when convened. Convention representation is allocated to Local Unions according to constitutionally defined membership formulas. When the International Executive Council is in session outside an International Convention, Article VIII gives it the highest authority in the International Union. The International President separately serves as chief executive officer, enforces and interprets the Constitution subject to the constitutional review structure, and possesses specified supervisory and inspection powers.

Local 556's recent developments make the distinction between ordinary oversight, a financial review, temporary leadership duties, and a formal intervention consequential. The following record separates documents read in full from indexed notices and reproduced statements.

A temporary leave and assignment of acting duties should be described on their own terms. Current operative bylaws, any Financial Monitor appointment, the complete review, and subsequent action are the documents needed to resolve the remaining questions.

Core Governance Principle

Distributed authority with defined International control points.

Authority is distributed because members, Local officers and boards, International bodies, and—where governing documents so provide—industry structures exercise different functions. International control points nevertheless remain consequential. They include approval of Local bylaws, constitutional interpretation and enforcement, financial inspection and audit authority, participation in collective bargaining, final written approval of collective bargaining agreements, specified strike approvals, internal appeals, and charter-related powers.

The resulting structure should not be characterized simply as either centralized or decentralized. The more useful inquiry is functional: for a particular decision, who initiates it, who administers it, who votes on it, whose approval is required, and who can review or reverse it?

Analytical Significance

That question is the organizing principle for the remainder of CrewSignal's TWU governance reporting. Elections, bargaining, contract ratification, grievance administration, finances, discipline, merger policy, and seniority integration may allocate authority differently.

The purpose of this series is not to assign a generalized label to TWU governance. It is to identify the actual decision rights and protections established by governing documents and carrier-specific practice, and then distinguish those verified structures from institutional claims or analytical hypotheses.

Services funded by dues are the practical test. CrewSignal asks what representation members receive for the money collected, who controls the resources and service decisions, and what evidence allows members to evaluate performance. Relevant measures include access to representatives, grievance timeliness and outcomes, bargaining support, financial reporting, and correction of service failures. Organizational size, an approval requirement, or a new oversight assignment does not determine the result of that evaluation by itself.

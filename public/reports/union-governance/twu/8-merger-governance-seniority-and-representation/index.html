Four Separate Systems

An airline consolidation can create simultaneous questions about contract continuity, seniority integration, bargaining, operational integration, and union representation. Those questions do not necessarily share the same decision-maker or legal source.

  1. TWU internal merger policy: governs matters assigned to the union's internal integration rules where applicable. Its current standalone text remains unresolved.
  2. Carrier-specific CBAs: may create independent successorship, seniority, bargaining, fence, timetable, furlough, or arbitration protections.
  3. McCaskill-Bond / Allegheny-Mohawk: supplies the federal seniority-integration framework for covered transactions, subject to statutory exceptions including qualifying same-representative internal policy and CBA provisions.
  4. National Mediation Board representation: separately determines Railway Labor Act representation disputes involving the combined craft or class.

Why the Internal Policy Can Matter

McCaskill-Bond generally imports the Allegheny-Mohawk seniority protections for covered airline combinations. Where the same collective bargaining agent represents the combining craft or class at both carriers, the statute recognizes that agent's internal integration policies, if any. Applicable CBA provisions can also be legally significant.

That is why recovery of TWU's current standalone Airline Merger Policy remains consequential. It is not merely an institutional-history question.

Representation Is a Separate NMB Process

The current NMB Representation Manual treats a merger broadly for representation purposes and permits an application seeking a determination that a single transportation system exists. The Board evaluates the record case by case and may consider operational, labor-relations, management, workforce, branding, ownership, and other integration evidence.

If the Board finds a single transportation system, it then addresses representation for the combined craft or class under its representation procedures. Existing certifications remain effective until the Board issues a new certification or dismissal.

Successorship protection is not the same as combined-craft representation. A CBA can protect recognition and agreement continuity for the incumbent list without displacing the NMB's authority over representation of a post-integration craft or class.

JetBlue: Contractual Process Architecture

JetBlue Article 3 is procedurally explicit. If JetBlue decides to operationally merge with another airline, TWU agrees to file a single-carrier application within the contractual period after closing. The agreement then provides a defined merged-contract bargaining process, mediation phase, and binding-arbitration backstop for unresolved contract issues.

Its seniority provisions separately invoke McCaskill-Bond / Allegheny-Mohawk principles and contain list-integration and displacement protections. If JetBlue itself is acquired and operational integration occurs, the agreement keeps the groups separate until seniority integration and requires written successor recognition and CBA assumption before the successorship transaction is finally concluded, subject to the RLA.

Southwest and Allegiant: Different Contract Designs

Southwest Article 3 uses a different architecture, including a seniority-dispute arbitration mechanism and acquired-by-another-carrier separation and successorship provisions. Allegiant's current agreement expressly invokes McCaskill-Bond and Allegheny-Mohawk for seniority integration, includes a required seniority-arbitration timetable, protects Allegiant-list Flight Attendants against specified transaction-driven furlough, and provides expedited arbitration for identified merger-clause disputes.

The comparison is descriptive: common International affiliation does not erase carrier-specific contractual design.

FSI / Local 578: Current Contractual Incorporation of TWU Policy

The signed FSI 2026–2031 agreement provides present-tense confirmation of the TWU Airline Merger Policy's contractual relevance. If the other Flight Attendant group is also TWU-represented, Section 3 applies both the CBA and the TWU Airline Merger Policy, prohibits a system flush, and provides that the CBA controls in a conflict.

If the other group has another union or no union, the agreement instead applies an Allegheny-Mohawk framework, maintains group separation until seniority integration, and prohibits a system flush. Representation of the integrated Flight Attendant group is expressly left to NMB rules and procedures.

This creates a documented hierarchy of authorities without supplying the missing text of the internal policy itself.

The Section 3.C trigger requires the specified transaction to result in both operational and flight-attendant list integration. Section 3.C.5 does not require integration merely because an acquisition occurs. The predecessor contained the same principal merger provisions, so their continuation should not be presented as a new 2026 gain. Member Ratification of Seniority Integration Cannot Be Assumed

Historical TWU integrations show that membership voting can be part of a transaction-specific seniority process. CrewSignal does not convert that historical practice into a universal current TWU rule. Where a seniority-integration agreement is submitted to members, the reporting identifies the source of the voting right—union policy, CBA, process agreement, Local rule, or transaction-specific decision.

Open Document Question: TWU Airline Merger Policy

The current standalone policy remains the principal unresolved governance document. CrewSignal continues to seek evidence answering: who adopted the current policy and under what constitutional authority; its effective date and scope; how integration representatives are selected; whether members possess any approval or ratification right; the current seniority standard and allowable equities; the arbitration mechanism; how two different TWU Locals are handled; how conflicts with a CBA are resolved; and which International, Division, Local, or committee actor may interpret or modify the policy.

Historical evidence that a prior TWU policy called for dovetailing is retained as historical evidence, not promoted into a current 2026 rule.

Funding Legal, Advisory and Integration Work

Procedural protection and representation funding are separate questions. JetBlue Article 3.E.4 assigns the company the cost of arbitration over unresolved merged-contract issues; Article 3.E.5 splits specified seniority-arbitration costs. Article 34.N.1.l requires the parties to address union legal and business expenses in specified transactions, without fixing an automatic reimbursement amount. JetBlue Article 3.E.4–5, printed page 6 · Article 34.N.1.l, printed page 162.

Southwest Article 3.5.B likewise divides the specified seniority-arbitration costs equally. Its acquired-by clause and JetBlue's acquired-by clause provide a 36-month seniority-integration period, distinct from a joint-contract bargaining deadline. Southwest Article 3.5.B and 3.6.A, printed page 3-2 · JetBlue Article 3.F.1, printed page 7.

For an actual transaction, the review should identify who retained advisers, what work they performed, which entity paid, whether an employer reimbursed the expense, and how released representative time was funded. Current standalone TWU policy and transaction records are still needed to establish any additional internal integration costs or voting rights.

Significance

TWU merger governance is best understood as overlapping but distinct layers: internal union policy, carrier-specific contract protections, federal seniority law, and NMB representation jurisdiction. CrewSignal keeps those layers separate so that a successorship clause is not mistaken for a representation guarantee, a historical policy is not mistaken for current text, and common union affiliation is not mistaken for identical carrier-level protection.

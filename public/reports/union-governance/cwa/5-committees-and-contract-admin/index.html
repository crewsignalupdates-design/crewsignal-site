Committees have different mandates

Article XVI establishes committees with distinct composition, selection and duties. Some consider proposals, some review finances or restricted funds, and some process credentials or appeals. They should not be grouped into a single category of general advice.

The parent bargaining framework

Article XVII’s general rule names CWA International as the collective bargaining representative and places bargaining under Executive Board direction. Agreements are in the International’s name and require approval by an authorized representative. Proposed negotiating subjects require the applicable bargaining council, Board or authorized representative’s approval.

A bargaining council formulates the agenda for its represented units. It includes a representative from each affected local under the general rule; ordinary voting is by show of hands, with a 20% request triggering a weighted vote. Telecom bargaining has additional provisions for issues crossing district or sector lines, including the agreement of affected vice presidents and a presidential role in resolving an impasse.

Members of a bargaining unit may select a bargaining committee by popular vote, through Convention delegates or by another Board-approved method that assures fair representation. Selection remains subject to constitutional and national financial and numerical limits. The committee must be consulted on agreements amending or augmenting the contract throughout its term.

Sources: Constitution, XVII.1–4, PDF pp.33–35.

Approval and ratification are separate

Under Article XVII.5, a contract is subject to secret-ballot ratification by affected members when the affected Convention delegates, bargaining council representatives or members determine ratification to be desirable. The council sets procedures, or the Board does so where no council exists. The parent text should therefore not be described as an automatic member-ratification guarantee for every agreement.

Applicable sector rules and merger terms may reserve further rights. CWA-AFA’s merger instrument retains its National Mediation Board bargaining rights and contract and grievance administration. Those provisions must be examined before applying the parent’s general bargaining description to a flight-attendant agreement.

Article XIII.10 also states that a local is not authorized to make contracts or incur liabilities for the parent union. Local participation in bargaining or enforcement is not a general power to bind CWA International financially.

Sources: Constitution, XVII.5, IX.6 and XIII.10, PDF pp.35,15,26; CWA-AFA merger agreement, Appendix A §§4–6, PDF pp.161–162.

Strike votes, national approval and fund support

A local strike requires a majority of voting members by secret ballot and separate national approval under Article XVIII. The Board must determine approval within 10 days after the specified strike vote and imminent-strike notice. Convention may also approve strike action. General and inter-district strikes have their own affected-member and national-supervision provisions.

Ending a strike is another decision: the local, vice president or executive officer, Board and Convention have roles defined by the applicable provisions. Unauthorized strikes can lose assistance and expose a local to charter proceedings. A member authorization vote, a national strike authorization and an award of financial assistance should be identified separately.

The 2025 “CWA Unbreakable” resolution changes strike benefits and fund-allocation rules. The financial analysis explains the benefit schedule, reserve floors and allocation conditions. A fund’s existence or a policy benefit schedule does not establish the amount paid in a particular strike.

Sources: Constitution, XVIII.1–10, PDF pp.36–38; Resolution 80A-25-01.

Contract enforcement and internal review

The relevant contract defines the grievance and arbitration machinery. The Constitution assigns local grievance responsibilities and specified above-local support, while its appended internal appeals rules address certain union decisions. Those internal rights have express exclusions and endpoints, including the special 70/30 local rule and limits on individual appeals over a decision not to arbitrate.

The representation analysis explains those boundaries and the evidence needed to assess service. A committee’s existence, an authorized staff position or a representational spending total does not by itself demonstrate timely handling or a successful outcome in a particular case.

Sources: Constitution, XII.4(d), XIII.9 and Internal Appeals Procedures, preamble and III; PDF pp.19,24–25,54,58–59.

Significance

Advice, oversight, approval and binding decision-making are different powers. Readers should be able to identify who selects negotiators, approves the agenda, signs an agreement, determines ratification, authorizes a strike and handles enforcement. For CWA-AFA, the same inquiry must account for the merger’s reserved rights and the actual financial or professional support provided by the parent.

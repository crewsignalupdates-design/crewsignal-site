Parent-union scope and current leadership

CWA represents workers across multiple industries, including telecommunications, media, manufacturing, public service and aviation. It does not use one uniform base or domicile structure. Geographic districts, industrial sectors and divisions, and chartered locals carry out different functions under the parent Constitution.

The official leadership roster identifies President Claude Cummings Jr. and Secretary-Treasurer Ameenah Salaam, elected in July 2023. The current 20-position Executive Board comprises those two officers, seven district vice presidents, six sector/division vice presidents, the Canadian director and four at-large diversity members. The roster identifies the offices; the Constitution and applicable sector instruments define their powers.

Sources: About CWA; CWA leadership roster; 2023 Convention proceedings; Constitution, IX.1 and XII.

The constitutional allocation of authority

Article VII identifies the Convention as CWA’s highest governing authority, subject to member referendum. The Executive Board acts between conventions under the Constitution and Convention decisions; the President executes established policy. Those are separate functions with separate limits.

The Convention interprets and amends the Constitution. The Board may interpret provisions where Convention has not done so, subject to the constitutional constraints and referendum. National officers and Executive Board members may speak at Convention, but they may not serve as voting delegates or vote there.

Article IX.6 permits nonuniform affiliation and merger terms. A sector’s powers cannot therefore be inferred solely from its position on an organization chart. The authority analysis maps the governing bodies and the powers each exercises.

Sources: Constitution, VII.1, VIII.7(c), VIII.12, IX.4(k) and IX.6; PDF pp.7–15.

The boundary with CWA-AFA

The merger agreement preserved the sector’s internal structure, flight-attendant jurisdiction and specified consent protections against dissolution, merger or impairment of its authority. It retained National Mediation Board bargaining rights and contract and grievance administration. The agreement also addresses the sector’s budget process, premerger net assets and specified office or staffing changes.

These provisions require a more specific account than describing every sector power as a uniform, revocable delegation from national officers. Merger disputes have a stated negotiation and binding-arbitration process. The historical withdrawal option was time-limited; it is not a continuing unrestricted right to leave at any time.

The CWA-AFA analysis examines the sector’s governing bodies and asks what benefit accrued from the merger, including what CWA legal, financial or advisory assistance can be documented and traced.

Sources: CWA-AFA Constitution and Bylaws, Appendix A §§4–6, 9–12 and related memoranda, PDF pp.161–166.

Significance

National authority is substantial and bounded. The governing question is which provision grants a power, who exercises it, and what consent, appeal, referendum or other control applies. Financial and service claims need their own records; institutional structure alone does not establish the value received by a sector or member.

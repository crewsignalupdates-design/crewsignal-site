When a Local Council President cannot attend the Board of Directors, Article V.H first assigns the Vice President, then Secretary, then an authorized elected Board delegate. Article V.I separately governs proxies when a representative cannot remain. At MEC meetings, Article VII.D.6 also permits specified Council Representative or member substitutes, but regular MEC officer elections may be voted only by elected Local Council Officers. These separate provisions make a blanket statement about all votes being cast only by directly elected officers inaccurate.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, V.H–I and VII.D.6–7, physical pp.30–31,42–43.

Member petitions and the 2026 procedural updates

  • Special Local Council meeting: a written petition from 30% of the council’s active members in good standing requires the International Secretary-Treasurer to call the meeting and publish the petitioned agenda. Article III.D.3.
  • Calling the Board into session: a petition from 30% of the active membership requires the Secretary-Treasurer to ballot the active membership within seven days. If a majority of active members votes to convene the Board, the further notice and meeting deadlines in Article V.D.5(b) apply. A petition alone does not convene it.
  • Balloting the Board: 15% of Board members may petition the Secretary-Treasurer to ballot that body on an issue under Article V.C.
  • Petition process: Article III.D.10 requires recording a single intended purpose with the Secretary-Treasurer before circulation and submission within 60 days. It now recognizes electronic signatures through the Membership Hub or another officially authorized system. A petition seeking a local recall meeting is limited to one local officer.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, III.D.3/10 and V.C/D.5(b), physical pp.21–22,28–29.

The 60-day submission period and authorized electronic-signature provisions appear in the operative text with 2026 annotations. This is an adopted-text finding, not an inference from a pending agenda proposal.

Significance

Members have direct local ballots and petition rights, while sector officer selection and many policy decisions operate through representative bodies. Their practical effect depends on participation, access to information and how the procedures are used. Formal rights alone do not establish where member control is most effective.

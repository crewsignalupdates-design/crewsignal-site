Analysis

CWA-AFA operates through Local Councils, airline Master Executive Councils, a sector Board of Directors, a sector Executive Board and International Officers. Its own governing documents and merger protections operate within the parent CWA framework. The sector president also holds the corresponding CWA vice presidency.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, Articles III–VII; Appendix A §§4–6, physical pp.18–43,160–164; CWA Constitution, XII.10 and XV.2(d), physical pp.22,27.

Begin with the parent CWA analysis for the parent constitution and accounts, then use the sector pages below to examine the rights and resources specific to flight attendants.

What benefit did the merger bring AFA?

What benefit did the 2004-era merger bring AFA, and what identifiable legal, financial or advisory services did CWA subsequently provide? This is a continuing question in the analysis, with an answer grounded in the evidence currently available.

The record documents institutional protections, named assistance and parent-funded campaign expenditure. CWA’s 2004 Defense Fund table records $231,000 allocated and $197,285.50 spent on United Airlines Media during July 2003–June 2004. More recent records identify a funded research project serving United bargaining, Delta organizing and regional boarding pay. These are concrete examples; they do not establish the merger’s complete net financial return.

Sources: 2004 CWA proceedings, physical pp.39–40; 2025 Defense Fund Oversight Committee report, physical pp.4,8.

The merger history separates commitments, delivered services and reported expenditure. The financial analysis examines routed dues, reciprocal transactions, sector-paid specialists and unresolved cost allocations. Gross transfers from CWA are not a net-benefit calculation.

Current governing record

  • Adopted amended budget action: March 11, 2026 — $65.36 monthly dues from June 1, 2026 and optional annual rebate provisions; the finance page distinguishes adopted terms from proposals and actual execution.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, Policy Manual X.E.1, physical p.137; adopted action, pp.1–2; United MEC explanation, March 17, 2026.

Significance

Institutional rights, services delivered, financial cost and member outcomes are distinct questions. A complete assessment needs each: the governing rule identifies authority; service records show the work; reconciled accounts identify who paid; outcomes show what members received. These pages state where the reviewed record answers a question and where it remains incomplete.

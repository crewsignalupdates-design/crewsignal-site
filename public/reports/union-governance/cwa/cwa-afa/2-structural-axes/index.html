Analysis: authority is assigned by body

CWA-AFA’s structure combines local membership decisions, airline representation and sector-wide governance. The Board of Directors and the sector Executive Board are different bodies. They also differ from the parent CWA Executive Board. The table identifies the governing responsibility rather than implying one uniform chain of command.

The two sector governing boards

The Board of Directors has final sector governing authority under Article V. Its regular meeting is annual; the constitution differentiates the agenda for even and odd years. The Executive Board maintains continuity and manages affairs between those sessions, but its powers do not include changing the constitution, dues structure or assessments.

Voting rules are also different. Board of Directors roll calls count the active members in good standing represented by each delegate. Executive Board members have one vote each. International Officers’ ex-officio status does not itself supply a vote; the Board of Directors chair has a specified tie-breaking exception. See the body-specific voting analysis.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, V.A–D/G and VI.A–C, physical pp.28–36.

The boundary with parent CWA

The merger agreement preserves the sector’s internal organization and flight-attendant jurisdiction, with specified consent protections concerning dissolution, merger and impairment of authority. It retains bargaining and grievance administration, discretion over specified assets and a sector budget process coordinated with CWA. These terms must be considered alongside parent rules.

The sector president’s CWA vice-presidential office creates a formal parent governance connection. It does not convert the sector into a geographic district or erase the separate rules governing the sector’s own councils and boards.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, Appendix A §§4–6,9–11, physical pp.161–163; CWA Constitution, IX.6, XII.10, XV.2(d), physical pp.15,22,27.

Budget authority and accountability

The International Secretary-Treasurer prepares the sector budget for Board of Directors review and approval before implementation. At the airline level, the constitution calls for an annual report to members that includes expenditures, quarterly expenditure statements to the MEC and access to specified records for members in good standing. Internal budget-and-expenditure reports have a viewing provision and are not designated for public republication.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, IV.E.1(g); VII.B.1(i), VII.C.2(c), physical pp.26,38–40.

The current dues decision is the March 11, 2026 adopted amended action, which sets $65.36 monthly dues beginning June 1 and authorizes optional annual rebate arrangements. The finance analysis distinguishes that decision from the earlier proposal and subsequent execution.

Sources: Adopted action, pp.1–2; United MEC explanation, March 17, 2026.

Significance

An accountability question should identify the decision and the body empowered to make it. Local elections, airline decisions, sector budgets and parent service allocations involve different actors and records. The formal distribution of powers does not establish participation levels, responsiveness or the quality of representation; those require evidence of actual practice.

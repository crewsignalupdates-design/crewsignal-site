Separate sector advocacy from government election enforcement

CWA-AFA’s public statements document organizing-related advocacy at SkyWest. The Labor Department separately reported election litigation against SkyWest InFlight Association (SIA). These records concern different actors and proceedings. A government suit against SIA does not establish a legal expense paid by CWA-AFA or a subsidy supplied by parent CWA.

A documented CWA-AFA action

On September 21, 2023, CWA-AFA reported sending SkyWest a demand for reinstatement of two flight-attendant organizers. The union alleged retaliation for organizing. This is evidence of a sector advocacy action and the union’s stated position; the announcement does not establish the allegation as a court finding or disclose the related legal bill, staff cost or payer allocation.

Sources: CWA-AFA reinstatement-demand announcement, September 21, 2023.

These selected enforcement announcements are dated historical records, not a complete docket chronology. This review has not established the present disposition of the cases, and does not describe either suit as a current pending case, a final victory or a completed remedy. The 2026 announcement does not establish the outcome of the separate 2024 suit.

What these events do and do not show financially

The CWA-AFA announcement identifies an action taken by the sector. The OLMS records identify actions taken by the federal government against SIA. Neither set supplies a complete SkyWest organizing budget, an itemized CWA-AFA legal-services ledger or an allocation of parent CWA assistance. The review therefore leaves the amount and ultimate payer of any sector costs unresolved.

An invoice, payroll allocation or reimbursement entry would need to identify the actual client, matter and reporting period. SIA’s own legal spending, government enforcement work, CWA-AFA organizing expenses and any parent-funded service must be reconciled separately. A common airline connection is insufficient to combine those costs.

The outstanding financial questions

  • What SkyWest-specific organizing, research and legal work did CWA-AFA authorize from FY2023 onward, and under which project or matter codes?
  • Which lawyers or staff performed the work, for which client, and which entity paid their fees, salary, benefits and expenses?
  • Did parent CWA supply grants, fund-project support, staff services or reimbursements? If so, what were the authorized and actual amounts?
  • Can the sector reconcile its direct expenditures with parent support, reimbursements and any costs paid by another entity?
  • For any claim about the result of litigation, what final order or settlement supports that claim and what evidence connects it to the expenditure being evaluated?

These questions seek a cost and service trail. The absence of a complete allocation in the reviewed records is not evidence that no resources were used.

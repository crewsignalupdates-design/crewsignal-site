Analysis: airline agreements and coordinated support

CWA-AFA’s official contract directory organizes bargaining by airline. Its professional negotiators assist elected flight-attendant negotiating committees with strategy and contract language. The applicable airline agreement supplies the substantive rights and procedures; the sector’s governing rules assign responsibilities for administering that work.

Sources: Official contracts directory and bargaining description; Constitution and Bylaws, III.C.3 and VII.B–C, physical pp.18,38–40.

The existing analysis How CWA-AFA Negotiating Committees and Staff Negotiators Work examines the relationship between elected committees, professional staff and financial reporting.

Committees as operational tools

United’s published grievance committee duties include coordination of MEC grievances, work with staff attorneys and local grievance representatives, and distribution of settlement and System Board decisions. Its reserve committee assists local reserve committees and works with the grievance committee on system-wide reserve violations. These examples preserve the distinction between local casework and airline-wide coordination.

Sources: United MEC Grievance Committee; United MEC Reserve Committee.

Sector governance also uses committees: the Board of Directors has agenda and budget committees, and the Executive Board has a standing Organizing Committee. Their mandates and membership rules come from the constitution. A committee’s stated duty is not itself evidence of a particular service outcome.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, V.J–L and VI.D, physical pp.31–33,37.

Compensation and the cost of representation

Article VII.C.6 states that MEC office carries no salary and that MEC operating expenses are paid by the union. That does not mean the role has no financial support. Flight-pay-loss, reimbursed expenses and airline-paid release arrangements are separate questions requiring the applicable policy, agreement and financial record.

The existence of central support also does not identify a net subsidy from parent CWA. The release-time discussion and service-cost analysis distinguish who records and ultimately bears a cost.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, VII.C.6 and Policy Manual IV, physical p.41 and finance policies.

Current airline-merger governance rules

The current Policy Manual contains specific rules for merging airline MECs. These govern airline integration, a separate subject from AFA’s historical merger with CWA.

Airline-merger process and continuing protections
StageOperative requirementWhat the wording means
Within 30 days of financial closeThe International President convenes affected MECs jointly to outline the merger process.The agenda includes officer elections, separate agreements, committees, councils, budgets, offices and assets.
Within 12 months of financial closeThe President or designee must ensure the process is scheduled.This is a scheduling requirement; the provision does not require completed consolidation within 12 months.
Merged MEC with separate CBAsDecisions affecting a particular CBA rest with members of the respective previous MEC.MEC consolidation does not erase contract-specific decision rights.
Duplicate Local CouncilsCouncils continue until a merged working agreement is signed; their officers or successors continue until combined-council elections are completed.Signing and election completion are distinct milestones.
Within 90 days after signingThe Secretary-Treasurer initiates Local Council Officer elections at bases with duplicate councils.The rule says initiate, not complete within 90 days.
Committee continuityThe merged MEC maintains separate grievance committees and System Board members until a merged agreement; its negotiating committee includes at least one member from each affected airline until then.The policy preserves specified contract-specific representation during integration.

Sources: Constitution and Bylaws, Policy Manual X.E.1–2, physical pp.137–138.

Significance

Local officers, MECs, committees and staff carry different parts of contract administration. The useful questions are who had responsibility, what work was performed, what result followed and who paid. The constitutional structure identifies authority; contracts, case records and reconciled accounts are needed to evaluate performance and financial value.

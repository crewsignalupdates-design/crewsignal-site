Who elects each office?

CWA combines direct local voting with delegated national elections. Members elect local officers and Convention delegates by secret ballot. Convention delegates elect the President and Secretary-Treasurer; district delegates elect district vice presidents. A district-wide direct membership ballot is not the parent Constitution’s rule for selecting its district vice president.

Only members in good standing may vote or hold elective office under the general rule, and officeholders must meet constitutional and applicable legal requirements. Principal local offices require a majority; local bylaws may permit plurality election for other offices and delegates. Convention delegates and voting strength

Ordinary Convention voting gives each local delegate one vote. A request by 20% of delegates triggers a per-capita roll call. A local divides its weighted votes equally among its delegates, with any remainder assigned to the delegation chair. These two voting methods should be distinguished from the number of delegates a local may send.

The Constitution controls the allocation formula and credentials basis; the manual supplies the worked bands above. 600 is not an automatic fourth-delegate boundary. The manual bases voting credits on paid member-months and excludes agency fee payers and associate members from those credits. The Constitution separately protects voting strength affected by authorized strikes.

National officers and Executive Board members may speak at Convention but cannot be voting delegates. Good-standing members may observe general Convention meetings. The permanent rules also provide opportunities for debate and appeal of a chair or parliamentarian ruling.

Sources: Constitution, VIII.4–5, VIII.11–12 and Permanent Rules VII and IX; PDF pp.9–12,50–53; Operating Procedures Manual, Part IX.11 and voting-strength chart, PDF pp.147–148.

Local election administration and challenges

The local election committee conducts elections and referenda, tallies ballots and issues tentative certification. A written challenge is due within 10 days of tentative certification. The committee must make its final determination within 20 days of that certification; local governing-body and membership appeals remain available.

If an appellant has attempted to exhaust those local remedies without a final decision within 60 days and wishes to continue, the written appeal to the appropriate geographic vice president must be filed within 70 days of tentative certification. Certified officers serve while the challenge is pending unless the determination is reversed or a properly ordered rerun produces a different result.

These are the Constitution’s internal local-election rules. They are distinct from disciplinary appeals, general complaints, sector-specific procedures and any external statutory filing requirements.

Sources: Constitution, XV.4(a)–(c), PDF pp.28–29.

Discipline and other internal appeals

Disciplinary charges follow their own written, signed and sworn factual requirements and timing rules. The Constitution provides an unbiased trial body, union-member counsel, witness and evidence rights, cross-examination, an accurate record and a written decision. Local and national convictions have specified appeal routes, distinct from election challenges.

The 2025 amendments added national mutual-respect grounds and permit a mutually agreed video trial under the stated travel or overnight-accommodation conditions. General complaints alleging constitutional or bylaw violations use the appended Internal Appeals Procedures. One route should not be substituted for another merely because each involves a dissatisfied member.

Sources: Constitution, XIX–XX and Internal Appeals Procedures; PDF pp.38–42,54–59; 2025 proceedings, pp.198–208.

Significance

Members influence parent leadership through their choice of delegates and through separate constitutional controls. The direct-versus-delegate distinction changes the actual route to influence. A sound account identifies the electorate, voting method, term and review procedure for the particular office or decision, while keeping sector exceptions visible.

Authority depends on the decision

CWA’s geographic, industrial and local structures intersect, but they are not interchangeable steps in one uniform chain. The Constitution assigns specific powers and preserves member controls. Applicable merger instruments can also reserve sector rights.

Executive Board composition and operation

The current official roster has 20 positions: President and Secretary-Treasurer; seven district vice presidents; six sector/division vice presidents; the Canadian director; and four at-large diversity members. Those offices have different electoral routes. The elections analysis identifies the relevant electorates and terms.

The Board meets at least twice yearly. One-third of its members can trigger a meeting called by the Secretary-Treasurer if the President refuses. A majority constitutes a quorum, and the President votes only to break a tie. Vacancies follow separate rules; for example, a vice-presidential vacancy at least one year before the next regular Convention requires a special election within 60 days by the eligible local presidents using their prior Convention voting strength.

Sources: CWA leadership roster; Constitution, IX.1–3 and XXIII.2; PDF pp.12–13,46.

Geographic districts, sectors and Canada

Districts provide an administrative structure and must hold the prescribed district and state or area meetings. Their programs must be consistent with national policy. Their inability to levy independent dues or assessments is a concrete financial limit; a district title does not establish an independently funded treasury.

Sector and division provisions organize particular industries and occupations. Some sector presidents also serve as CWA vice presidents under their own governing rules. Article IX.6 allows different affiliation terms, so a general district rule cannot automatically be applied to every merger sector.

CWA-SCA Canada has specified autonomy in Canadian governance, policy and operations, remains bound by the CWA Constitution, and has self-financing and shared-cost provisions. Its director is a voting member of the parent Executive Board. These arrangements are another reason to identify the applicable instrument rather than assume identical authority for every organizational unit.

Sources: Constitution, VI.6, IX.6, XI.1–8 and XII.12; PDF pp.7,15–17,21.

Local authority and parent intervention

Locals adopt consistent bylaws, carry out assigned representation, conduct elections and maintain financial records. They must obtain an annual audit by a competent person, provide financial statements to members at least annually, furnish an annual statement to the national Secretary-Treasurer and report a Department of Labor audit. Local property is held for the members while the local remains chartered.

The Board may inspect local financial records and order an independent audit, at union expense, when necessary and proper to protect the local membership and union. That audit power is distinct from removing officers, revoking a charter or placing a local in temporary administration.

Charter revocation requires specified grounds, notice, preparation time for a defense and a two-thirds Board vote. An appeal is due within 30 days and goes to the next Convention or Presidents’ Meeting, whichever occurs first; a two-thirds vote is required to sustain the Board’s finding. Temporary administration has specified constitutional grounds and procedures. The Constitution does not grant an unlimited discretionary takeover power.

Sources: Constitution, IX.4(e), XIII.5–12 and XXIII.6; PDF pp.14,22–26,46.

These are the Constitution’s internal procedures. They do not determine whether a particular use of those powers complied with all applicable law or with a sector’s reserved merger rights.

Financial authority and oversight

The Executive Board must cause an annual independent audit of national accounts and publish the results to the membership. The Secretary-Treasurer keeps the union’s assets and records, supplies quarterly statements to the Board, uses Board-approved depositories, pays supported bills with the required countersignature and invests under Convention directions or Board directions when Convention has not supplied them.

Locals establish dues subject to the governing framework; Convention sets the parent per-capita obligation and may prescribe minimum dues. Special assessments have separate approval and use restrictions. The financial analysis distinguishes these constitutional powers from the actual filing categories, current collection process and sector-specific funding arrangements.

Sources: Constitution, VI.1–4, IX.4(d),(f) and XII.2; PDF pp.6–7,13–14,18.

Significance

Different units can exercise different powers without every decision being ambiguous. The useful inquiry follows the authority, the limiting rule, the record of its exercise and the available review. Financial reporting needs the same precision: an administrative unit, a sector, an internal council and a federal filer should not be assumed to have identical boundaries.

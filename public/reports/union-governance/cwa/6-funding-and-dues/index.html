Financial position and reporting scope

The parent union’s original IRS returns report expenses exceeding revenue in each of the fiscal years ending May 31, 2023, 2024 and 2025—a combined $107,427,758. Its substantial investment holdings coexist with benefit obligations, internal fund rules and operating budgets that use reserve funding. These measures describe different parts of the financial position and require separate analysis.

Sources: FY2023 original IRS XML; FY2024 original IRS XML; FY2025 original IRS XML, Parts I, X and XI; 2026 Finance Committee report, PDF page 7.

CWA is the parent filer under OLMS file 000-188 and EIN 53-0246709. Its fiscal year runs June 1–May 31. CWA-AFA reports separately under OLMS file 000-376. Internal districts, sectors, councils and federal reporting entities are not interchangeable. Parent, sector and local financial totals cannot be combined without eliminating inter-entity transactions.

The current page preserves all 150 previously published annual LM-2 detail rows, while qualifying their source status. It adds the independently inspected IRS returns, budget and fund evidence, compensation periods, 162 grant disclosures and unresolved reconciliation questions. The LM-2 source and control page provides the full verified FY2024 Statements A–B and source-completeness notes.

Sources: FY2024 parent LM-2, Items 1–3; indexed FY2026 sector report 945683, file 000-376; FY2023 original IRS XML; FY2024 original IRS XML; FY2025 original IRS XML.

Dues authority, collection and settlement

The Constitution assigns local membership-dues decisions and the Convention’s per-capita rules distinct roles. Districts are administrative units and cannot levy dues. Affiliation instruments can preserve different sector arrangements. The Constitution and operating rules must therefore be read with the applicable merger terms rather than assuming a uniform allocation for every member.

Sources: CWA Constitution, Article VI.1–4 on dues, Article XI.7 on districts, Article XIII on locals, and Article IX.6 on nonuniform affiliations.

Constitutional terminology does not change a filing’s classifications. In the verified FY2024 LM-2, Item 36, Dues and Agency Fees, is $124,224,565, while Item 37, Per Capita Tax receipts, is $0. A distinct disbursement line, Item 56, reports $5,322,848 of per-capita tax. A source described in dues guidance as per-capita remittance should not silently be relabeled as Item 37 revenue.

Sources: FY2024 partial LM-2, Statement B, PDF page 4.

The August 2025 collection policy introduced separate invoicing when dues were not collected. Following the 2026 local petition and Special Convention, CWA’s July 16 guidance reports rescission and says that, from July 6, 2026, per-capita owed for active members and agency-fee-payers again would be deducted from the local’s overall dues payment. March–June invoices remain due, and the notice sets an 18-month correction process for member status. This change concerns collection and remittance mechanics; it does not itself establish a new dues rate.

The July notice’s opening convention date conflicts with its later July reference and the official July 1 Special Convention call. The review preserves that conflict and does not invent an exact vote tally or resolve the date by assumption.

Sources: March 13, 2026 S-T guidance, historical invoicing policy; July 16, 2026 S-T guidance, rescission and implementation; governing-document and change ledger.

Fund rules, strike support and the internal MRF loan

Convention Resolution 80A-25-01, adopted by voice vote on August 11, 2025 and effective August 17, changes Members’ Relief Fund (MRF), Strategic Industry Fund and Growth Fund funding rules. It provides a $14 million investment-income funding stream subject to reserve floors. AFL-CIO and Canadian Labour Congress affiliation charges are paid first, with the remainder split equally between the Strategic Industry Fund and Growth Fund.

The $14 million is not a guaranteed net distribution to the two operating funds, an assumed investment return, or an immediate $75 million transfer. Actual fund balances and distributions require the fund rollforwards.

Sources: adopted Resolution 80A-25-01, CWA Unbreakable; 2025 proceedings, PDF page 267, adoption.

The 2025 Finance Committee report describes a $40.4 million internal loan refinanced through the MRF in June 2015, with debt originating in 2006, a reported balance of $39,683,674.05, and maturity on December 31, 2026. Although the agreement was interest-only, principal payments had begun. The 2026 report states that cumulative principal payments since May 2024 total $1,014,736.42. No later payoff or refinancing outcome was verified.

The budget’s $1.2 million “Internal Loan Repayment” line is defined in the report as interest; it must not be used as a principal-reduction figure. An internal fund loan must also not be added to consolidated external liabilities without eliminating the internal relationship.

Sources: 2025 Finance Committee report, PDF page 3; 2026 report, PDF page 4; budget page 7 and definition page 12.

CWA-AFA merger benefits and the service-financing question

What benefits did the 2004-era CWA merger bring to AFA, and what financial trail identifies legal, financial or advisory services that CWA actually provided to CWA-AFA? This question guides the continuing CWA-AFA analysis. It requires both the merger instrument and evidence of later performance.

The parent evidence identifies relevant starting points: separate budget and consolidated-statement boundaries; affiliate payments; the “AFA MEC ADMIN” classification; restricted-fund rules; and grants to separately identified charities. None is automatically the value of services delivered to the sector. A valid trail must identify the payer, payee, fiscal period, service or project, reimbursement terms, and any matching entry in the recipient’s records.

Evidence needed to evaluate merger support
QuestionRecords that can answer it
What was promised?Executed merger agreement, amendments, contemporaneous member materials and approval records.
What was delivered?Named matters, staff assignments, case work, research products, bargaining support and contemporaneous reports.
Who bore the cost?CWA and sector ledgers, inter-entity settlements, invoices, staff time allocations, grant approvals and fund disbursements.
What benefit reached members?Documented service outputs, negotiated or adjudicated outcomes, timing and comparable costs; distinguish union assertions from independently supported outcomes.

A parent payment may be a return of collected dues, reimbursement, a restricted grant or additional support. Sector-paid outside counsel is not established as a CWA-provided service merely because the sector belongs to CWA. Missing invoices do not establish that no support occurred; organizational capacity does not establish that a particular service was delivered.

Sources: 2026 Finance Committee report, budget-scope footnote; FY2023 original IRS XML; FY2024 original IRS XML; FY2025 original IRS XML, Part IX and Schedule I; current CWA-AFA Constitution and Bylaws, Appendix A merger agreement.

Significance and outstanding records

The verified record supports a substantive financial analysis: three years of accrual expense excesses, a declining net-asset position, significant investment holdings and benefit liabilities, reserve-funded budgets, distinct member-related cash movements, and grant disclosures that need reconciliation. It does not supply a single percentage of dues returned as service or a reliable measure of representation quality.

  • Obtain audited financial statements and notes, current fund rollforwards and investment valuation records; reconcile them to Form 990 and LM-2.

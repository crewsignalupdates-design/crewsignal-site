International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers

Contract Administration, Enforcement & Committees

How National Airlines and CommuteAir contract rights move through local representatives, District 142 and formal dispute procedures—and which records can demonstrate effective service.

Reviewed November 11, 2026

Contract Sources and Date Qualifications

The two agreements establish different enforcement procedures and expense obligations. Ratification, signature, implementation and amendable dates also need to be distinguished in the posted source record.

  • National: the CBA cover says September 20, 2024; the district landing page labels a September 2029 endpoint. Signed Article 23 states November 6, 2023 and a five-year term from signing. The published dates remain unreconciled.
  • CommuteAir: the landing page labels July 1, 2024–July 1, 2028. Signed §23 retains ratification-date placeholders and a four-year term from ratification. The district announced ratification on June 6, 2024 and described implementation as a further step.

An executed amendment or implementation record could reconcile these dates. Until then, CrewSignal does not present a calculated endpoint as a verified amendable date. The discrepancies do not establish that either agreement is invalid. PDF page references below count the cover; National's printed page numbers differ.

National: Local Resolution and District Escalation

  • Base and steward access: base representatives can settle local grievances; stewards or employees first attempt informal resolution. Article 16.A–C, PDF pp. 44–46.
  • General Chair: the agreement authorizes the General Chair or designee to investigate disputes and advance unresolved cases through the applicable contract or disciplinary process. Article 16.A, C–D, PDF pp. 44–47.
  • Grievance Review Board: two district and two company representatives review grievances, at least three times annually unless the parties agree otherwise. Article 16.B, PDF pp. 44–45.
  • System Board: union, company and neutral members hear disputes after the contractual grievance process. Article 16.F–H, PDF pp. 50–54.
  • Joint committees: the agreement recognizes Hotel, Scheduling, Safety and ASAP representatives. Article 21.F, PDF p. 65.

The district's operational role is written into the contract. Its effectiveness depends on representatives being accessible, cases reaching the appropriate stage, and decisions being explained and implemented. Board schedules, case records and completed remedies would show how the formal structure works in practice.

CommuteAir: A Separate Grievance Process

  • Step 1: following informal discussion, the Domicile Chairperson may file a written grievance if considered justified. Section 17.B–C, p. 69.
  • Step 2: the District General Chairperson may appeal to the Vice President Flight Operations or designee. Section 17.C.2, p. 70.
  • Step 3: the President-Directing General Chairperson or designee may appeal to the System Board. The agreement also provides review/resolution meetings and grievance mediation. Section 17.C.3–E, p. 70.
  • System Board: company, union and neutral members decide properly submitted disputes. Section 18, pp. 72–74.

National's Grievance Review Board should not be substituted for this procedure. Each agreement has its own time limits, submission rules and exceptions. For service evaluation, the important records show who exercised discretion at each stage, why a case advanced or stopped, and how the member was informed.

District Reporting and Member Communication

Under District 142's 2025 bylaws, Article 8, §§6–7 (p. 6), withdrawn grievances require specified close-out letters, including certified mail to the grievant, and quarterly agreement-level grievance reports go to local grievance committee leadership. Article 8 also addresses written grievance routing and action without unnecessary delay.

These are concrete communication duties. Completeness, timely delivery and meaningful explanations can be evaluated against the required records. They are internal distribution obligations; a missing public copy does not establish that the report or notice was never delivered.

The annual budget, audit-comparison and General Chair activity-report requirements in Article 10 (p. 17) provide additional ways to connect service work with financial accountability. The current record does not establish whether all these duties were fulfilled for either carrier throughout the review period.

The Cost of Performing the Work

  • National: the union pays actual time lost for union-business release; board/witness costs and jointly incurred necessary expenses are allocated separately. Article 21.E, PDF pp. 64–65; Article 16.H, PDF p. 53.
  • CommuteAir: union-business flight-pay-loss invoices include a 28% override for benefits/accruals and administration, payable within 45 days. Counsel/witness/board-member costs and shared neutral/hearing costs have separate rules. Section 13.G.2(d), p. 60; Section 18.F.3, p. 74.

These clauses identify the records that make services financially traceable: approved release or engagement, employer or vendor invoice, actual payment, reimbursement and work performed. They establish obligations, not actual amounts paid. The financial analysis distinguishes district, International and local spending and the unresolved allocation of shared costs.

Significance: Enforcement as a Member Service

Contractual machinery gives members a path for enforcing their rights. Its value depends on the quality of access, investigation, decision-making and follow-through. A meaningful comparison would show:

  • Response times and case ages, grouped by carrier, case type and review period.
  • Cases resolved, withdrawn, escalated and still open, with understandable reasons and member communication.
  • Pay or other remedies actually implemented, rather than amounts requested or announced alone.
  • The staff time, specialist support, release and expenses attributable to the work, including reimbursements between institutions.

The reviewed records establish procedures and selected activity. They do not yet support a comprehensive performance ranking. Committee names and budget totals become useful measures when the record also shows what work occurred, how long it took, and what members received.

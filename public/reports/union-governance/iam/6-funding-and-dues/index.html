Financial Transparency & LM Reporting

What the International, District 142 and local accounts show about resources, transfers and the cost of representation for National Airlines and CommuteAir.

Reviewed November 11, 2026FY2023 onward; coverage limits stated

Financial Disclosures

IAM finances span the International, District 142 and local lodges. These are distinct reporting bodies, with transfers between them and different workgroups sharing resources. CrewSignal examines what each level collects, retains and spends, and what evidence connects those resources to representation for National Airlines and CommuteAir flight attendants.

A local filing is a lodge-wide account, which may cover several carriers. A district payment or International subsidy does not by itself establish the service received by a particular airline. The analysis below keeps the entity, fiscal period, reporting form and source completeness visible.

How Resources Reach Representation

  • International: central administration, specialist resources, funds and support payments to subordinate bodies.
  • District 142: carrier bargaining and enforcement administration, General Chairs, collection and remittance of dues, and district-wide expenses.
  • Locals and carrier representatives: member access, local participation, case preparation and services whose cost may appear in more than one treasury.

The 2025 IAM Constitution sets the per-capita and district/local framework (Articles VII and XXII). The District 142 bylaws specify district collections, local remittances and reporting (Article 10, pp. 15–17). Those rules identify responsibilities; the actual remittance and service records establish what occurred.

Significance

Money does not necessarily travel in a single downward chain. An employer may deduct dues or initially advance paid release time; a district may remit funds upward or distribute a local share; the International may pay a subsidy or absorb staff costs centrally. A combined comparison must eliminate transfers between union bodies and identify the final cost and beneficiary workgroup.

Lobstermen Guarantee and Loan Exposure

FY2023 Item 69, PDF p. 269 discloses a $5 million Bank of Labor credit line to Lobstermen Co-op guaranteed through a Truist standby letter of credit; the line was fully drawn at year-end. FY2024 Schedule 2, PDF p. 6 records $5,250,200 of new advances to Lobsterman 207, raising the loan receivable from $1,054,250 to $6,304,450, with no repayments. The listed purpose is financial assistance and the repayment term is on demand.

The relationship between the guarantee and later advances, the approval record and the basis for collectibility remain unresolved. A loan receivable is an asset, not a grant or demonstrated flight-attendant service. The filings alone do not establish that the advance was a payment under the guarantee.

Why District 142 Matters for Both Carriers

NMB records establish IAM certification for CommuteAir flight attendants in 2008 and National Airlines flight attendants in 2022. District 142's CommuteAir and National Airlines pages identify the carrier agreements and district assignments. District-level financial and service records therefore matter to both workgroups.

The local mapping differs by carrier. Current district/local sources identify CommuteAir with Local 2339N; the Local 2508 directory identifies a National/MCO assignment alongside other carrier assignments. These links establish representation context, not a carrier-specific allocation of every district or local dollar.

National Airlines Dues Checkoff

National Article 21.H, PDF pp. 65–67 sets out union-security and checkoff provisions, including employee authorization for deductions and remittance to the district Secretary-Treasurer. It also calls for a monthly employee list showing location, status and dues deducted. These are specific records with which to reconcile amounts collected and transmitted.

The agreement does not state a standalone National monthly dues amount on its face. The deduction authorization and any certified rate changes matter. A district rate, an International per-capita rate and a member's complete monthly obligation are different quantities.

The posted agreement has inconsistent cover and signed-duration dates. That qualification is explained in Contract Administration, Enforcement & Committees; no unqualified amendable date is inferred here.

Significance

The reported balances remain unreconciled. The affiliate-clearing ledger, valuation policies, financial statements and any corrected filing would establish which amounts represent actual year-end obligations. The discrepancy alone does not establish insolvency, loss, diversion or misconduct.

Form 990 Part XII, PDF p. 12 indicates no independent accountant compilation/review or independent audit for 2024. Internal or parent-body reviews are distinct. Neither an arithmetic check nor a positive tax-return balance substitutes for the missing reconciliation.

District funds and carrier stations: the reviewed filing is a district-wide report with affiliate collections and representational spending. It does not provide separate station treasuries or a complete National/CommuteAir allocation. The liability disagreement must be resolved before the apparent financial structure is used to assess financial condition.

These categories do not support a common service-efficiency percentage. Costs must be considered with actual staffing, case volumes, response times, work delivered and remedies implemented.

Earlier Oversight and Financial Context

The January 26, 2022 trusteeship notice alleged administrative and financial deficiencies, including an unreported account of approximately $270,000. These are the union's allegations. The district announced that trusteeship ended January 3, 2023.

That history makes earlier corrected reports and a closeout reconciliation relevant to the 2023 opening balances. The FY2023 tax returns contain limited prior-year comparisons, but a standalone FY2022 filing review is not complete. The 2022 episode, the FY2024 reporting disagreement and the September 2026 supervision announcement remain distinct; the reviewed evidence does not establish a causal connection among them.

Key Findings

  • The reviewed International FY2023–24 filings establish a substantial central treasury and support payments, but do not allocate all costs or outcomes to flight attendants.
  • District 142's FY2024 LM-2 and Form 990 require a reconciliation of liabilities, net assets and opening valuations before financial condition can be assessed from those balances.
  • Parent and district payment blocks have differing totals and classifications. Actual payment records are needed to explain the difference and prevent double counting.
  • CommuteAir's Local 2339N figures remain historical and unreverified here; National/MCO has a current connection to multi-carrier Local 2508. Neither local report represents the complete cost of that carrier's representation.
  • Professional fees, subsidies and release-time obligations are useful financial inputs. Service quality also requires evidence of access, work performed, timeliness and outcomes.

Other Airline-Sector Activity

Delta historical organizing context: Reuters reporting dated November 30, 2023 described a coalition in which IAM supported ramp, cargo and tower workers, CWA-AFA organized flight attendants, and the Teamsters organized technicians. This historical account establishes the division of organizing roles; the reviewed material does not quantify the participating unions' cost shares.

JSX election underway: the NMB report for November 2–6, 2026 lists JSX Air, IAM and approximately 127 flight attendants in case R-7692, with a December 1, 2026 count date. As of this review, that is organizing activity rather than a completed certification or a represented workgroup with an IAM contract. Organizing costs and established-member services should remain distinguishable.

What the Financial Record Can Establish

The public filings supply entity-wide resources, reported transfers and categories of spending. They do not yet supply a complete carrier-by-carrier dues-remittance and service-cost account for National or CommuteAir. Missing records remain unknown, rather than zero.

The International's FY2024 membership schedule distinguishes 547,882 total members from 290,693 dues-paying members; District 142 likewise reports several membership categories. Meaningful per-member comparisons require actual payer-months and the same workgroup and period for costs and outcomes. International Schedule 13, PDF p. 49; District 142 Schedule 13.

Questions the Available Record Does Not Yet Answer

  • Which original or amended filings and attachments complete the FY2023–25 series?
  • What year-end obligation explains District 142's affiliate liability, and how does it reconcile with Form 990?
  • How do the parent/district payments reconcile, and what staff work or specialist assistance did they support for each carrier?
  • What dues and other resources were retained at each level after remittances and reimbursements?
  • What do the service records show about response time, case aging, work delivered and remedies actually implemented?

Until those records are sufficiently complete and comparable, expenditure ratios, institutional size and the number of managerial layers do not establish a return on dues or a service-quality ranking.

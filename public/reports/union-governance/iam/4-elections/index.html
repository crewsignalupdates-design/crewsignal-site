International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers

Elections, Terms & Member Control

How members select leaders, gain access to nominations and ballots, and challenge decisions at the International, district and local levels.

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific time). Dates and unresolved questions reflect the public record reviewed through that date.

Member Control at Each Level

International officers, district officers, local officers and convention delegates are selected through distinct processes. The constitutional framework supplies common rules, while approved bylaws and election notices determine the applicable procedures for each body. Member control therefore includes access to nominations, the opportunity for a contest, balloting, records and internal review.

Election rules describe how accountability is intended to operate. Turnout, candidate access, contested races and the handling of protests are separate evidence of how it operates in practice.

International Officers: The 2025 Cycle

The Constitution provides for International officers to be nominated and elected by the membership, with defined U.S./Canadian constituencies. The 2025 U.S. notice described local nominations, endorsement requirements and a membership mail ballot when a qualifying contest emerged. The term runs from July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2029. (Article III §1, printed p. 13; November 25, 2024 election notice)

IAM’s January 28, 2025 report stated that no competing candidates received enough nominations to require a full election. That establishes the announced outcome of the nomination process; it is not evidence of a contested membership-ballot victory. (January 28 outcome announcement)

The distinction matters because nomination and endorsement requirements influence whether members reach a competitive ballot. The reviewed announcement does not, by itself, measure the accessibility of candidacy or the depth of member participation.

District 142: The 2026 Election

Article XXII §7 of the Constitution permits district elections by a membership secret ballot or by qualifying elected delegates, subject to the district’s approved rules. District 142’s Article 9 provides its own nomination, endorsement, referendum, vacancy and appointment procedures. Its four-year referendum/plurality rule for covered full-time officers expressly excludes General Chairs; the rule should not be applied indiscriminately to every representative on a roster. (Constitution, printed pp. 82–84; District bylaws, Article 9, pp. 6–13)

The current district timetable lists nominations on September 4, endorsements on October 16 when needed, and the general election on December 11, 2026. The vote is upcoming as of this review; a sample ballot is not a result. The general rules list installation for January 12, 2027. (2026 district election page; General rules)

The October 30 dispensation concerns specified ties in the endorsement process where the tied candidates already had the required four other local endorsements. It does not waive ballot qualification generally. (Dispensation, p. 1)

The district’s published materials contain differing November 26/27 wording for absentee requests. CrewSignal does not resolve that discrepancy into a definitive deadline. The official election materials and a confirmed instruction from the responsible election office are necessary for an individual voting decision. (District 142 general rules, absentee-ballot instructions)

Local Elections, Terms and Balloting

Local officers ordinarily serve three-year terms. Nominations take place in November, or September at the local’s option, followed by secret-ballot elections at the first meeting in December, or October under that option. Newly organized locals and vacancies have separate provisions. (Article B §§2–4, printed pp. 100–104)

For U.S. locals, Article B §4 requires mailed notice of nominations and the election, with an absentee-ballot application, at least 60 days before the election. The mailing requirement expressly excludes Canadian locals. Absentee procedures, approval of alternative voting arrangements, candidate observers and preservation of election records are also addressed in the article. Write-in votes for candidates not nominated under the procedure are not tabulated. (Article B §4, printed pp. 103–104)

These constitutional safeguards remain relevant to airline locals with dispersed members and irregular work schedules. The applicable approved local bylaws and actual notice are needed to establish the election arrangements for a particular local.

Bylaws and Eligibility

Subject to International approval, local bylaws may impose an attendance qualification of up to 50% of regular meetings during the specified 12-month period. The same provision excuses listed circumstances, including verified illness, vacation, approved IAM business, employer regular or travel assignments, and reserve military leave. It should not be read as an unconditional attendance barrier. (Article B §3, printed p. 102)

The Constitution also protects an otherwise eligible member from disqualification solely because an employer delays or defaults in transmitting authorized withheld dues, provided the member is not otherwise delinquent. Other membership, employment and local-rule qualifications remain applicable. (Article B §3, printed pp. 101–102)

IAM’s Bylaws & Internal Disputes department assists with the development and interpretation of subordinate bylaws. The reviewed public record does not provide a fully reconciled set of current local election documents and officeholder records for both Local 2339N and Local 2508.

Convention Delegates and Absentee Access

Convention delegates have their own membership-based nomination and secret-ballot process under Article II §§2–3. Delegate representation and voting strength use separate membership formulas. Convention participation should therefore be assessed separately from International officer elections. (Article II §§2–3, printed pp. 4–7)

The 2025 text adds conditional email requests for absentee ballots and a 21-day Canadian request interval. The dedicated address must be included in the notice and the arrangement must be consistent with local bylaws. This is a request method, not authorization to cast a vote by email. The provision is also cross-referenced in local and district election procedures. (Article II §3, printed pp. 6–7; 2023–2025 comparison)

Internal Remedies and Oversight During Supervision

Constitutional interpretation and internal disputes provide another route for member control. Article VI §1 makes the President’s constitutional decisions subject to the Article L appeal process; the headquarters department describes its role in interpretation and internal-dispute records. The availability of a procedure is distinct from evidence that it supplied a timely or effective remedy in a particular case. (Article VI §1, printed p. 30; Official department description)

The September 2026 supervision announcement said the District 142 election would continue. The later election materials support a continuing scheduled process, while the reviewed record does not establish the final investigation outcome or complete supervisory mandate. The separate 2022–2023 trusteeship should not be treated as the same event. (September 3 statement; earlier oversight chronology)

Significance

Members’ ability to influence decisions depends on more than the interval between elections. Relevant evidence includes whether members can qualify for nomination, obtain absentee access, understand who controls their services, inspect the applicable records and obtain reasoned responses to a protest.

Formal safeguards, participation and outcomes are separate parts of the assessment. A membership vote, an uncontested nomination outcome, a delegate decision and an appointment confer authority through different processes; each needs its own account of member access and accountability.

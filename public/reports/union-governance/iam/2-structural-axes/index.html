International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers

Governance Structure & Decision Authority

Who controls decisions and resources, how authority reaches carrier representation, and how members can hold each level accountable.

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific time). Dates and unresolved questions reflect the public record reviewed through that date.

Authority Framework

The Constitution defines powers; officers and governing bodies exercise them. IAM’s International, Air Transport Territory, District 142, locals and carrier representatives have different responsibilities. Local and district bylaws supplement the Constitution and require International approval. (Article I §§3–4, printed pp. 2–3)

The relevant questions are who can authorize assistance, who controls the money, who may review or override a decision, and how the member can obtain an explanation or challenge the result. A directory or organization chart establishes a formal position; it does not establish response time or service quality.

International Governing Bodies and Officers

  • Executive Council: between conventions, the Council holds the International’s executive and judicial powers. It may require officer reports and has control over International funds, deposits, investments and property. These powers place central oversight and resource decisions above the local service level. (Article V §§1–3, printed pp. 27–28)
  • International President: the President enforces governing rules, interprets constitutional questions subject to the Article L appeal process, and has supervisory powers over subordinate bodies. Local and district bylaws require the President’s approval. (Article VI §1, printed p. 30; Article VI §§7–9, printed pp. 32–35)
  • Bylaws and internal disputes: the headquarters department assists with bylaws and constitutional interpretation and maintains internal-dispute case records. Its role helps explain the route from a local disagreement to International review. (Official department description)

Formal oversight can provide a remedy when local administration fails. Its effectiveness depends on accessible procedures, reasons for decisions and follow-through; the existence of a supervisory power alone does not establish those results.

Air Transport Territory

The Air Transport Territory began on May 1, 2022 and includes Districts 141 and 142. It is a sector-level coordination and International support layer, distinct from the district and local bodies through which members receive day-to-day representation. (Territory establishment announcement; Air Transport Territory)

Its contribution is best evaluated through actual assigned work—bargaining participation, training, research or other specialist assistance—and the staff time or funds supporting that work. Representation & Member Services distinguishes documented assistance from available capacity.

District 142: Service Administration and Financial Control

The Constitution defines a district as a delegate body of affiliated locals and places its jurisdiction under Executive Council authority. It also provides for dues collection through the district, remittance of International and district per capita, and return of the remaining local portion with an explanation. That is a collection-and-allocation system, not proof that every service cost follows one downward chain. (Article XXII §§1–4, printed pp. 80–81)

  • Spending authority: District 142’s bylaws require an Executive Board vote for specified transfers, donations and loans, within constitutional limits. (Article 3 §4, p. 2)
  • Budget and reports: the bylaws provide for receipt-and-expense reporting to local recording secretaries on request, an annual expense budget, audit comparisons, and weekly General Chair activity reports. (Article 10 §2(b), p. 15; Article 10 §§2(w), 3(d), p. 17)
  • Audit and access: the Constitution calls for semiannual district audits distributed to specified International officials and affiliated locals. It also provides for access to relevant agreements and legally required financial-report information. These internal controls are distinct from an independent accountant’s audit opinion. (Article XXII §10, printed p. 87; §§13–14, printed pp. 89–90)

These provisions identify who authorizes spending and which records should document it. Whether those controls operated, and what members received, requires the actual budgets, distributions and service records. The financial analysis keeps reported transfers, retained resources and unresolved balances separate.

Local and Carrier Representation

Locals remain the formal membership units. District 142’s current directories identify CommuteAir under Local 2339N and National at MCO under Local 2508, alongside other carrier assignments. The National connection therefore identifies a local service relationship without establishing an exclusive National treasury or every historical membership assignment. (Local 2339N directory; Local 2508 directory)

Carrier agreements then determine the grievance and committee procedures through which representatives act. Local officers, carrier committees, shop stewards and district General Chairs should be identified by the responsibility they perform. Their authority and election or appointment route are separate questions. See Elections, Terms & Member Control and Contract Administration, Enforcement & Committees.

Supervision and the Current Authority Map

The September 3, 2026 notice announced District 142 supervision and Coveny’s departure while stating that elections and representation would continue. The October 30 election dispensation identifies a supervisor in its copy list. The public documents reviewed do not provide a complete current delegation of spending and operational powers or a final investigation disposition. (Supervision announcement; October 30 dispensation)

This limitation matters when attributing present decisions to an ordinary office or a supervisory appointee. The dated announcement establishes the intervention; a comprehensive authority map during supervision requires the operative mandate. The separate 2022–2023 trusteeship is summarized in the overview chronology.

Significance

Each management level should be evaluated through its contribution to representation, the cost it imposes, the decisions it controls and members’ ability to hold it accountable. Additional review may supply expertise or cause delay. The number of levels alone does not determine which effect members experienced.

A useful assessment follows the member’s request through authorization, funding, delivery and review, with the time and result at each stage. This page establishes the formal decision points; the service and financial sections examine the evidence of what happened.

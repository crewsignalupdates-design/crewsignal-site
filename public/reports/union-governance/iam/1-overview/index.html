International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers

Overview & Governing Documents

Current governing rules, recent structural developments, and the relationship among the International, District 142, local bodies and carrier representation.

Reviewed November 11, 2026 (Pacific time). Dates and unresolved questions reflect the public record reviewed through that date.

Current Governance Status

On September 3, 2026, IAM announced that it had placed District 142 under supervision and that John Coveny had stepped down as president and directing general chair. The joint statement said the district’s election and representation work would continue while an investigation proceeded. These are the union’s statements about the action and intended continuity; the announcement did not establish detailed final findings or measured service continuity. (September 3 announcement)

An October 30 election dispensation copied “Supervisor Battle,” documenting a supervisory role in late October. The reviewed public record does not include a final investigation report or a later notice ending supervision. (October 30, 2026 dispensation, p. 1)

The source describes supervision. The Constitution separately addresses direct supervision and suspension or trusteeship in Article VI, §§7–9, printed pp. 32–35. The announcement alone does not establish that the formal trusteeship procedure was invoked. (2025 Constitution, PDF pp. 50–53)

Governing Documents

The IAM Constitution supplies the organization-wide rules. Approved district and local bylaws add the rules for their respective bodies, while each carrier agreement establishes its own representation and enforcement provisions. The current public versions relevant to this review are:

  • IAM Constitution — effective January 1, 2025: public copy hosted by IAM Local 463; District 142 copy. Both public copies reproduce the same effective edition.
  • District 142 Bylaws — effective May 1, 2025; approved August 5, 2025: current bylaws, with both dates on the cover. The IAM Bylaws & Internal Disputes page explains the headquarters review and interpretation function; it is not a substitute for the district’s operative bylaws or each local’s approved rules.
  • National Airlines: District 142 carrier page and flight-attendant agreement.
  • CommuteAir: District 142 carrier page and flight-attendant agreement.
  • District 142 election: 2026 timetable and election materials and general election rules.

The posted National and CommuteAir contract records contain date inconsistencies. Ratification, signature, implementation and amendable dates are distinguished in Contract Administration, Enforcement & Committees.

What Changed Between the 2023 and 2025 Constitutions

A comparison of the full edition effective January 1, 2023 with the 2025 edition identified these material text changes:

  • Absentee-ballot requests: Article II §3 adds conditional emailed requests and a 21-day Canadian request interval. The provision appears in the convention-delegate procedures and is cross-referenced by other election rules. It concerns requesting a ballot, not casting a vote by email. (Printed pp. 6–7; PDF pp. 24–25.)
  • Officer protections during trusteeship: Article VI §9(a) adds Article VII §5 alongside Article L in its procedural-protection and acquittal-reimbursement references. This change does not establish the procedure used for the separate 2026 supervision. (Printed p. 33; PDF p. 51.)
  • Unemployment-stamp duration: Article G §3 adds a rule referring to 12 months or the established return/recall period, followed by a retirement- or withdrawal-card application. The wording does not create an unconditional 12-month cap for every circumstance. (Printed p. 125; PDF p. 143.)

No substantive change was detected in the compared core election terms, district/local structure, per-capita formulas, fund allocation, strike-benefit schedule or ordinary audit controls. An unchanged formula can still produce a different annual charge through its adjustment mechanism. The edition comparison is complete for the operative text reviewed; a complete proposition-by-proposition adoption and voting history remains unavailable.

Organizational Form and Airline Representation

IAM is an international union whose local and district bylaws operate under the Constitution. The Air Transport Territory, established effective May 1, 2022, includes Districts 141 and 142. District 142 is the principal district examined here for National and CommuteAir flight-attendant representation. (Constitution, Article I §§3–4, printed pp. 2–3; Air Transport Territory announcement)

National’s first-contract ratification was announced in November 2023; CommuteAir’s latest ratification announcement in this review is June 2024. District 142’s directory identifies CommuteAir with Local 2339N and National at MCO with Local 2508, which also carries other airline assignments. A lodge-wide account therefore cannot automatically be treated as one carrier’s representation budget. (National ratification; CommuteAir ratification; Local 2339N; Local 2508)

Earlier Oversight: 2022–2023

IAM announced a separate District 142 trusteeship on January 26, 2022, alleging administrative and financial deficiencies. The district later reported that it exited trusteeship on January 3, 2023. The allegations and the account of restored controls are union statements; the underlying records are necessary to evaluate their full financial significance. (January 2022 notice; January 2023 exit announcement)

This earlier episode makes pre-2023 records pertinent. It does not establish a causal connection to either the separate 2026 supervision or the FY2024 cross-filing discrepancy discussed in Financial Transparency & LM Reporting.

International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers

Representation & Member Services

How National Airlines and CommuteAir members obtain assistance, what support is documented, and how resources can be connected to services and results.

Reviewed November 11, 2026

The Member's Route to Representation

CrewSignal evaluates representation through the member's actual route to assistance: the local contact, the official who can advance a matter, the specialist support available, and the records showing what was delivered. An organizational chart identifies responsibilities; service records show how those responsibilities operate.

National Airlines and Local 2508

District 142's Local 2508 directory identifies a National Airlines assignment at MCO under Jim Samuel, alongside assignments involving other airlines. This establishes a current public local-and-carrier connection. Local 2508's accounts cover a wider workgroup than National alone, and the directory does not establish every historical or nationwide National assignment.

In a March 20, 2026 membership bulletin, James M. Samuel reported his assignment to National's day-to-day contract administration and work with Jesse Wilson on earlier grievances. The undated carrier landing page still lists Wilson. The dated bulletin supplies the clearer account of that assignment; the later District 142 supervision announcement is a separate development relevant to current authority.

CommuteAir and Local 2339N

The district directory connects Local 2339N with CommuteAir at IAH/IAD and identifies Jesse Wilson as General Chair. The local officers page and local grievance guidance provide routes to local and district assistance. Officer listings differ across some public pages, so a combined, undated roster would be misleading. The grievance guidance also retains references to ExpressJet; the applicable CommuteAir agreement supplies the carrier's contractual procedures.

The separate contract-administration analysis follows each carrier's grievance and escalation provisions. The financial analysis explains why local affiliation and the scope of a filing must be established before attributing money to a carrier.

Evidence of Work Delivered

The public record contains examples of actual bargaining and representational activity, as well as descriptions of available capacity. The distinction matters when evaluating what members receive for their dues.

  • Parent participation in National bargaining: a September 5, 2022 district bulletin names International territory representatives Joe Stassi and Jeff Tobius among those participating in the August 30–September 1 first-contract sessions. It documents participation, while leaving staff hours and cost allocation unspecified.
  • CommuteAir bargaining preparation: IAM's May 30, 2023 account reports flight attendants' preparation at the Winpisinger Center. It is evidence of a training service used by the workgroup; no attributable training cost is supplied.
  • Contract milestones: District 142 announced National's first-contract ratification on November 6, 2023 and CommuteAir ratification on June 6, 2024. These are concrete bargaining milestones. Complete payroll implementation and each member's realized benefit require additional evidence.
  • National contract intervention: Samuel's March 2026 bulletin describes work on grievances, meetings with local leadership, and a company supplemental-pay announcement following union intervention. It is the union's account of the activity; recovery amounts and completed payments are not established in that bulletin.

Specialist Resources and Case Management

IAM's Legal Department describes advice and representation for union bodies, while Strategic Resources describes bargaining analysis, research and related support. These pages identify institutional capabilities. A carrier-specific financial trail would identify the assignment, work product, staff time or invoice, paying treasury, and result.

District 142 publishes a Grievance Manager platform with guidance covering investigation, grievance stages and arbitration. Its 2023 retrospective reports the platform's introduction. The public materials do not supply comparable carrier-level caseloads, case ages, response times or implemented remedies. Platform availability is therefore evidence of operational capacity, with performance still an open question.

Connecting Cost, Access and Results

The financial analysis identifies parent, district and local resources. A service comparison also needs to identify who authorized the work, who performed it, who initially paid, who ultimately bore the expense after reimbursements, and which workgroup benefited. A local may perform work whose expense appears in a district or International account.

  • Access: availability of representatives, time to a substantive response, and a usable escalation route.
  • Case handling: cases opened, resolved, withdrawn and pending; their ages; explanations for decisions; and the cause of delays.
  • Results: remedies awarded and actually implemented, including pay, reinstatement and changes in employer practice.
  • Resources: assigned staff hours, release time, professional fees, training and a stated method for allocating shared costs.

Comparable periods and case types matter. A win rate can reflect which cases are accepted; a low spending figure can reflect shared resources or limited service. The current record supports selected activity and a representation map, but not a complete carrier-level efficiency score or return-on-dues calculation.

Current Organizing Activity: JSX

The National Mediation Board's November 2–6, 2026 activity report lists JSX Air case R-7692, IAM and approximately 127 flight attendants, with an election underway and a December 1, 2026 count date. As of this review, this is organizing activity rather than a completed certification or an existing IAM contract. Organizing resources and services to already represented workgroups should remain identifiable separately.

Significance

IAM's local, district and International levels have identifiable roles in representation, and there is evidence of actual support for these flight-attendant groups. The unresolved issue is how consistently that support reaches members and at what attributable cost. Evaluating approval times, service delivery and implemented outcomes will show whether a management layer adds expertise and capacity, adds delay, or does both in different circumstances.

Scope and method

This analysis distinguishes three decisions: the pursuit of separate legacy settlements, the choice of representation process, and the rejection of a proposed final-offer procedure in 2015. Continental Micronesia (CMI) remains a distinct legacy group. The full comparator supplies the contract, pension and implementation matrices; this companion evaluates the bargaining choices without attributing every interval to a single cause.

Why was a joint-representation alternative not implemented?

The documented course was legacy bargaining followed by a contest for sole systemwide representation—not a recovered association agreement that one side demonstrably vetoed. CWA-AFA represented United; IAM represented Continental and CMI. The NMB's April 1, 2011 decision included CMI in the single transportation system, and its June 30 certification selected CWA-AFA for the combined craft or class. Single-system determination; certification.

Cooperation could have taken more than one form: an association of incumbent unions; a temporary bargaining arrangement preserving their respective rights; or a negotiated handoff to one representative with protected participation and servicing. Those are different from separate legacy administration or a multi-legacy committee inside one certified union. An earlier NMB decision records an actual CWA/IBT association for US Airways/America West passenger-service employees. It demonstrates a precedent, not an offered flight-attendant solution at CO/UA. 33 NMB No. 27, association and member-consent record.

Management's May 24, 2010 communication contemplated joint bargaining as an alternative to continuing standalone Section 6 talks. The later NMB decision records management's account that only ALPA accepted the joint route at that stage. The general invitation is relevant evidence, but it is not a detailed CWA-AFA/IAM governance proposal or a ruling that one union caused the subsequent delay. Company communication; 38 NMB No. 38, printed p.129.

Both unions articulated legacy-contract priorities. In the June 2010 hearing record, CWA-AFA urged completion of United's existing talks before closing; IAM explained its concern about prematurely combining employees. IAM's February 25, 2011 ratification announcement described its Continental interim settlement and anticipated later negotiations after winning combined representation. These sources require examining both organizations' strategies, not assigning Continental's pre-certification decisions to CWA-AFA. Senate hearing, printed pp.22 and 75–79; IAM announcement.

Separate settlements before joint bargaining: a strategic choice

After certification, United and CWA-AFA agreed to expedite a legacy-United settlement before joint negotiations. The February 2012 union announcement reported an immediate 10 percent wage increase and a $5,000 signing bonus and expressly described improvements before moving to a single contract. Interim gains therefore cannot be dismissed as imaginary, nor does their existence demonstrate that the chosen sequence was optimal. September 2011 company announcement; February 2012 union announcement.

Section 35.B of the 2012 United agreement required notice 30–60 days after ratification and joint negotiations within 120 days after notice. This was a starting timetable, not a deadline for finishing the JCBA. The union announced the joint launch on August 27, 2012, after separate subsidiary agreements. The full comparator distinguishes that launch from the first substantive sessions in December. 2012 agreement, Section 35.B; August 27 announcement; joint-bargaining chronology.

Assessment. Placing separate settlements ahead of joint bargaining contributed to its timing and should be evaluated as a strategy, not described solely as an external constraint. The unresolved counterfactual is whether a feasible parallel or earlier joint process would have delivered a better overall result sooner. That judgment requires considering interim earnings, concessions, preparation, representation authority and the terms management was willing to accept.

A stronger starting agreement is not a guarantee of additive improvements in a successor. Management can price both stages together. The appropriate test is what the first stage purchased and what opportunity it consumed: immediate relief, protection of an existing process, narrower remaining disputes, or a dependable next stage. A commitment to commence talks does not itself supply the last of those.

The August 2015 proposal and rejection

United proposed a further negotiated timetable followed, if necessary, by a final-offer recommendation. CWA-AFA leadership rejected that format. The union's account dates discussions to August 17; the written company proposal is dated August 18. The documents concern a specific voluntary process for the three-group JCBA, not a formal NMB proffer or a seniority or grievance case. Union report, August 19; written proposal, August 18, Sections 1–3.

A nonbinding proposal still merits serious evaluation

The rejection was consequential as a choice even though the process did not guarantee a contract. A scheduled neutral recommendation might narrow differences or produce an acceptable package. It might instead influence later bargaining unfavorably, consume time or fail either approval process. These are competing possibilities, not findings about an event that never occurred.

The union's explanation acknowledged that the recommendation would be nonbinding. It feared that a detrimental recommendation would nevertheless become a basis for later bargaining. Thus the question was not merely preservation of a member vote—the proposal retained one—but the merits and risks of its selection method and standards. Stated reasons for rejection.

Leadership's judgment should be examined against the actual text, its description to members and any counterproposals. Management's choices also require scrutiny: retaining its own approval, setting economic and selection criteria, and responding to possible modifications. The record reviewed here does not establish a revised executed process or a counterproposal that resolved those objections. It therefore supports evaluating the decision, not computing a definite number of months it caused.

Why the American arbitration mechanism was different

The American NPA required final and binding interest arbitration if a tentative JCBA was not reached or was rejected, within its agreed economic and effectiveness provisions. United's proposal retained post-recommendation approval. An enforceable decision on unresolved terms and an approval-conditioned recommendation are different bargaining mechanisms. NPA A.5 and B.

The comparison concerns process, employee choice, substantive value and implementation separately. Faster resolution does not establish better terms for every employee; retaining a vote does not establish that an open-ended alternative is costless. The American award and implementation analysis follows the consequences of that binding mechanism.

What remains unresolved

Evidence boundary. No recovered CWA-AFA/IAM association or protected-handoff proposal and rejection establishes who prevented that alternative. Nor does the August 2015 record establish rejection of a guaranteed binding contract, rejection of every possible arbitration form, or the terms and timing of an earlier settlement that would certainly have resulted. Original interunion proposals, responses and any revised process terms remain the most useful further evidence.

The America West/US Airways companion supplies the earlier bargaining-track history. Its early joint start and later East reopening should not be collapsed into the different sequencing adopted at United. The institutional lesson is to test each stage's benefits, opportunity costs and completion mechanism against what the parties could actually agree to.

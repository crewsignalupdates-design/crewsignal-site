Sources and method

The comparison separates the original West negotiations, the East agreement's duration, joint bargaining, agency assistance, member votes and implementation. Company and union explanations are attributed to their issuers. Agency docket entries establish the activity they record, not every proposal exchanged or the motives of the participants. Archived communications are distinguished from signed instruments and agency findings.

Two contracts, different bargaining positions

At the 2005 merger, West's flight-attendant agreement was already amendable; East's restructuring-era agreement had a future amendable date. US Airways' 2005 Form 10-K lists May 4, 2004 for West and December 31, 2011 for East. A contract being amendable does not mean employees have no agreement. What did CWA-AFA seek before and during joint bargaining?

West leaders advocated continued improvements to their existing agreement while pursuing merger protections. A Master Executive Council (MEC) statement preserved in a May 21, 2005 archive capture committed to continuing West negotiations. A September update described mediated West talks alongside merger-committee work on fences. The archive date is not necessarily the statement's authored date. These records show concurrent objectives; they do not establish a formally adopted condition that no joint talks could begin before West members ratified a successor. May archive; September update.

West's transition Q&A later explained the compromise as securing displacement and flying protections after management rejected a broader fence proposal. That is the union's explanation of its negotiating choice, not an adjudicated finding that no better outcome was obtainable. The implemented course was a transition arrangement followed by joint bargaining without a completed West-only successor. Transition Q&A; NMB chronology.

East's later reopening presents a different question. When the joint committee interrupted talks in February 2011, the union's announcement continued to seek a merged agreement and coordinated East/West action. The subsequent East mediation case arose within an existing joint dispute. No adopted East-only-settlement prerequisite has been established in the reviewed record. February 10, 2011 union announcement.

Assessment. An opening preference, an enforceable prerequisite, and a later request for interim relief are different strategic acts. Joint bargaining's eventual start does not disprove an earlier preference, but a preference alone cannot establish a period of delay. The relevant unanswered questions concern actual proposals, decisions, responses and postponed opportunities, not the repeated use of the phrase contract first.

This chronology changes the performance question. It is not simply why the union waited until 2011 to involve the Board. It is what happened to the West proceeding, what the parties achieved through joint negotiations and facilitation, and how they used the additional East process. The original notices and recess correspondence remain important limits on assigning responsibility.

Management strategy, union choices and member decisions

Executive oversight and direct negotiating conduct require separate attribution. The company placed labor relations within President Scott Kirby's portfolio effective October 1, 2006; its March 2009 reorganization assigned Labor Relations oversight to Stephen Johnson effective March 16, reporting to CEO Doug Parker. These dated assignments establish a changing management structure, not personal authorship of every offer or uninterrupted responsibility in one office. September 2006 announcement; March 2009 announcement.

CWA-AFA's February 2011 statement accused management of withholding meaningful wage improvements while seeking concessions in benefits and protections. Such allegations identify subjects to investigate; they are not substitutes for the actual proposals or findings of bad-faith bargaining. Conversely, inherited contract complexity does not demonstrate that the length of negotiations was unavoidable. Contemporary union account.

The September 2012 tentative agreement failed ratification with 51 percent opposed and 85 percent participation, according to the union. Its November strike-authorization announcement reported 94 percent approval but described release from mediation and cooling-off conditionally. A membership vote and an exercisable strike date were not the same milestone. September result; November announcement.

Assessment. Leadership should be judged on available alternatives, priorities, recommendations, communications and results. Management should be judged on its offers, responses and willingness to resolve integration terms. Members decided whether proposed packages were acceptable. Late-stage rejections affected those offers; they cannot explain why earlier years had not produced an acceptable agreement. Bargaining activity is not automatically effective bargaining, but elapsed time alone does not identify which unchosen strategy would have succeeded.

Was binding arbitration offered and rejected?

A binding whole-JCBA offer and its rejection by either mainline flight-attendant party have not been established in the reviewed 2005–2013 record. This is a bounded evidentiary finding, not a declaration that no proposal ever existed. Failed ratification, an informational explanation of the RLA, or arbitration language governing existing rights is not evidence of a case-specific offer to resolve all new-contract terms.

Assessment. The absence of a demonstrated completion backstop is a legitimate issue in evaluating the bargaining arrangements. It cannot be converted into a documented refusal by a particular party. The distinction permits criticism of the process without inventing an offer, a veto or an attainable alternative settlement.

What the final settlement resolved—and deferred

US Airways announced 80 percent approval among voting members on February 28, 2013 and immediate pay increases. It also described the agreement as opening four-party discussions for the American merger. The Red Book was both an East/West settlement and the LUS starting agreement for the next transaction. Company-issued ratification announcement.

The final book separately specified February 28 effectiveness, the March 2013 bid-month wage scale, a conditional $1,700 ratification payment and a merger-dependent $40,000 early-out benefit for eligible participants. Its implementation letter preserved specified predecessor scheduling, reserve and hours-of-service arrangements pending later changes. Red Book, Sections 3 and 39 and Letters 1–2.

The interval from September 27, 2005 corporate closing to February 28, 2013 was 2,711 days; the interval from the February 21, 2006 joint start was 2,564 days. These are elapsed calendar intervals, not a calculation of avoidable delay or lost compensation. The full contract-outcome matrix addresses the substantive gains, losses and deferrals without treating one book as proof of immediate common operations.

The institutional lesson and the remaining record

In a prepared statement included in the June 17, 2010 Senate hearing record, CWA-AFA invoked the West experience when urging completion of United's existing negotiations before its merger closed. This documents the union's stated lesson; it does not prove the proposed safeguard was adopted or was the best available strategy. S. Hrg. 111-1061, prepared statement, printed pp.75–79.

The performance test is whether a chosen sequence delivered worthwhile interim improvements, retained leverage and provided a dependable route to common terms. A preliminary settlement can protect employees during a transition, but it can also consume time and bargaining leverage. Its merits depend on the actual bridge and next-stage arrangements, not on the label standalone or joint.

The Continental/United companion examines the different representation-first sequence and the rejected 2015 nonbinding process. The US Airways/American report explains the negotiated representation handoff and binding backstop. These are different institutional choices, not evidence that every merger offered the same alternatives.

Remaining documentary limits. The original West recess instruction and responses, the actual East notices, any adopted standalone-settlement prerequisite, and any AW/US whole-JCBA arbitration offer and replies remain unresolved. No financial cost or specific number of avoidable days is assigned to those unestablished decisions. Archive capture dates are not silently substituted for authored or execution dates.

How to read these precedents

A single word—“seniority”—can conceal several different employment clocks. Occupational or bidding seniority may determine schedules, bases, furlough and recall, while classification or pay seniority controls wage progression. Company service can affect vacation, benefits or travel. Pension credited service is a separate plan question. A base fence can protect local work without restoring the same systemwide rank.

Representation, seniority integration, contract integration and operational integration are also separate processes. They may overlap, but one does not automatically complete the others. CrewSignal’s Seniority Integration framework explains the governing concepts, including McCaskill-Bond and the Allegheny-Mohawk procedures.

Central caution: These cases do not prove that APFA or CWA-AFA follows one permanent integration philosophy. They show how transaction form, bargaining authority, incoming-group participation, inherited list rules, applicable law and negotiated protections shaped different outcomes.

Executive findings

  • Pan Am / United: an operating-unit transfer with conditional hiring and separate incoming-group representation produced neutral arbitration, but not full date-of-hire integration. The award protected a senior incumbent block and then used a ratio that gave the transferring group partial occupational-service recognition. [H2] [H1]
  • AirCal / American: APFA’s later account says the incoming group received date-of-hire bidding seniority by agreement. The original integration agreement has not been recovered, so pay, company service, pension and base terms remain separate unanswered questions. [H6] [H7]
  • Reno Air / American: APFA and American agreed before the operational merger to place the Reno group below the incumbent American list. The Ninth Circuit held that APFA owed no pre-entry duty of fair representation and did not violate its later duty by implementing the pre-existing agreement; it did not hold that endtailing is inherently fair. [H12] [H13]
  • TWA / American: ordinary systemwide occupational placement, classification/pay service, company service, local St. Louis rights and pension credited service received different treatment. “Stapled” describes only part of the result. [H14] [H15] [H16]
  • US Airways / American: APFA and CWA-AFA jointly established a date-based process using legacy American occupational dates and legacy US Airways integration dates, with training adjustments and preservation of each list’s internal order. Representation transfer, list completion, the JCBA and common operations remained separate milestones. [H25] [H29] [H30]
  • United / Continental / CMI: the NMB representation case covered three predecessor groups. The integration process gave each group committee representation, preserved internal order and reconciled different training-date rules; the final list, JCBA and common scheduling did not occur at the same time. [H34] [H36] [H38] [H40]
  • Modern law: McCaskill-Bond establishes fair-and-equitable process protections in covered transactions. It does not impose one universal numerical formula or guarantee date-of-hire placement. [H23] [H24]

Pan Am Pacific Division / United: asset transfer, hiring commitments and hybrid arbitration

United’s acquisition concerned Pan Am’s Pacific Division—routes, aircraft and associated operating assets—not the whole Pan Am company. The DOT proceeding described a proposed transfer that included route authority, aircraft, spare parts, facilities, real property and personnel. [H1] United later brought 1,202 accepting Pan Am Pacific Flight Attendants into United employment on February 11, 1986 under conditional offers described in the subsequent litigation. [H2]

The seniority mechanism combined incumbent-union bargaining with separate constituency representation in arbitration. CWA-AFA’s predecessor organization, then known as AFA, negotiated a December 1985 letter with United. The incoming Pan Am group received a five-person committee selected through IUFA, separate counsel and equal initial company-funded arbitration resources; the incumbent United group had its own committee and counsel. The two lawyers selected Arbitrator Arthur Stark. [H2]

The outcome protected the first 1,500 positions for incumbent United Flight Attendants and then inserted one transferring Pan Am Flight Attendant for every 7.47 United Flight Attendants. The court described this as partial credit for Pan Am service. The ratio was a method of occupational-list placement; it should not be converted into a percentage of pay, pension, vacation or all company service. [H2]

DOT had declined to impose the labor-protective provisions requested by the unions, and the Ninth Circuit upheld that agency decision. The seniority procedure was therefore privately negotiated rather than imposed through a DOT Allegheny-Mohawk order. [H3]

Evidence limit: The acquisition agreement, employment offers, December 1985 letter and Stark awards have not been recovered in full. The court opinions establish the process and result but do not supply every original benefit, base, furlough or recall term.

AirCal / American: reported date-of-hire bidding integration, incomplete governing record

AirCal is important because APFA’s own later history describes a materially different result from Reno Air and TWA: date-of-hire bidding seniority by agreement. [H6] Contemporaneous reporting said 506 AirCal Flight Attendants were offered comparable jobs, but the actual accepted and integrated population has not been established. [H11]

The representation record is incomplete but not absent. APFA filed an application while TWU represented the AirCal group, and the NMB record says a secret-ballot election followed. A later NMB annual report says the AirCal certifications were extinguished after the merger. [H9] [H10]

APFA’s archives separately refer to a seven-year “A-scale” merge, while another retrospective article describes a six-year pay-scale convergence. Those references cannot be silently reconciled or treated as proof that bidding seniority itself was fenced for six or seven years. [H7] [H8]

Evidence status: moderate. The original integration agreement, exact certification continuation, wage schedules, base protections, benefit-service rules and incoming-group approval process remain missing. Date-of-hire bidding integration should be identified as APFA’s retrospective account—not as a fully reconstructed agreement.

Reno Air / American: pre-entry agreement and endtailing

The Reno transaction involved stock control followed by an operational merger on August 31, 1999. The NMB record described separate IBT and APFA representation and reported roughly 447 Reno Air Flight Attendants and 21,672 American Flight Attendants, although those figures are not a final integrated-list audit. [H12]

APFA and American had already agreed to place the Reno Air Flight Attendants below the incumbent American list. In McNamara-Blad, the Ninth Circuit held that APFA did not owe the Reno employees a duty of fair representation before they entered the American bargaining unit and did not violate its later duty merely by implementing the previously completed agreement. [H13]

That holding is narrower than a general approval of endtailing. It addresses when APFA’s statutory duty attached and whether implementation of the existing agreement stated a claim. It does not establish the formula as inherently fair or supply the missing pay, benefit, company-service, pension, base or furlough terms.

TWA / American: occupational endtailing with separate service and local protections

American acquired TWA assets through TWA Airlines LLC in a bankruptcy transaction. The purchase agreement contemplated conditional employment offers and distinguished compensation, benefit and pension treatment rather than automatically carrying every seller-side right into American. [H14]

The December 2001 seniority agreement assigned the main former-TWA cohort an American occupational date of April 10, 2001, behind American Flight Attendants already on the list. Later TWA trainees or hires received later dates, while internal TWA order was preserved. [H16]

Other service clocks received different treatment. The agreement recognized TWA classification/pay and company service and established conditional St. Louis local bidding rights. American’s 2002 filing separately said that TWA LLC employees could participate in the American pension plan beginning January 1, 2002 without receiving prior TWA credited service in that plan. The retained source set therefore does not support either “all TWA service was erased” or “all TWA retirement service was preserved.” [H15] [H16]

The NMB found a single transportation system in March 2002 and extended APFA’s certification in April after IAM did not submit the required showing. [H17] [H18] Later cases addressed particular standing, preemption, limitations, jurisdiction and McCaskill-Bond issues; they did not transform the original formula into a general judicial standard of fairness. [H19] [H20] [H21] [H22]

Reader-facing shorthand: “TWA was stapled” is accurate only for ordinary systemwide occupational placement. A complete account must separately explain pay/classification service, company service, St. Louis bidding rights, pension credited service, furlough exposure and later statutory challenges.

US Airways / American: a jointly designed modern benchmark

The American/US Airways process is the most relevant modern APFA/CWA-AFA benchmark. APFA and CWA-AFA agreed to a seniority protocol that used American Occupational Seniority Dates and legacy US Airways Seniority Integration Dates. It reconciled differences in how training time was credited and preserved the internal order of each predecessor list. [H25]

The result should not be described as a raw company-date-of-hire merge. The legacy US Airways input list already included adjusted dates from the earlier America West/US Airways history, and the protocol preserved those inherited relationships rather than rebuilding that predecessor integration from scratch. [H27] [H32]

The broader transition was jointly planned. The APFA/CWA-AFA Agreement on Bargaining and Representation created equal legacy components on the joint negotiating committee, planned APFA certification for the combined group and preserved continuing CWA-AFA responsibilities for the legacy US Airways agreement during the handoff. [H29]

APFA was certified for the combined craft or class in September 2014 after CWA-AFA declined competing certification; this was not a head-to-head election. [H30] The integrated list, adjusted occupational accrual, JCBA bargaining and common operations followed different timelines. APFA’s own Q&A emphasized that the occupational adjustment did not itself change classification, company, pay or benefit seniority. [H26] [H28]

For the complete representation, contract and implementation record, see CrewSignal’s US Airways / American historical comparator.

United / Continental / Continental Micronesia: three groups, adjusted dates and overlapping timelines

The United merger involved three Flight Attendant constituencies, not two. The NMB’s single-system determination included United, Continental and Continental Micronesia; the Board then authorized a CWA-AFA/IAM election and certified CWA-AFA after the combined vote. The tally does not disclose how each predecessor group voted. [H34] [H35] [H36]

The later seniority process gave United, Continental and CMI two committee representatives each. It retained each predecessor group’s existing competitive-bidding order. United dates already reflected training start, while Continental and CMI dates were adjusted by defined training-credit ranges before later cohorts moved to actual first-day-of-training treatment. [H38] [H39] [H40]

CMI shared Continental’s training-adjustment schedule because of their common training history, but it remained a distinct predecessor group with its own employment, contract and retirement background. The 2016 JCBA retained separate retirement and transition provisions, so a common bidding formula did not establish identical company or pension service. [H43]

The list was published for member access before JCBA ratification, the contract became effective later in August 2016, and common scheduling did not begin until October 2018. Joint bargaining had begun years before list completion. [H37] [H41] [H44]

The applicable 2015 CWA-AFA constitution included a bounded neutral-arbitration backstop for unresolved questions. It is therefore inaccurate to describe the policy as excluding arbitration altogether. [H42]

For the complete contract, bankruptcy, representation, retirement and implementation record, see CrewSignal’s United / Continental historical comparator.

What the six cases show

1. Union identity alone does not explain the outcome

APFA’s record includes reported date-of-hire bidding integration at AirCal, endtailing at Reno Air, a layered occupational/service/base result at TWA, and a jointly designed adjusted-date process at US Airways/American. CWA-AFA’s predecessor negotiated a hybrid ratio process for the Pan Am operating-unit transfer, while the later United/Continental/CMI process used existing dates, training adjustments, protected internal order and a limited neutral-review mechanism.

2. Incoming-group voice and placement are different questions

The Pan Am group received separate committee and legal representation but did not receive full date-of-hire placement. Reno Air’s incoming group appears to have had little direct role in the APFA-American agreement, yet the court outcome turned primarily on bargaining-unit timing. Formal participation can affect legitimacy and dispute resolution without dictating the numerical formula.

3. “Seniority” must be disaggregated

TWA is the clearest example: occupational rank, classification/pay service, company service, local St. Louis bidding and American pension credited service did not move together. The modern mergers likewise show that a common bidding list does not equalize retirement, vacation, benefits or every base right.

4. Modern law governs process, not one formula

McCaskill-Bond incorporates fair-and-equitable Allegheny-Mohawk procedures for covered transactions and addresses same-representative policies and qualifying collective-bargaining provisions. It does not guarantee date-of-hire placement, prohibit every ratio, or supply one list formula for all transactions. [H23] [H24]

5. The integration clocks can overlap

United joint bargaining began before the final three-group list. In American/US Airways, representation, list integration, contract arbitration and operational implementation also overlapped. A universal sequence in which representation must be settled, then the list completed, and only then bargaining begins is not supported by these histories.

Implications for a hypothetical American / United process

The historical record does not determine the outcome of a future American / United transaction. It does identify the questions that a credible process would have to answer.

CWA-AFA’s current Constitution and Bylaws, amended March 9, 2026, provides an internal process that uses existing competitive-bidding seniority dates, reconciles training-day differences, preserves each predecessor list’s internal order, and sends unresolved data or methodology disputes to a neutral under defined limits. In a merger with a non-CWA-AFA carrier, that policy does not automatically control before CWA-AFA becomes the certified representative of the combined group or the parties lawfully agree to use it. [H45]

Evidence limits and held claims

  • Pan Am: original acquisition agreement, employment offers, seniority LOA and Stark awards are not in the retained source set.
  • AirCal: original integration agreement, full NMB election/certification continuation, pay schedules and service-credit terms remain missing.
  • Reno Air: the signed seniority agreement, Flight Attendant-specific certification record, exact transferred roster and benefit implementation remain missing.
  • TWA: the record is strongest for the purchase agreement, seniority agreement, NMB decisions and reported plan treatment; individual benefit calculations and all later docket dispositions were not reconstructed.
  • US Airways / American: the signed seniority protocol is available, but the complete earlier East/West date computation and some transition instruments remain incomplete.
  • United / Continental / CMI: public process notices establish methodology and milestones; the restricted employee list and signed list-certification/receipt documents were not accessed.

An unresolved record is not evidence that a protection, vote, agreement or service right did not exist.

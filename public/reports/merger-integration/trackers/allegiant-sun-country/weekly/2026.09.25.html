Status Summary

CrewSignal identified no new exact-week company release, SEC filing, independent report, Flight Attendant transition agreement, JCBA announcement, seniority-integration protocol, single-operating-certificate milestone, or NMB representation action changing the Allegiant / Sun Country record for September 19–25.

Allegiant’s August Form 10-Q therefore remained the controlling public baseline. Allegiant Air and Sun Country continued to operate separately under FAA rules, the company continued to target a 2028 single operating certificate, and operational combination remained limited pending that certificate and the necessary JCBAs.

Allegiant Flight Attendants remained represented by TWU Local 577, while Sun Country Flight Attendants remained represented by IBT Local 120. No public joint-union Flight Attendant protocol, combined bargaining committee, seniority process, interim work-protection agreement, NMB application, or single-transportation-system determination was identified.

The NMB activity report for September 21–25 listed no application, election authorization, ballot instruction, or closed representation case involving Allegiant, Sun Country, or their Flight Attendants.

Integration Dashboard

  • Allegiant Air and Sun Country continued to operate as separate airlines under FAA rules.
  • Allegiant had applied for a single operating certificate and continued to identify 2028 as the expected timing.
  • The company stated that operational combination would remain limited until the single operating certificate and required JCBAs were in place.
  • Allegiant said necessary joint bargaining would likely follow NMB certification of a single post-transaction representative for the affected craft or class.
  • Allegiant Flight Attendants remained represented by TWU Local 577, while Sun Country Flight Attendants remained represented by IBT Local 120.
  • No public TWU–IBT Flight Attendant transition protocol, bargaining committee, seniority process, or interim representation arrangement was identified.
  • The formal NMB question commonly described as a single-carrier determination is whether a single transportation system exists for the Flight Attendants craft or class.
  • Common ownership, company support, customer integration, and FAA planning do not themselves initiate a representation case or automatically establish a single transportation system.
  • Any organization or individual may file an application seeking the determination, after which the NMB conducts a case-specific investigation.
  • Relevant factors include combined schedules or routes, standardized uniforms, common branding, integrated scheduling or dispatching, centralized labor and personnel operations, common management, a combined workforce, and common ownership.
  • After a single-transportation-system finding, every applicant must establish support from at least 50 percent of the combined Flight Attendant craft or class.
  • An incumbent representative may rely on current-representation evidence, while a filer representing none of the covered Flight Attendants must use valid authorization cards.
  • Applicants and intervenors may submit or supplement the combined-craft showing within 30 calendar days after the determination.
  • Existing certifications remain effective until the NMB issues a new certification or dismissal.
  • No exact-week public event changed that procedural baseline.

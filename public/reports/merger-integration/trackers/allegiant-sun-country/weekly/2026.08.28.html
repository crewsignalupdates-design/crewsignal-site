Status Summary

CrewSignal identified no new exact-week company release, SEC filing, independent report, Flight Attendant transition agreement, JCBA announcement, seniority-integration protocol, or single-operating-certificate milestone that changed the Allegiant / Sun Country labor-integration record for August 22–28.

The August 10 Form 10-Q remained the controlling public integration baseline. Allegiant reported that Allegiant Air and Sun Country continued to operate as separate airlines under FAA rules, that a single operating certificate was expected in 2028, and that the ability to combine operations would remain limited until the certificate and necessary JCBAs were in place. The filing also said that, where necessary, joint bargaining would likely begin after the NMB certified a single post-transaction representative for the affected craft or class.

Allegiant Flight Attendants remained represented by TWU Local 577, while Sun Country Flight Attendants remained represented by IBT Local 120. No public TWU–IBT joint Flight Attendant protocol, negotiating committee, seniority process, interim work-protection agreement, NMB application, or NMB single-transportation-system determination was identified by August 28.

The NMB’s August 24–28 activity report listed no new representation application, authorized election, ballot instructions, or closed representation case. It therefore supplied no new public procedural milestone for the Allegiant / Sun Country Flight Attendants craft or class.

Integration Dashboard

  • Allegiant Air and Sun Country continued to operate as separate airlines under FAA rules.
  • Allegiant had applied for a single operating certificate and continued to identify 2028 as the expected timing.
  • The company stated that operational combination would remain limited until the single operating certificate and required JCBAs were in place.
  • Allegiant said necessary joint bargaining would likely follow NMB certification of a single post-transaction representative for the affected craft or class.
  • Allegiant Flight Attendants remained represented by TWU Local 577, while Sun Country Flight Attendants remained represented by IBT Local 120.
  • No public joint TWU–IBT Flight Attendant transition protocol, bargaining committee, seniority process, or interim representation arrangement was identified.
  • The formal NMB question commonly described as a single-carrier determination is whether a single transportation system exists for the Flight Attendants craft or class.
  • Carrier notice, common ownership, company support, commercial coordination, and FAA integration planning do not themselves initiate the representation case or automatically establish a single transportation system.
  • Any organization or individual may file an application seeking the determination, after which the NMB conducts a case-specific investigation.
  • Relevant factors include combined schedules or routes, standardized uniforms, common branding, integrated scheduling or dispatching, centralized labor and personnel operations, common management, a combined workforce, and common ownership.
  • After a single-transportation-system finding, every applicant must establish support from at least 50 percent of the combined Flight Attendant craft or class.
  • An incumbent representative may rely on a certification, current collective bargaining agreement, seniority list, dues-checkoff record, or other evidence of current representation.
  • A filer representing none of the covered Flight Attendants must establish its showing with valid authorization cards.
  • Applicants and intervenors may submit or supplement the combined-craft showing within 30 calendar days after the determination.
  • Existing certifications remain effective until the NMB issues a new certification or dismissal.

CrewSignal Watch Points

  • Will TWU Local 577 and IBT Local 120 establish and publish a member-accountable Flight Attendant transition protocol before commercial and operational integration advances further?
  • Watch for disclosure of who files or joins an NMB application, whether the incumbent unions proceed jointly or competitively, and what showing each organization relies upon.
  • Watch for authorization-card activity while distinguishing a nonincumbent card campaign from the current-representation evidence available to incumbent unions.
  • Watch for interim protections addressing scope, mixed crews, aircraft transfers, scheduling, training, bases, benefits, furloughs, and movement of work before a combined certification and JCBA are in place.
  • Does the public sequencing of representation, joint bargaining, and the single operating certificate create a risk that company integration will outpace transparent union planning?
  • Which union’s merger policy, governance structure, representation-vote practices, and enforcement record offer Flight Attendants the clearer member voice and stronger protections?
  • Watch present integration facts including centralized Flight Attendant labor administration, common crew scheduling, standardized uniforms, combined training, mixed crews, base integration, and single-operating-certificate progress.

Alaska / Hawaiian

Week ending 2026.08.28

Status Summary

In an August 31 update describing bargaining conducted during the reporting week, the Alaska / Hawaiian CWA-AFA Joint Negotiating Committee said it met management in Seattle from August 24 through August 27. The session concentrated on Section 30, Training, and Section 19, Grievance. The parties attempted to package the two sections, completed multiple rounds of discussion, and reported progress on several items, but they did not reach agreement. Both sections remained open.

The committee also resumed its comprehensive review of the reserve provisions in both legacy agreements. It said the Alaska agreement was being used as the language base while provisions from both contracts would be reviewed for the strongest elements. Vacations, sick leave, leaves of absence, insurance benefits, general and miscellaneous provisions, domiciles, and commuter policy remained in ongoing discussion; seniority and charters remained on hold pending related bargaining.

A pre-merger Hawaiian committee update published August 26 documented continuing efforts to preserve Hawaiian language and culture during integration. The I Mua Ke Alo committee said it had met with the company nearly every month in 2026, was seeking earlier participation before decisions were made, and had secured a September meeting with the chief executive officer and senior leadership in Honolulu.

Pilot labor integration also drew public pressure during the week. ALPA called on management to complete the pilot JCBA and said pilot negotiations had been underway for approximately 18 months. That development is relevant to the company’s broader labor-integration pace but remains separate from the Flight Attendant bargaining process.

The NMB’s August 24–28 activity report listed no new representation application, authorized election, ballot instructions, or closed representation case. CrewSignal identified no new Flight Attendant certification, integrated-seniority-list implementation milestone, or additional single-operating-certificate development during the reporting week.

Integration Dashboard

  • The CWA-AFA Joint Negotiating Committee met management for four days from August 24 through August 27.
  • The parties attempted to resolve Training and Grievance as a package but did not close either section.
  • The committee reported progress on individual issues while confirming that both Training and Grievance remained open.
  • The committee resumed comparison of the reserve provisions in both legacy Flight Attendant agreements.
  • The Alaska agreement remained the language base, with provisions from both agreements subject to review for the strongest elements.
  • Seniority and charter provisions remained on hold pending bargaining over related subjects.
  • The I Mua Ke Alo committee continued seeking earlier influence over company decisions affecting Hawaiian language, culture, and legacy identity.
  • The announced September meeting with senior company leadership created a new forum for pre-merger Hawaiian cultural-integration concerns.
  • ALPA’s public pilot-contract pressure reflected a parallel labor-integration issue but did not change the Flight Attendant JCBA record.
  • The NMB had already certified CWA-AFA as the representative of the combined Flight Attendants craft or class.
  • Same-union continuity avoided a contested representation election but did not itself complete common contract, reserve, training, grievance, seniority, or cultural protections.
  • No new exact-week Flight Attendant certification or integrated-seniority-list implementation milestone was identified.

CrewSignal Watch Points

  • Watch the September 8–10 bargaining session for movement on Training, Grievance, reserve, benefits, domiciles, commuter policy, and the subjects needed to release Seniority from hold.
  • Does same-union continuity produce a sufficiently timely and enforceable common agreement when operational integration continues ahead of unresolved contract sections?
  • Watch whether the reserve review and targeted member input produce clearly explained common language that protects both legacy groups.
  • Watch whether CWA-AFA secures meaningful pre-decision participation for the I Mua Ke Alo committee rather than consultation after integration choices have already been made.
  • Watch for publication and implementation of the integrated seniority list and for clear explanations of its effects on bidding, bases, training, and aircraft assignments.
  • Watch the pilot JCBA separately as a comparison of management’s pace and sequencing across represented employee groups.

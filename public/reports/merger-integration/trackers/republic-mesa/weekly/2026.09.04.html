Republic / Mesa

Week ending 2026.09.04

Status Summary

On September 1, the National Mediation Board found that Republic and Mesa constituted a single transportation system for the Flight Attendants craft or class. The Board converted pre-docket file CR-7275 into representation case R-7690 and continued the investigation to determine the representation consequences.

The decision reported 2,962 covered Flight Attendants as of May 29, including 625 at Mesa and 2,337 at Republic. It also disclosed that CWA-AFA was not acting as an intervenor and agreed that its Mesa certification should be transferred to IBT. The September 1 decision did not itself issue the new combined certification or authorize an election.

The Board relied on substantial integration rather than requiring every public-facing indicator. Its findings included common ownership and boards, common executive and labor-management functions, a ratified JCBA with significant implementation, an integrated seniority list presented April 21, movement toward common personnel policies and uniforms, and an FAA-approved transition plan leading toward a single operating certificate.

The decision also provides a practical explanation of how representation proceeds. Any organization or individual may apply; a carrier’s notice does not itself commence the dispute. A formal merger label is not indispensable because the Manual also covers consolidations, purchases, leases, operating contracts, acquisitions of control, and similar transactions. After the single-system finding, any intervenor had 30 days to file with at least 50 percent support across the combined craft or class, while the existing certifications remained effective.

Integration Dashboard

  • The NMB found Republic and Mesa to be a single transportation system for Flight Attendant representation purposes on September 1.
  • CR-7275 was converted into NMB representation case R-7690.
  • The decision identified a combined craft or class of 2,962 Flight Attendants as of May 29.
  • CWA-AFA stated that it was not acting as an intervenor and agreed that its Mesa certification should transfer to IBT.
  • The September 1 determination did not itself issue the final combined certification or authorize a representation election.
  • The NMB found substantial integration even though some public-facing indicators were absent because Republic and Mesa operate as regional carriers.
  • The Board relied on common ownership, management, labor relations, personnel functions, JCBA implementation, seniority integration, uniforms, and operational-transition work.
  • Total operational integration and a completed single operating certificate were not prerequisites to the finding.
  • A representation matter begins with an application rather than with carrier notice alone.
  • The Manual’s transaction definition reaches beyond transactions formally called mergers.
  • After a single-system finding, every applicant must establish support from at least 50 percent of the combined craft or class.
  • Incumbents may rely on current-representation evidence, while a filer representing none of the employees must use valid authorization cards.
  • Any intervenor had 30 calendar days from September 1 to submit an application and qualifying combined-craft showing.
  • Existing Republic and Mesa certifications remained effective pending a new certification or dismissal.
  • A vote was possible but not automatic because the NMB first had to resolve participation and showing-of-interest questions.

CrewSignal Watch Points

  • Watch R-7690 for an intervenor filing, election authorization, certification, dismissal, or request for additional evidence.
  • Will CWA-AFA and IBT provide members with a direct explanation of the agreement to transfer the Mesa certification to IBT?
  • What member-voting, governance, dues, grievance, and financial-accountability protections will apply under the final representation structure?
  • Watch whether any competing applicant establishes 50 percent support across the entire combined craft or class rather than one legacy group.
  • Watch continued JCBA, seniority, uniform, training, benefits, scheduling, base, and payroll implementation.
  • Does the joint-union transition model provide greater member protection and transparency than a contested representation election would have provided?

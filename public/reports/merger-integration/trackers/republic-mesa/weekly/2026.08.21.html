Status Summary

CrewSignal identified no new exact-week company or SEC integration announcement, Flight Attendant JCBA implementation notice, integrated-seniority-list update, or public NMB disposition changing the Republic / Mesa representation record. The NMB weekly activity report for August 17–21 listed no action in Flight Attendant case CR-7275 and no new airline representation application relevant to this transaction.

CR-7275 therefore remained the principal public representation proceeding. The case was docketed May 1 for an estimated 2,390 Republic / Mesa Flight Attendants and lists IBT and CWA-AFA together. The public record reviewed through August 21 did not show a single-transportation-system determination, request for a combined-craft showing, election authorization, certification, or dismissal.

The Merger Transition Agreement continued to define the parties’ joint architecture. IBT Local 135 and CWA-AFA agreed to file the NMB application jointly, created a joint negotiating committee, assigned seniority integration to the union parties, and agreed that neither union would oppose a single-carrier finding. Those commitments organize the transition but do not replace the NMB’s independent craft-specific determination or disclose the final certification and governance structure.

The standing implementation record also remained unchanged. The combined Flight Attendant JCBA had been ratified, Mesa Flight Attendants were to remain on Mesa benefits until completing Republic transition training and beginning work on Republic aircraft, and the unions had described seniority integration as proceeding under a date-of-hire standard.

Integration Dashboard

  • NMB case CR-7275 remained publicly pending for an estimated combined craft or class of approximately 2,390 Republic / Mesa Flight Attendants.
  • The NMB activity report for August 17–21 disclosed no determination, election authorization, certification, dismissal, or other action in CR-7275.
  • The Merger Transition Agreement required IBT Local 135 and CWA-AFA to initiate the Flight Attendant NMB proceeding jointly while the carriers agreed to provide support and evidence.
  • The NMB must independently determine whether Republic and Mesa constitute a single transportation system for the Flight Attendants craft or class.
  • A joint application, ratified JCBA, common ownership, and carrier support may be relevant evidence, but none independently compels the NMB finding or selects the final representative.
  • The combined Flight Attendant JCBA remained in phased implementation.
  • Mesa Flight Attendants were to remain on Mesa benefits until completing Republic transition training and beginning work on Republic aircraft.
  • The unions continued to describe seniority integration as proceeding under a date-of-hire standard.
  • After a single-transportation-system finding, every applicant must establish support from at least 50 percent of the combined Flight Attendant craft or class.
  • An incumbent representative may use a certification, current collective bargaining agreement, seniority list, dues-checkoff record, or other evidence of current representation.
  • A competing filer representing none of the covered Flight Attendants would have to establish its showing with valid authorization cards.
  • Applicants and intervenors may submit or supplement the combined-craft showing within 30 calendar days after the determination, and existing certifications remain effective until the NMB issues a new certification or dismissal.
  • Republic’s support-function integration and single-operating-certificate work may provide relevant evidence, but operating-certificate planning alone does not automatically establish a single transportation system.

CrewSignal Watch Points

  • Watch CR-7275 for a single-transportation-system finding, dismissal, request for additional evidence, election authorization, certification, or another formal NMB disposition.
  • If the NMB issues the finding, watch the 30-calendar-day supplementation period and identify every applicant or intervenor, the form of each showing, and whether an election becomes necessary.
  • How will the joint IBT–CWA-AFA arrangement become a final certification and durable governance structure, and what direct role will Flight Attendants have in reviewing that transition?
  • Will members receive clear disclosures identifying which union controls dues, grievance authority, bargaining decisions, elections, financial accountability, and day-to-day representation after the NMB acts?
  • Watch for additional implementation notices covering payroll accuracy, boarding pay, uniforms, training, benefits, scheduling, bases, and the transfer of Mesa Flight Attendants to Republic systems.
  • Watch implementation of the integrated seniority list for effects on bidding, base placement, furlough protection, aircraft transfers, and access to training.
  • Which outcomes demonstrate that the joint-union model protects Flight Attendants better than a contested election or single-union transition, and which claimed advantages remain unproven?

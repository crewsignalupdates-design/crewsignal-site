Republic / Mesa

Week ending 2026.08.07

Editor’s Note: CrewSignal uses public records to compare how unions structure, negotiate, communicate, and protect Flight Attendants during mergers and representation proceedings. It may raise questions supported by the record; it will not fabricate facts or state conclusions the evidence cannot establish. This report is informational and does not constitute legal, investment, or merger advice.

Status Summary

A Mesa CWA-AFA implementation update identified additional JCBA provisions as effective or partially effective August 1. The update listed Article 17, Notices, Contact and Communication, with Section 17.C still under development and review; Article 22, General, with Sections N.8 and N.9 still to be determined; Article 23, New Equipment; and Article 25, Bases, with implementation clarifications affecting Sections 25.D and 25.E.

The public implementation record continued to state that Mesa Flight Attendants would remain on Mesa benefits until completing Republic transition training and beginning work on Republic aircraft. The union also continued to describe seniority integration as underway under a date-of-hire standard.

The principal public representation matter remained NMB case CR-7275, docketed May 1 for approximately 2,390 Republic / Mesa Flight Attendants and listing IBT and CWA-AFA together. No public single-transportation-system determination, election authorization, certification, or dismissal for that case was identified through August 7.

The Merger Transition Agreement supplies the joint structure behind the case: IBT Local 135 and CWA-AFA agreed to initiate the NMB proceeding jointly, created a joint negotiating committee, established a union-led seniority-integration process, and agreed that neither union would oppose a single-carrier finding. Those commitments organize the parties, but the NMB must still make its own craft-specific determination and representation disposition.

Integration Dashboard

  • Additional JCBA provisions became effective or partially effective on August 1, while identified exceptions and implementation details remained unresolved.
  • Mesa benefits were to remain in place until affected Flight Attendants completed Republic transition training and began working on Republic aircraft.
  • The jointly negotiated Flight Attendant agreement had been ratified in January with 91 percent approval.
  • Seniority integration remained underway under the unions’ announced date-of-hire standard.
  • The Merger Transition Agreement created a joint IBT Local 135 and CWA-AFA negotiating structure and assigned seniority integration to union merger representatives.
  • The agreement required the unions to initiate the NMB proceeding jointly while the carriers agreed to support the application and provide evidence.
  • NMB case CR-7275 is the public Flight Attendant application contemplated by the agreement and covers an estimated combined craft or class of approximately 2,390 employees.
  • The NMB must independently determine whether Republic and Mesa constitute a single transportation system for the Flight Attendants craft or class.
  • A joint application, a ratified JCBA, common ownership, and carrier support may be part of the record, but none independently compels the NMB finding or selects the final representative.
  • Any organization or individual may file an application, although the unions contractually assigned the filing role to IBT and CWA-AFA jointly in this transaction.
  • After a single-transportation-system finding, each applicant must establish support from at least 50 percent of the combined Flight Attendant craft or class.
  • Incumbent representatives may use certifications, current collective bargaining agreements, seniority lists, dues-checkoff records, or other current-representation evidence, and the public CR-7275 entry does not disclose which evidence was submitted.
  • A competing filer representing none of the covered Flight Attendants would have to prove its showing with valid authorization cards.
  • Applicants and intervenors may submit or supplement the combined-craft showing within 30 calendar days after the determination, and existing certifications remain effective until a new certification or dismissal.
  • Republic’s operational and single operating certificate work may supply relevant integration evidence, but operating-certificate planning alone does not automatically establish a single transportation system.

CrewSignal Watch Points

  • Watch CR-7275 for a single-transportation-system finding, dismissal, request for additional evidence, election authorization, certification, or another formal NMB disposition.
  • If the NMB issues the finding, watch the 30-calendar-day supplementation period and identify every applicant or intervenor, the form of each showing, and whether an election becomes necessary.
  • How will the joint IBT–CWA-AFA arrangement become a final certification and durable governance structure, and how will Flight Attendants participate in or review that transition?
  • Does the joint arrangement provide members with clear accountability, financial transparency, grievance authority, voting rights, and an enforceable division of responsibility between the two unions?
  • Watch the unresolved August implementation exceptions, including provisions still under development or listed as to be determined.
  • Watch JCBA implementation for payroll accuracy, boarding-pay credits, uniforms, training, benefits, scheduling, bases, and the transfer of Mesa Flight Attendants to Republic systems.
  • Watch implementation of the integrated seniority list for effects on bidding, base placement, furlough protection, aircraft transfers, and access to training.
  • Which aspects of the joint representation model demonstrably protect Flight Attendants better than a contested election or a single-union transition, and which outcomes remain unproven?

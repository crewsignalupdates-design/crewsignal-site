United / JetBlue

Week ending 2026.09.04

Status Summary

JetBlue unveiled details of BlueFirst on September 1. The carrier-specific domestic first-class product included a new seating configuration, premium food and beverage service, and seatback ordering routed to JetBlue inflight crewmembers. The company described BlueFirst as part of its JetForward strategy rather than as a Blue Sky product shared with United.

JetBlue continued to state that Blue Sky was not a merger, that JetBlue and United remained separate airlines, and that mixed-carrier connecting journeys required separate itineraries. CrewSignal identified no exact-week common ownership, shared control, integrated Flight Attendant labor administration, joint CWA-AFA–TWU Local 579 protocol, NMB application, or single-transportation-system determination.

The absence of a formal merger label is relevant but not conclusive under the NMB Manual. Its merger procedures also cover a consolidation, purchase, lease, operating contract, acquisition of control, or similar transaction. Any organization or individual may file an application asking whether a single transportation system exists; carrier notice, company terminology, or union concern alone does not commence the representation matter.

Filing would trigger a case-specific investigation, not an automatic vote. The NMB would examine actual integration in routes, branding, scheduling or dispatching, labor and personnel functions, management, workforce, and ownership. Blue Sky’s current customer-facing cooperation does not automatically satisfy that test.

Integration Dashboard

  • BlueFirst was announced as a JetBlue product under JetBlue’s JetForward strategy.
  • The product included new JetBlue-specific inflight service and seatback-ordering responsibilities.
  • BlueFirst did not establish common United / JetBlue Flight Attendant work rules or labor administration.
  • JetBlue continued to state that Blue Sky involved two separate airlines and was not a merger.
  • Mixed JetBlue- and United-operated connecting travel continued to require separate itineraries.
  • United Flight Attendants remained represented by CWA-AFA, while JetBlue Inflight Crewmembers remained represented by TWU Local 579.
  • No public joint Flight Attendant transition or representation protocol was identified.
  • The NMB Manual’s transaction definition is broader than a transaction formally titled a merger.
  • Any organization or individual may file an application seeking a single-transportation-system determination.
  • Carrier notice or public use of the word partnership does not itself initiate the representation investigation.
  • Filing initiates fact-finding but does not automatically establish a single system or trigger a vote.
  • The Board would examine actual operational, managerial, labor, personnel, workforce, branding, and ownership integration.
  • Commercial cooperation, reciprocal loyalty benefits, and cross-selling do not automatically establish a single transportation system.
  • After a single-system finding, applicants would need at least 50 percent support across the combined Flight Attendant craft or class.
  • Incumbents may use current-representation evidence, while a nonincumbent would need valid authorization cards.
  • Applicants, incumbents, and intervenors would have 30 calendar days after a single-system finding to submit or supplement the combined-craft showing.
  • Existing certifications would remain effective until a new certification or dismissal.

CrewSignal Watch Points

  • Watch whether Blue Sky remains customer-facing or develops centralized labor, personnel, scheduling, training, management, workforce, or ownership features.
  • Will CWA-AFA and TWU Local 579 publish a joint Flight Attendant protocol before any representation application is filed?
  • Watch for authorization-card activity and identify exactly what representation question, workforce, and organization each card supports.
  • What direct role would United and JetBlue Flight Attendants have in choosing a representative or reviewing a joint-union arrangement?
  • Which union’s governance, merger policy, representation-vote practices, and contract-enforcement record would provide stronger member protections if Blue Sky materially deepens?
  • Watch BlueFirst implementation separately from Blue Sky to avoid treating a JetBlue-only product as cross-carrier labor integration.

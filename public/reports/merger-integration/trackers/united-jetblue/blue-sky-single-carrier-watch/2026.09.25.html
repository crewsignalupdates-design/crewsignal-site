Representation Path Update

Blue Sky Single-Carrier Watch

September 25, 2026 — No Material Single-System Movement

Method: This series measures Blue Sky against the National Mediation Board’s published representation procedures and against the documented Alaska / Hawaiian, Allegiant / Sun Country, and Republic / Mesa examples. CrewSignal distinguishes confirmed facts, announced future plans, and analytical inferences. It does not assume that a commercial partnership, common marketing, or reciprocal customer benefits automatically establish a single transportation system.

Current Assessment

CrewSignal’s assessment remained unchanged through September 25. Blue Sky continued to show customer-facing coordination through sales, loyalty, reciprocal benefits, and marketing, but the public record did not establish common ownership, common management, centralized Flight Attendant labor relations, integrated crew scheduling, combined training, or a combined workforce.

JetBlue continued to state that the airlines were not merging and would remain separate. Mixed JetBlue-operated and United-operated connecting journeys still required separate itineraries, and the operating carrier remained identified.

What Changed This Week

No Blue Sky-specific company, SEC, union, or NMB development materially changed the single-system analysis.

  • United CWA-AFA published the complete 2026–2031 agreement and a JIT site for tracking implemented provisions.
  • United and CWA-AFA implemented a carrier-specific CQ training Letter of Agreement concerning assignment timing, notice, reserve days, and limited pay protection.
  • United opened its 2027 vacation-election process under the United agreement.
  • A new United Flight Attendant class was assigned to ORD, SFO, and IAD.
  • Those items strengthened the record of separate United contract, training, scheduling, hiring, and base administration rather than common Blue Sky labor administration.

Indicator-by-Indicator Review

  • Combined schedules or routes: eligible flights from both networks appeared in partner sales channels, but a mixed-carrier single itinerary was not publicly available.
  • Common marketing: present through the Blue Sky brand, cross-carrier sales, loyalty earning and redemption, and reciprocal benefits.
  • Integrated scheduling or dispatching: not publicly established for airline operations or Flight Attendant work.
  • Centralized labor and personnel operations: not publicly established; United and JetBlue continued separate contracts, benefits, hiring, training, bases, and union representation.
  • Common management, officers, or boards: not publicly established.
  • Combined workforce: not publicly established.
  • Common or overlapping ownership: not publicly established.
  • Standardized uniforms or common Flight Attendant policies: not publicly established.

Representation Trigger and Representation Vote Path

  • An organization or individual must file an application seeking a single transportation system determination.
  • Carrier notice, partnership language, customer integration, or public speculation does not substitute for the application.
  • The NMB then investigates current integration evidence on a case-specific basis.
  • A single-system finding moves the matter into the representation phase but does not automatically create a vote.
  • Every applicant must establish support from at least 50 percent of the combined Flight Attendant craft or class.
  • Incumbents may use certifications, current agreements, seniority lists, dues-checkoff records, or other current-representation evidence; a filer representing none of the employees must use valid authorization cards as its showing of interest.
  • Applicants and intervenors have 30 calendar days after the determination to submit or supplement their showing.
  • If a representation dispute remains, the NMB may use a secret ballot or another appropriate method, so a vote is possible rather than inevitable.

Comparison with the Other Trackers

  • Alaska / Hawaiian demonstrates same-union continuity: combined certification occurred without a contested election, while the JCBA, seniority integration, and legacy-system issues remained substantial.
  • Allegiant / Sun Country demonstrates common ownership and a declared integration path with two incumbent unions, while the public filing strategy and transition protocol remain unresolved.
  • Republic / Mesa demonstrates an actual single-system finding based on common ownership and management, centralized labor and personnel functions, JCBA implementation, an integrated seniority list, common policies, and operating-certificate transition work.
  • Blue Sky remained materially less integrated in labor, management, ownership, and workforce terms than the Republic / Mesa record.

What to Watch Next

  • Launch of a single itinerary combining flights operated by both carriers.
  • Centralized labor relations, personnel policies, discipline, benefits, training, scheduling, or base administration affecting both Flight Attendant groups.
  • Common management, overlapping boards, ownership changes, or a shared operating-control structure.
  • Mixed crews, movement of Flight Attendant work, common uniforms, or a combined workforce.
  • A CWA-AFA–TWU protocol, organizing campaign, authorization-card collection, or NMB application.

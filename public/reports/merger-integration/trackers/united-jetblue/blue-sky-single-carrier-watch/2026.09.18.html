Representation Path Update

Blue Sky Single-Carrier Watch

September 18, 2026 — Baseline Assessment

Method: This series measures Blue Sky against the National Mediation Board’s published representation procedures and against the documented Alaska / Hawaiian, Allegiant / Sun Country, and Republic / Mesa examples. CrewSignal distinguishes confirmed facts, announced future plans, and analytical inferences. It does not assume that a commercial partnership, common marketing, or reciprocal customer benefits automatically establish a single transportation system.

Current Assessment

CrewSignal’s current assessment is that Blue Sky remains in a customer-facing collaboration stage rather than a representation-level single-system stage. The public record establishes cross-carrier sales, reciprocal loyalty benefits, and shared marketing, but it does not establish common ownership, common boards, common executive management, centralized Flight Attendant labor relations, integrated crew scheduling, or a combined Flight Attendant workforce.

JetBlue expressly says the airlines are not merging and will remain separate. Mixed-carrier connecting journeys still require separate itineraries, and the partnership is not a codeshare. Those facts are not conclusive for all time, but they are material to the present assessment.

Indicator-by-Indicator Review

  • Combined schedules or routes: customers can shop eligible flights from both networks, but the companies have not publicly described a combined operating schedule or mixed-carrier single itinerary as currently available.
  • Common marketing: present through the Blue Sky brand, cross-carrier sales, loyalty earning and redemption, and reciprocal benefits.
  • Integrated scheduling or dispatching: not publicly established for airline operations or Flight Attendant work.
  • Centralized labor and personnel operations: not publicly established; United and JetBlue continue separate Flight Attendant benefits, hiring, bases, scheduling, contracts, and union representation.
  • Common management, officers, or boards: not publicly established.
  • Combined workforce: not publicly established.
  • Common or overlapping ownership: not publicly established.
  • Standardized uniforms or common Flight Attendant policies: not publicly established.

What Could Trigger a Representation Case

A conventional corporate-merger announcement is not the only transaction form recognized by the NMB Manual. Its definition also includes a consolidation, purchase, lease, operating contract, acquisition of control, or similar transaction. That broader definition does not mean Blue Sky presently qualifies. An applicant would still need to file and support a case, and the NMB would evaluate the actual relationship on its facts.

  • An organization or individual files an application seeking a single transportation system determination.
  • The filing is supported by evidence of representation or a showing of interest.
  • The NMB conducts a pre-docket investigation and examines current integration evidence.
  • Carrier notice, public partnership language, or union concern alone does not replace the application.
  • The Board may find that a single system exists, decline to make that finding, or request additional evidence.

What Could Lead to a Representation Vote

  • A single-system finding moves the matter into the representation phase but does not automatically create a vote.
  • Every applicant must establish support from at least 50 percent of the combined Flight Attendant craft or class.
  • CWA-AFA and TWU, as incumbents at their respective carriers, may use certifications, current agreements, seniority lists, dues-checkoff records, or other current-representation evidence.
  • A filer representing none of the covered employees must establish its showing with valid authorization cards.
  • Applicants and intervenors have 30 calendar days after a single-system determination to submit or supplement the combined-craft showing.
  • If a representation dispute remains, the NMB may use a secret ballot or another appropriate method. A qualifying filing can therefore make a vote possible, but not inevitable.
  • Existing certifications remain in effect until the NMB issues a new certification or dismissal.

Comparison with the Other Trackers

  • Alaska / Hawaiian demonstrates same-union continuity: combined certification occurred without a contested election, but the JCBA, seniority integration, and legacy-contract issues remained substantial.
  • Allegiant / Sun Country demonstrates common ownership and an announced integration path with two incumbent unions, while the public filing strategy and transition protocol remain unresolved.
  • Republic / Mesa demonstrates a completed single-system finding based on common ownership and management, centralized labor and personnel functions, JCBA implementation, an integrated seniority list, common policies, and operating-certificate transition work.
  • Republic / Mesa also demonstrates that every public-facing indicator need not be present, but substantial managerial, operational, financial, and labor integration must be established.

What to Watch Next

  • Launch of a single itinerary combining flights operated by both carriers.
  • Centralized labor relations, personnel policies, discipline, benefits, training, scheduling, or base administration affecting both Flight Attendant groups.
  • Common management, overlapping boards, ownership changes, or a shared operating-control structure.
  • Mixed crews, movement of Flight Attendant work, common uniforms, or a combined workforce.
  • A CWA-AFA–TWU protocol, organizing campaign, authorization-card collection, or NMB application.
  • Any company, union, court, SEC, DOT, FAA, or NMB document that materially changes the operating or representation facts.

Railway Labor Act

Bargaining, mediation, release, and presidential intervention under the Railway Labor Act

Statute: Railway Labor Act (RLA), 45 U.S.C. §151 et seq. Institutions: National Mediation Board · Presidential Emergency Boards

Reviewed October 20, 2026 · General process information, not legal advice or a case-specific self-help determination.

Origins and Scope of the Railway Labor Act

The Railway Labor Act (RLA) was enacted for railroads in 1926 and extended to airlines in 1936. The National Labor Relations Act was enacted in 1935; airline labor relations follow the RLA’s separate framework.

The RLA addresses representation and collective bargaining for covered employees. Its major-dispute procedures concern creating or changing pay, rules, and working conditions. Disputes over the interpretation or application of existing agreements follow a different process.

Airline agreements become amendable rather than simply ending on an expiration date. The status quo generally continues while the applicable bargaining and mediation procedures run their course. The outline below describes major-dispute procedures, not an individual determination of when any workforce may take self-help.

The Section 6 Bargaining Cycle

Section 6 notices propose changes to an existing agreement. Direct bargaining gives the parties an opportunity to settle those changes. If they cannot agree, either side may request NMB mediation; the Board may also offer its services.

Opening notices, an amendable date, and a first bargaining meeting are distinct milestones. A member survey or strike-authorization vote is not a substitute for an NMB procedural action.

National Mediation Board and Mediation

The NMB assists the parties in reaching agreement but does not impose a negotiated contract. It manages mediation and decides when its efforts have been exhausted. There is no fixed statutory deadline for completing mediation.

A mediator’s schedule is not a public finding about either party’s reasonableness. The absence of a carrier from a weekly activity report also does not establish that talks stopped. NMB training and voluntary facilitation are distinct from statutory mediation.

Release and the 30-Day Cooling-Off Period

When the NMB concludes that further mediation will not resolve the dispute, it proffers binding arbitration. If either party rejects that proffer, the Board releases the parties into a 30-day cooling-off period. The parties must maintain the status quo during that period.

A request for a status conference, a union declaration of impasse, or a strike vote does not itself start the cooling-off period. Settlement efforts may continue. Self-help is subject to completion of the applicable statutory steps and any additional lawful intervention.

Presidential Emergency Boards (PEBs)

A Presidential Emergency Board is conditional, not a mandatory step in every airline dispute. The President may create one when the statutory essential-transportation conditions are met.

The NMB explains that a PEB generally has 30 days to investigate and report, followed by another 30-day status-quo period. Its recommendations offer a basis for settlement; they are not automatically a new collective bargaining agreement.

This is the rail-and-air framework. The separate rules for commuter railroads should not be imported into an airline timeline.

Single-Carrier and Representation Issues

NMB representation proceedings determine the representative for the relevant craft or class. Following a single-transportation-system determination, the Board separately addresses representation consequences.

A certification determination does not itself supply a joint agreement, assign an internal union local, or establish that operational integration is complete. Representation, bargaining, ratification, and implementation therefore require separately identified evidence.

Reading an Airline Bargaining Timeline

Elapsed time can describe a bargaining history, but it is not proof of fault, an impending release, or the quality of a future agreement. Comparisons should use the same starting and ending events and identify whether the outcome was a full contract, a limited bridge agreement, or another arrangement.

Practical Implications for Current Disputes

Look for the specific event: a formal opening, a mediation docket, a proffer of arbitration, a release notice, a ratification result, or an implementation date. Announcements of intent and completed procedural actions are not interchangeable.

This resource provides general information, not legal advice or authorization for self-help. The governing agreement, statutory requirements, and case-specific actions control.
